# Accelerators back the agents, not the network

None of the startups we found from Y Combinator, a16z speedrun, Techstars, 500 Global, HF0, South Park Commons, Sequoia Arc, Peak XV Surge, Accel Atoms, Blume, Google for Startups or the other programs in scope builds XELOR's whole loop. That loop runs: shortage, then an RFQ written from records, then a supplier quote with no login, ranking, award to PO, a supplier record earned from receipts and inspections, shared capacity and a factory passport, all inside an SME ERP. What accelerators have funded, heavily, is the front half of the loop: buyer-side AI procurement agents that sit on top of someone else's ERP. The closest are Lumari (YC X25), Mandel AI (YC S23, $3.9M seed), SpaceFlow (YC S26), Tenkara (HF0, $7M seed), Magentic (Sequoia Arc 2024, $18M Series A) and Cronwell (Techstars NYC 2025). Beside them sits a second cluster of seller-side order-intake agents (Comena, Korso, Smartbase). The product thesis closest to XELOR's belongs to **Tiny (YC F24)**, an AI-agent ERP for Excel-run supplier factories already working with automotive and electronics suppliers in **India, Vietnam and Indonesia**. We found no evidence that Tiny has a supplier network. In India, Surge and Atoms cohorts show no SME-factory supplier network. The live threats there come from outside accelerators: IndiaMART (which owns Busy accounting), Procol (Surge 03), 1Buy.AI, and credit-backed marketplaces heading to IPO such as OfBusiness and Zetwerk. The four steps from RFQ to PO are crowded and well funded. Earned supplier records, peer capacity sharing, the portable passport, and the bundling of all of it inside a GST-native SME ERP remain open. One caveat runs through everything below: most accelerator directories were blocked to the researchers, so if a company is missing here, that does not prove it doesn't exist.

## YC funds more than twenty adjacent companies, but each takes one side of the transaction

YC has the densest portfolio near XELOR. It splits into four groups: buyer-side procurement agents, seller-side order-intake agents, managed RFQ marketplaces, and a few "AI-native ERP for manufacturers" bets. The buyer-side agents are the most direct overlap. **Lumari (X25)** runs "100s of always-on AI agents" that handle sourcing and RFQs, expedite POs, and chase quote follow-ups and delivery status for direct materials ([Fondo](https://fondo.com/blog/lumari-launches)). It has about $500K from YC, Matador and Sterling Road ([extruct](https://www.extruct.ai/hub/lumari-io)). **Mandel AI (S23)** coordinates suppliers "from RFQ through invoice" across email, portals, EDI and the ERP ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/mandel-ai)). It raised a **$3.9M seed at a reported $30M post-money in March 2026** and claims $1B+ in material spend processed for aerospace, pharma and industrial clients ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/03/25/y-combinator-backed-mandel-ai-raises-3-9m-to-automate-global-supply-chains/)). **SpaceFlow (S26)** normalises multi-round quotations and chases suppliers. That is the closest analogue to XELOR's ranking step, and SpaceFlow claims about $400M in annual supplier spend processed with four staff ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/industry/supply-chain-and-logistics/san-francisco-bay-area)). Every one of these companies sells to US or EU mid-market and enterprise buyers. None runs its own ERP or a supplier network.

The seller-side cluster is XELOR's mirror image. Its agents read incoming POs and RFQs and push them into the supplier's ERP. **Korso** (listed as P26 on one source and S26 on another) parses RFQs, prices them against history, drafts quotes and chases supplier ETAs for manufacturers ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/QKn-korso-ai-agents-for-manufacturing)). **Comena (S25)** extracts line items from emailed POs into the ERP and has about $1M including Bessemer ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/comena)). **Smartbase (X26/P26)** calls itself an "AI-native ERP for manufacturers", but today it only converts customer POs into ERP orders ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/smartbase)). This matters for XELOR in a specific way: XELOR's quote links will increasingly be answered by software, not people, so interoperability with supplier-side agents becomes a design requirement.

**Tiny (F24)** deserves the closest watch. It pitches itself as "Rippling for factories", an agent-driven ERP for "the four million factories still primarily relying on Excel", and says it is "already working with several factories in Indonesia, Vietnam, and India, focusing on suppliers in the automotive and electronics industries" ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/MH3-tiny-rippling-for-factories)). That is XELOR's customer profile almost word for word. The launch text describes no supplier network, no-login quoting, scorecard or passport, and we found no funding or traction beyond the YC profile. Among marketplaces, **Jiga (W21)** is the proof that RFQ networks can make money. It raised a **$12M Series A led by Aleph in November 2025** and is reported to be profitable ([Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/press-release/20251119-jiga-secures-12m-series-a-to-eliminate-hardware-sourcing-bottleneck-threat/)). But its buyers are hardware engineers, and it is the marketplace, not the buyer, that holds supplier performance.

## Outside YC, the field thins to a handful of buyer-side agents

Across the other US programs, the closest match is **Tenkara**, which is backed by HF0. Its agents automate "supplier discovery, procurement, compliance, and freight" for US manufacturers, and it raised **$7M led by True Ventures in March 2026** ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/ex-shark-tank-founder-and-thiel-fellow-raises-7m-led-by-true-ventures-for-tenkara-to-build-ops-agents-for-us-manufacturers-302729128.html)). **Cronwell** (Techstars NYC, Spring 2025, $220K) automates supplier research, RFQs and quote comparison for industrial manufacturers ([Techstars](https://jobs.techstars.com/companies/cronwell)). On the RFQ step it is as close as any YC company. **Laibl** (Techstars, Fall 2025) runs AI agents that vet suppliers, contact them on several channels and return verified quotes. It is explicitly two-sided: suppliers get "a steady flow of qualified opportunities". Its market, though, is CPG packaging, not discrete parts ([Techstars Notion](https://techstars.notion.site/Laibl-24bf180a1252809daaa9ec1280d6f4ec)). **Factor** (South Park Commons and Afore, $6M seed led by Gradient in December 2021) automated supplier selection, order tracking and supplier payment for hardware makers and reported 250+ customers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/12/13/factor-raises-seed-round-led-by-google-ai-team-to-streamline-an-overlooked-part-of-the-supply-chain/)). We could not confirm whether Factor is still active.

The most instructive a16z speedrun company is not in manufacturing. **Vereda (SR006, January–April 2026)** is a WhatsApp-native procurement agent for Brazilian farmers, who use WhatsApp daily at a rate of about 96%. It closed a **$4.8M seed on 12 June 2026** and claims $155M in GMV ([speedrun](https://speedrun.a16z.com/companies/vereda); [Briter](https://www.briter.co/companies/vereda)). It is the strongest evidence that a top-tier program will fund messaging-first, no-app procurement in an emerging market, which is a close behavioural analogue to India. Its SR006 batchmate **Modern Industrials** automates quoting and order entry for building-materials distributors ([speedrun](https://speedrun.a16z.com/companies/modern-industrials)), another seller-side mirror.

In Europe, **Magentic (Sequoia Arc 2024, London)** runs agents inside a manufacturer's ERP, email and Teams that select suppliers, negotiate, place orders and clear invoices. It raised a seed round led by Sequoia ($5.5M per its own release) and then an **$18M Series A led by Felicis**, and claims one customer runs more than 1M orders a year through it ([Seeking Alpha PR](https://seekingalpha.com/pr/20172972-magentic-raises-5_5m-to-meet-demand-for-ai-driven-savings-in-tariff-hit-supply-chains); [Vestbee](https://vestbee.com/insights/articles/magentic-raises-18-m)). **Eluvium** (London, Berlin and Istanbul; $700K, February 2025) offers AI RFQ and sourcing agents for manufacturers ([vcbacked](https://www.vcbacked.co/company/eluvium)). Its reported Google for Startups link is unconfirmed: it is absent from the May 2026 MENA-T cohort list ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/startups/meet-the-newest-cohort-of-our-mena-t-startup-accelerator)). Sequoia is also the direct investor, not via Arc, behind **Tacto**, a $50M supplier "operating system" for Germany's industrial mid-market ([Munich Startup](https://www.munich-startup.de/en/96697/ai-startup-tacto-secures-50-million-euros/)).

Emerging-market programs mostly back managed marketplaces, not software. 500 Global funded **Proglix**, raw-material procurement for Indonesian SMEs with a $1.35M seed ([500 Global](https://500.co/content/daily-markup-692-proglix-raises-us1-35m-to-make-raw-materials-shopping-easier-for-indonesian-smes)), and co-invested in **Inflow**, a Vietnamese fashion factory-matching network of 150+ manufacturers with a $2M seed. Aggregators link Inflow to Iterative, but we could not confirm that on Iterative's own site ([Vietcetera](https://vietcetera.com/en/inflow-secures-2m-funding-to-revolutionize-fashion-supply-chain)). Google's 2026 MENA-T cohort includes **RFxAI** (Qatar), which handles the RFx tender lifecycle. Its 2025 Africa cohort includes **Midddleman**, which runs AI-assisted China sourcing for Nigerian SMEs ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/startups/meet-the-newest-cohort-of-our-mena-t-startup-accelerator); [Google Africa](https://blog.google/intl/en-africa/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/meet-the-15-startups-joining-the-2025-google-for-startups-accelerator-africa-cohort/)).

Several programs in scope turned up nothing relevant. For Neo, Pioneer, Antler, Entrepreneur First and Better Capital, researchers found no supplier-network or manufacturing-procurement company. Plug and Play and HAX/SOSV offer corporate pilots and Shenzhen sourcing help rather than competing portfolio companies, and none of the three runs factory-to-factory capacity sharing ([SOSV](https://sosv.com/prototyping-to-production-how-hax-helps-founders-build-hard-tech/); [Plug and Play](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/plug-and-play-opens-supply-chain-and-logistics-startup-accelerator-in-northwest-arkansas-300849662.html)). Some of the strongest functional comparables came through no listed program at all. In Europe these are Valuechain (multi-tier supplier collaboration for SME manufacturing chains) and Kreatize, Laserhub and Spanflug (managed capacity marketplaces). Elsewhere they are Bababos in Indonesia ($3M seed, raw materials for small manufacturers) and Brkz in Saudi Arabia ($17M, 1,100+ suppliers, $350M of RFQs processed) ([Innovate UK](https://iuk-business-connect.org.uk/perspectives/scaleup-programme-stories-valuechain/); [Accion](https://www.accion.org/bababos-manufacturing-raw-material-procurement-platform-has-secured-us-3m-in-a-seed-funding-round-led-by-east-ventures); [MENAbytes](https://www.menabytes.com/brkz-series-a-2/)).

## India's accelerators have left the SME-factory network empty

India's flagship programs have not backed XELOR's model. **Procol (Surge 03, 2020; also Blume)** is the only clear procurement company in Surge's visible history. It sells AI sourcing and supplier agents to enterprises such as Škoda, Aditya Birla Group, AM/NS India and Coca-Cola, and claims 200+ enterprise customers and $20B+ in procurement handled ([ThePrint/ANI](https://theprint.in/ani-press-releases/procol-unveils-game-changing-ai-agents-to-revolutionize-enterprise-procurement/2581176/)). It is the strongest proof that agentic RFQ software sells in India, but its buyers are large enterprises. **Surge 12**, announced on 28 September 2026 with a raised $5M per-company ceiling, covers AI, robotics, space, consumer, health, music and fintech. Only 5 of its 18 companies focus on India ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/28/peak-xv-goes-bigger-at-seed-with-new-surge-cohort-as-series-a-bar-rises/)). Accel Atoms' "AI and Industry 5.0" picks work on plant operations and CAD rather than suppliers: Ripik AI (Atoms 2.0) and Asets (Atoms 3.0) ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2024/03/accel-accelerator-atoms-cohort-ai-industry-startups-investment)). Google for Startups Accelerator India 2026 named **Jidoka** (AI vision inspection) but no procurement company ([Google](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/announcing-the-2026-cohort-of-google-for-startups-accelerator-india/)). Blume's relevant bets are Procol, Sourcewiz (quotation SaaS for export factories) and BIDSO, a toy contract manufacturer that raised a ₹63 Cr Series A in March 2026 ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/2021/10/blume-and-awi-lead-3-mn-seed-round-in-sourcewiz/); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/03/funding-bidso-rs-63-crore-series-a-led-by-blume-ventures)). Within YC, the India-linked companies (Waybill, Sculpt, Raven, Optifye) serve deep-tech buyers or the plant floor, and **YC's 156 India-headquartered startups** include no SME-factory ERP with a supplier network ([startupbooted](https://www.startupbooted.com/list-of-y-combinator-backed-indian-startups)).

India's real competition sits outside the accelerators, and it is better capitalised. **1Buy.AI** was founded by OfBusiness co-founder Nitin Jain and raised a ₹32.5 Cr seed led by 100Unicorns. It automates BOM-level RFQs for electronics manufacturers and claims up to 40% shorter RFQ cycles ([Indian Startup Times](https://www.indianstartuptimes.com/investment/serial-unicorn-founders-raise-%E2%82%B932-5-crore-for-1buy-ai-to-modernize-electronics-procurement/)). That makes it the closest Indian match to "short part, then RFQ from BOM", though it covers one category and the buyer side only. Category marketplaces earn on material margin. **NowPurchase** sells to foundries and raised $8.6M led by Bajaj Finserv in April 2026 ([Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/nowpurchase-raises-usd-86-mn-led-by-bajaj-finserv/503828)). Metalbook covers metals and Mstack covers chemicals. Managed capacity networks own the buyer relationship. **Karkhana.io** raised a $6.3M Series A in January 2024 to "ramp up supplier base" ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/2024/01/karkhana-io-raises-6-3-mn-in-series-a-round)). **Zetwerk** filed an updated draft prospectus (UDRHP) for a ₹2,600 Cr IPO after FY26 revenue of ₹15,913 Cr ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/zetwerk-files-udrhp-for-%E2%82%B92600-cr-ipo/)). **Fashinza**, an apparel network that raised $100M in 2022, is returning capital to investors ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/05/17/2311970/); [Clay](https://www.clay.com/dossier/fashinza-funding)). That is a warning about the unit economics of intermediated factory networks.

The most dangerous Indian incumbent is **IndiaMART**. It acquired Busy Infotech (GST billing, e-invoicing, inventory) for up to ₹500 Cr, and Busy's revenue grew 47% year on year in Q1 FY27 ([IndiaMART](https://corporate.indiamart.com/2022/01/25/indiamart-to-acquire-busy-infotech-for-rs-500-crores); [Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/news/indiamart-intermesh-targets-15-20-new-license-growth-for-busy-infotech-4531-PE1_COR)). It is the only player that owns both a huge MSME supplier directory and MSME accounting. If it connected Busy's purchase module to IndiaMART buyer RFQs, it would replicate much of XELOR's loop at once. We found no evidence that it scores suppliers from goods receipts (GRNs). **OfBusiness** is converting to a public company. Its FY26 profit was ₹724 Cr, and its lending arm Oxyzo holds ₹11,822 Cr in assets under management ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/ipo-bound-ofbusiness-fy26-profit-jumps-21-yoy-to-%E2%82%B9724-cr/); [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/exclusive/exclusive-oxyzo-records-rs-1494-cr-revenue-and-rs-375-cr-pat-in-fy26-11885541)). So it already owns SME raw-material purchasing and credit. **TranZact** shows how far SME ERP has got in India: a freemium ERP covering purchase and quotations, with 20,000+ SMEs and a $7M Series A, but no network ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/erp-startup-tranzact-raises-7-mn-in-series-a-funding)). The channel itself is also shifting. Meta launched "Business AI on WhatsApp" for Indian small businesses in May 2026 ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/introducing-business-ai-on-whatsapp-for-small-businesses-in-india/)), yet researchers found no funded Indian startup that specialises in collecting RFQs and quotes from MSME suppliers over WhatsApp.

Regulation is moving in XELOR's favour. The **MSMED (Amendment) Act 2026** received assent on 13 August 2026 and routes certain MSME receivables through TReDS ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/08/19/micro-small-medium-enterprises-development-amendment-act-2026/)). Since 30 June 2026, CPSEs must settle MSME invoices on TReDS ([DP Jadhav](https://www.dpjadhav.com/post/govt-mandates-treds-for-cpse-msme-payments-what-manufacturers-need-to-know)). Section 43B(h) already denies buyers a tax deduction for late payments to micro and small suppliers ([Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/faqs-understanding-section-43b-h-of-the-income-tax-act)). One correction for XELOR's rules engine: the statutory limit is 15 days when there is no agreement, not 45, and sources conflict on this point ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/finance-ministry-clarifies-section-43bh-45-day-msme-payment-rule.html)). ONDC's MSE-TEAM scheme aims to bring 5 lakh MSEs onto its B2B network and talks about sellers "building their reputation" there. That reputation is platform-level, not measured from operations ([MSME RAMP](https://ramp.msme.gov.in/ramp/sites/default/files/2026-05/Approved-msme-team-guidelines_0.pdf)).

## Steps one to four are crowded; steps five to eight are open

Mapping every company above against XELOR's capabilities gives a clear picture. The **RFQ-to-PO front end is crowded and richly funded**. ## Five threats, and where XELOR should aim

The first threat is a **same-thesis entrant with YC behind it**. Tiny already sells an agent ERP to supplier factories in India, Vietnam and Indonesia. If it adds supplier links and scorecards, it competes for the same factory owner, with a US funding advantage. The second is **IndiaMART/Busy closing the loop**: it has supplier discovery, buyer RFQs and MSME accounting under one roof, and Busy's growth shows the distribution is real. The third is **well-funded agents moving down-market**. Didero, Mandel and Magentic already target manufacturers and distributors, and Procol has India sales experience. With a cheaper tier and WhatsApp support, any of them could make XELOR's RFQ-to-PO steps look like a me-too procurement agent. The fourth is **credit-backed marketplaces flush with IPO money**: OfBusiness/Oxyzo, Zetwerk, NowPurchase and 1Buy.AI already own the SME factory's buying relationship and can add "vendor digitisation" software to lock in suppliers. Bizongo already markets AI vendor digitisation ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/bizongo/__egbH8DcTJwVXbFLnAOL9MEE_M1CFfcjpWD7pqhdZLeM)). The fifth is **channel commoditisation**. Meta's Business AI on WhatsApp, together with seller-side agents such as Comena, Korso and Modern Industrials, means the no-login link will soon stop being novel. Quotes will increasingly come back from machines, and XELOR has to accept them cleanly.

The opportunities follow from the empty half of the map. XELOR should lead with the earned record and the passport, not the RFQ agent. A supplier score built from GRN and incoming-inspection events, produced as a by-product of using the ERP, is something agents running on someone else's ERP cannot reproduce and marketplaces will not give away. Peer capacity sharing among suppliers who also run XELOR has no visible competitor in India or elsewhere. Unlike Zetwerk or Karkhana, it does not take margin, and Fashinza's wind-down suggests that is the healthier structure. The 2026 payment rules give the ERP a compliance hook. Tracking 43B(h) and the MSMED payment limit, capturing each supplier's Udyam status and exporting invoices to TReDS together make a reason to adopt that has nothing to do with the network. The earned record can also become a financing asset. KredFeed (Techstars) and Oxyzo show lenders will price MSME receivables, and a transaction-derived passport is better collateral evidence than the GST and credit scores Recordent sells today. The ecosystem offers partners too. Sculpt (YC S22, Pune) could produce supplier-side quotes that answer XELOR RFQs, Jidoka could feed inspection results, and ONDC's reputation layer could carry passports as credentials. One risk cuts the other way. The absence of funded SME-India supplier-network software may mean investors judge SME contract values too low, not that nobody has noticed the gap. The local playbook that has worked, TranZact's freemium ERP plus financing-led monetisation, is the model to test against.

## Conclusion

The accelerator record reframes XELOR's position. Investors have already validated, and heavily funded, the idea that AI can write RFQs and chase suppliers: more than twenty YC companies, plus Tenkara, Magentic and Cronwell. That has made the front of XELOR's loop a commodity before XELOR has sold it. What no program has funded is the trust layer that only appears when the buyer's ERP, the supplier's response and the receiving bay all live in one system: the earned record, peer capacity and the passport. That layer is hard to copy for structural reasons. An agent sitting on someone else's ERP never sees the inspection result, and a marketplace has no incentive to make supplier records portable.

The practical consequence is about sequencing and narrative. In India the fight is not against YC-style agents. It is against IndiaMART/Busy and credit-led marketplaces racing to own the SME factory's purchasing relationship, with Tiny as the one accelerator-backed company pursuing the same customer. XELOR's window is the period before those players treat transaction-earned supplier records as a product. The 2026 MSME payment mandates give a compliance-driven reason to be inside the factory's ledger first. Every claim of white space in this report should be retested once the blocked portfolio directories (YC, Surge, Atoms, Antler and EF) can be enumerated directly, and Tiny's funding and product roadmap is the first thing to verify.
