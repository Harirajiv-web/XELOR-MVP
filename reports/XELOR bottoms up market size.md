# Factory counts put XELOR's SAM near ₹500 crore

XELOR's market is best stated as **a ₹2,866 crore core TAM for the ERP (about $326M)**, a **serviceable market of about ₹504 crore ($57M)** in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and a **five-year SOM of about ₹13.7 crore ARR ($1.6M)**. That SOM is 220 paying plants, 3% of SAM, which sits at the low end of the penetration Indian SME software has actually reached. (USD figures throughout are approximate, at ₹88 to the dollar.) The ERP drives almost everything. India has **47,762 factories with 100 or more workers** in the latest Annual Survey of Industries (ASI), and at the investor brief's launch price of ₹6 lakh per plant per year, that headcount alone produces the core TAM. XELOR Market listings and future finance referral fees add a large theoretical ceiling, but under realistic conversion each contributes less than ₹50 lakh a year by year five. Their job is to grow the supplier network, not to carry revenue. The numbers are deliberately conservative. Factories with 20 to 99 workers, plants outside the two southern states and plants with 500 or more workers are all left out of SAM. Year-five penetration is held at the bottom of a 3 to 7% band that the research itself calls optimistic for India. Several inputs still need fixing before an investor reads them. The brief's ₹6 lakh price conflicts with the internal revenue model's ₹18 lakh and ₹54 lakh. The "5,000 named accounts" figure has no source. The ASI size-band counts were taken from the repo's own citation and were not re-read from the MoSPI PDF.

## The ERP line carries 96% of the value: 47,762 plants at ₹6 lakh

The ERP build starts from registered factories, not from the headline count of 9 crore MSMEs. ASI 2023-24 reports **2,12,990 factories in operation**. Grouped by persons engaged, 19,623 are in the 100–199 band, 15,299 in the 200–499 band, and 12,840 have 500 or more, for **47,762 plants with 100+ workers** ([MoSPI ASI Summary Results 2023-24, Statements 13A/16A](https://mospi.gov.in/uploads/publications_reports/publications_reports1759229789833_2b8ed8ad-314e-46b7-b123-b08cb17f1233_ASI_Summary_Results_2023-24C.pdf)). At ₹6 lakh a year, 47,762 × ₹6 lakh = **₹2,866 crore**, the same TAM the investor brief uses. Including the largest plants is fair for a TAM. Many 500+ factories are large-group sites that would choose SAP, but the TAM also leaves out the roughly **1,65,228 operating factories with fewer than 100 workers** (2,12,990 − 47,762), and some of those could buy at ₹6 lakh. A typical plant in the 100–199 band produces **about ₹74 crore of output with about 124 people**, which is the customer XELOR is describing.

SAM narrows that in two ways. The first is geography. The first clusters are Peenya, Hosur and Coimbatore, so SAM is Karnataka plus Tamil Nadu. Tamil Nadu holds **15.43% of India's factories**, the most of any state, and Karnataka ranks in the top five states by factory employment ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/tamilnadu/tamil-nadu-tops-in-factory-strength-and-employment-in-manufacturing-sector-asi-2023-24)). No sourced count of Karnataka factories was found, so the model assumes Karnataka has about 5.5% of them, and the two states together about 21%. The second is size. SAM keeps only the 100–499 worker bands (34,922 plants), because those are the factories where an MSME-priced ERP competes. Plants of 500 or more are mostly large enterprises. The result is 34,922 × 21% ≈ **7,330 plants × ₹6 lakh = ₹440 crore a year**.

The price is defensible against what Indian factories pay today. The most expensive SME manufacturing ERP plan found is **TranZact Enterprise at ₹3.6 lakh a year** ([TranZact pricing](https://letstranzact.com/pricing)). A full SAP Business One rollout for 5–50 users costs **₹14–75 lakh** ([Praxis](https://praxisinfosolutions.com/blog/sap-business-one-price-in-india-a-detailed-breakdown-of-sap-b1-cost/)). NetSuite's first year for 20 users runs **₹50 lakh to ₹1.5 crore** ([Aaxonix](https://www.aaxonix.com/resources/netsuite-erp-india-guide/)). At ₹6 lakh with no large implementation fee, XELOR costs about 1.7x the top SME plan and a fraction of mid-market ERP. The adoption gap is real but only partly measured. About **12% of Indian MSMEs use ERP software** ([RIS MSME Digitalisation Report](https://ris.org.in/sites/default/files/Publication/MSME_Digitalisation_Report.pdf)), but that average is dominated by micro firms. For 100+ worker plants, the realistic sale is usually replacing Tally plus Excel, not installing a first system.

The internal inconsistency has to be fixed before submission. The internal revenue model prices the ERP at ₹18 lakh (MSME) and ₹54 lakh (multi-plant). On the same 47,762 sites, those prices give a TAM of ₹18,727 crore. This report uses the ₹6 lakh stated in the investor brief throughout. One price should appear in every external document.

## Listings build the network but earn under ₹12 lakh by year five

XELOR Market is free, or ₹249 a month for Verified (₹2,988 a year, or ₹2,490 on an annual plan). This model uses **₹2,490 as the realised price** to stay conservative. Any MSME can list, so the TAM is every manufacturing MSME on Udyam. As of 31 July 2026, Udyam and Udyam Assist hold **1,83,17,965 manufacturing enterprises** out of 9.17 crore in total ([Kashmir Life](https://kashmirlife.net/jammu-kashmir-records-over-9-64-lakh-msme-registrations-since-2020-447368/)). At ₹2,490 that is **₹4,561 crore**, but this is a soft ceiling. Most of those units are informal micro enterprises registered on Udyam Assist, which exists for firms without a PAN or GST number, and most of them will never pay to be listed.

SAM is the three launch geographies. Bengaluru Urban district has **1,49,744 Udyam-registered manufacturing enterprises** ([YouthPower – DIC Bengaluru Urban](https://youthpower.in/institution/karnataka/bengaluru-urban/dic); [AIKosh dataset](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/datasets/details/district_level_manufacturing_msme_registered_enterprises_under_udyam_registration.html)). Peenya, with about **13,000–13,500 units**, sits inside that district ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/peenya-south-asias-largest-industrial-cluster-cries-for-urgent-govt-attention-enn25062104909)). Hosur adds about **3,000 MSMEs** ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/news-national/hosur-vendors-charge-destiny-1154)). Coimbatore is counted only through its **~620-unit foundry cluster** ([BEE cluster profile](https://beeindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/Coimbatore_0.pdf)), which deliberately undercounts the city. The total is 1,53,364 units × ₹2,490 = **₹38 crore a year**.

The SOM uses the strongest Indian benchmark available. IndiaMART has **2.18 lakh paying suppliers out of about 8.8 million storefronts, about 2.5%**, after roughly 25 years. Its Silver tier churns **about 7% a month** ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-indiamart-msme-lending-arm-q1-fy2027-earnings/); [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/stocks/indiamart-q1-fy27-results-revenue-up-11-percent/)). TranZact's ₹5.9 crore FY25 revenue against 20,000+ onboarded SMEs implies a similar 2–5% free-to-paid rate ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/tranzact/financials/)). The model assumes that by year five, **15,000 suppliers are listed** (about 10% of SAM, pulled in mainly by ERP plants inviting their own suppliers), and **3% of them pay**. That gives 450 × ₹2,490 = **₹11 lakh a year**. This is the plain finding: at one-eleventh of IndiaMART Silver's ₹32,000, listing revenue only matters at very large volume. Its value to XELOR is the supplier records and network density it creates for the ERP.

## Finance referrals are a later, basis-point business

Referral fees from RBI-regulated TReDS platforms and lenders are not live, and XELOR never handles money. The pool is real and growing. TReDS discounted **₹3.47 lakh crore in FY2025-26**, up from ₹2.35 lakh crore a year earlier ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/government-mandates-treds-for-msme-invoice-settlement-by-cpses); [M1xchange](https://www.m1xchange.com/m1xchange-reports-150-growth-in-h2-fy25/)). TReDS financing costs **7–11% a year** ([Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/trade-receivables-discounting-system-treds/)). Supply-chain finance facilitators such as Vayana charge **20–40 basis points** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/companies/short-of-funds-get-your-trade-financed-through-vayana-network-117073000522_1.html)). Loan referrals pay 1–2% of the amount disbursed ([Lendingkart](https://www.lendingkart.com/blog/earn-commission-on-the-same-day-by-partnering-with-lendingkart-business-loan/)). XELOR would not run the platform, so the model assumes **15 basis points of invoice value financed**.

On that basis the TAM is ₹3.47 lakh crore × 0.15% = **₹520 crore**. SAM is the financeable purchase flow of the 7,330 SAM plants. Each is assumed to buy about ₹40 crore a year, a conservative 54% of the ₹74 crore average output (ASI-wide, inputs are about 84% of output; [The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/08/27/dcm50-biz-survey-asi.html)). About 6% of that flow is assumed to be financed, which is TReDS volume as a share of MSME manufacturing output: MSMEs produce 35.4% of manufacturing output ([Rajya Sabha PQ](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/750487/1/PQ_265_29072024_U745_p435_p436.pdf)). The arithmetic is 7,330 × ₹40 crore × 6% × 0.15% ≈ **₹26 crore**. SOM starts in year three. By year five, 220 ERP plants carry ₹8,800 crore of purchases with verified receipts in XELOR. If 3% of that flow is financed through partners, that is ₹264 crore, and at 15 basis points it earns **₹40 lakh**. Two recent changes raise the upside but are not counted. CGTMSE now guarantees **75% of defaults on TReDS invoices where both buyer and seller are micro or small enterprises** ([The Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/government-guarantee-goes-live-for-small-firms-invoice-financing-on-treds)). And CPSEs must now settle MSME invoices on TReDS. XELOR's delivery and GRN records fit exactly the MSE-to-MSE trade this guarantee targets.

The five-year path stays below the benchmark band in every year. The research suggests **1–3% of SAM in years one to three and 3–7% by year five**, and notes that more than 10% in five years has no Indian precedent. XELOR's path reaches 1% only in year three and 3% in year five. Year one assumes both friends-and-family pilots convert to paid and eight more Peenya plants sign.

Two checks show the path is achievable without assuming a breakout. With a conservative **20% annual churn** for a new ERP vendor, the path needs about 22, 46, 74 and 116 new plants in years two to five, which is roughly ten a month by year five across three clusters. Against TranZact, the closest Indian peer, XELOR's year-five ERP revenue of ₹13.2 crore is about 2x TranZact's FY25 revenue of ₹5.9 crore, earned from fewer logos: 220 plants, against TranZact's estimated 330–1,000 paying-equivalents. The difference comes from price and direct cluster selling, not from faster adoption. The main risk is price. In a downside case, 150 plants at the TranZact-anchored ₹3.6 lakh gives **₹5.4 crore**. In an upside case, 7% of SAM (513 plants) at ₹6 lakh gives **₹30.8 crore**.

The two most useful fixes are cheap. The first is a count of Peenya plants with 100 or more workers, from the Peenya Industries Association, KASSIA or the Factories Department; the research only hypothesises "a few hundred", so year one's ten plants should be tested against that list. The second is the official Udyam split of small and medium manufacturing enterprises. Either would replace an assumption with a sourced number in the SAM.

## Conclusion

The sizing exercise changes how XELOR should describe itself. It is an ERP business with a network attached, not a marketplace with software attached. Listings and referrals look large as TAM ceilings (together over ₹5,000 crore) but produce barely half a crore by year five at realistic Indian conversion rates. Their real return comes through the ERP: suppliers recruited into the network make the ERP stickier for buyers, and verified receipts make the later finance product possible. Investors will therefore judge the opportunity on one question: can XELOR sign about ten plants with 100+ workers a month in South Indian clusters at ₹6 lakh? Every other line depends on that answer.

A modest SOM is also the stronger pitch. A five-year target of ₹13.7 crore on a ₹504 crore SAM leaves room in the clusters and the two states. It also leaves room in the factories under 100 workers and the plants of 500 or more that SAM excludes. Beyond SAM, TReDS volume is still growing almost 50% a year and the 2026 payment rules push more MSME invoices onto it, so the referral line could outgrow these estimates. The model does not count any of that headroom, and that restraint makes the numbers defensible.

## Ready-to-paste application answer

We sell an AI ERP to Indian factories with 100 or more workers, and we list their suppliers.

TAM: India has 47,762 factories with 100+ workers (Annual Survey of Industries 2023-24). At ₹6 lakh a year per plant, that is ₹2,866 Cr, about $326M.

SAM: We start in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and count only plants with 100 to 499 workers. That is about 7,330 plants, or ₹440 Cr a year. Supplier listings in Bengaluru, Hosur and Coimbatore (1.5 lakh MSMEs at ₹2,490 a year) and future finance referral fees take SAM to about ₹500 Cr, about $57M.

SOM: In five years we aim for 220 plants. That is 3% of SAM, the low end of what Indian SME software has reached. 220 × ₹6 lakh = ₹13.2 Cr. With about 450 paid listings and small referral fees, we reach about ₹13.7 Cr ARR, about $1.6M.

We plan on only 3% of listed suppliers paying, since IndiaMART converts about 2.5%. Listings are mainly how we grow the network.

Today we have 2 pilot factories and no paid customers. Peenya, with about 13,000 units, is first.
