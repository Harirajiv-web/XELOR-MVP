01 · Product model

What XELOR is

A manufacturing operating system with a controlled multi-agent coordination layer

The simple explanation

XELOR connects customer commitments, material, plans, plant execution, quality, maintenance, money, people and governance. Its agents do not replace these modules; they help a user ask one cross-functional question and receive one traceable, controlled answer.

02 · Agent map

Seven agents, one coordinated system

Each summary is intentionally brief; the companion PDFs hold the detail

ONYX · Supervisor

Turns a business goal into a controlled plan, assigns the right specialists, joins their evidence and presents one understandable result.

Grouping: ONYX Copilot, Agent OS, AI Operations

HEXA · Governance

Protects identity, permissions, policy, budgets, approvals and audit evidence so every mission stays inside the user’s authority.

Grouping: Organisation, Administration, Integration

MICA · Commercial

Explains what customers ordered, what was promised, what has shipped and what service or warranty obligation remains.

Grouping: Sales, Service / CSP

SPAR · Supply

Explains what stock exists, what has been ordered from suppliers and where material shortages threaten a plan or promise.

Grouping: Purchase, Inventory

AXLE · Planning

Connects what the product needs with what demand requires, then explains what to make, what to buy and which dates or capacities are at risk.

Grouping: Engineering, Planning

KILN · Operations & Quality

Explains what the plant is making, whether it passes quality controls and whether machines can keep the schedule running.

Grouping: Production, QMS & Audit, Maintenance

RASP · Finance & Working Capital

Explains the accounting truth, cash pressure, collections, expenditure and payroll consequences behind an operating decision.

Grouping: Accounts, Working Capital, Employee Spend, People / HRM

The shape of the team

ONYX is the only delegating supervisor. HEXA is the shared control layer. MICA, SPAR, AXLE, KILN and RASP are business specialists. A specialist cannot quietly recruit another agent or widen its own role.

Runtime behaviour

Ready nodes run in parallel waves. After each wave, XELOR stores a checkpoint. Failed nodes, timeouts or deadlocks stop the run safely; interrupted nodes can be recovered without pretending they completed.

05 · Controlled action

What happens when a mission needs follow-up work

The demo has approval-bound dispatch, not silent autonomous ERP writes

Read. The six live business reads gather company, order, stock, planned-order, production, inspection and finance evidence where required.

Assess. Specialists explain their own area. ONYX combines the evidence into a proposed cross-functional action plan.

Preflight. HEXA checks the user, tenant, permission, graph context and proposed action before any approval request.

Approve. The graph pauses for a human. Approval belongs to that exact waiting node and mission context.

Dispatch. Approved specialist assignments are appended as governed work items; retries do not duplicate them.

Verify. HEXA verifies records and audit evidence; ONYX reports the observed outcome and any remaining limits.

Investor-demo wording

Say “the system has created approved, traceable work for the responsible teams.” Do not say “the agents changed every ERP record automatically,” because that is deliberately outside the current runtime.

06 · Collaboration examples

How the groupings make one business answer

The value comes from joined evidence, not seven isolated chat windows

Delivery promise

MICA supplies the customer date; SPAR the material; AXLE the plan; KILN execution and quality; RASP the cash effect; HEXA the controls; ONYX gives one readiness answer.

Quality and audit readiness

KILN gathers inspections and drafts evidence/CAPA/audit packs. SPAR traces incoming material, AXLE the product/plan context, HEXA the approvals, and ONYX the readiness gaps.

Working-capital review

RASP leads cash, vouchers, scenarios and collections. MICA adds receivables context, SPAR purchasing/inventory pressure, AXLE planned demand, KILN operating impact, and HEXA control evidence.

Full command review

ONYX runs a broad operational view, keeps specialist facts separate, highlights conflicts and publishes a prioritised evidence-backed summary.

Five registered graph recipes

  • Foundation cross-functional readiness
  • Full command review
  • Controlled action mission
  • Finance working-capital review
  • Quality QMS/audit readiness

07 · Trust model

Why the user remains in control

Five boundaries apply to every mission

1 · IdentityVerified token determines the user and tenant
2 · AuthorityAgent allow-list AND the user’s permission must pass
3 · ActionSide effects need an approved ancestor node
4 · Business rulesThe normal domain service remains in charge
5 · EvidenceRuns, nodes, events, checkpoints, approvals and audit persist

Failure is explicit

The graph records a failed node, timeout or deadlock and stops. It does not fabricate a successful answer to keep the presentation smooth.

Recovery is durable

Stored checkpoints let a run recover interrupted work with idempotent capability rules and visible history.

AI is governed

A closed feature registry, provider router, opt-out, budget, kill switch, evaluation gate and hash-chained log surround AI use.

Code and humans decide

Deterministic code applies accounting, quality, tax, stock and workflow rules. A person controls approval-bound action.

08 · Current implementation boundary

What the MVP honestly demonstrates

A strong demo is clear about the boundary between live, controlled and future

Live now

  • Seven registered agent identities and prefixes
  • Sixteen allow-listed capabilities
  • Five versioned mission graphs
  • Parallel nodes, joins, checkpoints and recovery
  • RBAC, tenant fencing, approval waits and audit
  • Read, analyse, simulate and draft tools
  • Approval-bound, idempotent work-item dispatch
  • Decision Commander evidence joins and outcome attribution

Not claimed

  • No external model API is active
  • No external connector is active
  • Language reasoning is deterministic
  • No general-purpose direct SQL tool
  • No agent can widen the user’s permission
  • No automatic customer message, payment or posting
  • No dispatched work item is described as completed business work
  • No simulation or draft is labelled as fact
The one-line disclosure

Orchestration, ERP reads, approval gates and governed action dispatch are live. Language reasoning is deterministic; no external model API or connector is active.
