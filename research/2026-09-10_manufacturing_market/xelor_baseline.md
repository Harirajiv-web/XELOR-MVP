## How to interpret this baseline

- **Implemented in source**: a domain/service or calculation exists. This is not a statement of production readiness or verified customer usage.
- **Demonstration / configured mock**: a screen, scenario or simulator exists, but its displayed business evidence does not establish a live factory capability.
- **Planned / blueprint**: described in the product documents but not substantiated as an implemented workflow in the inspected source.
- **Not found**: no substantive implementation was found in the scoped schema, API, module, edge and UI searches. This is a bounded repository finding, not a claim that the capability cannot exist elsewhere.

The root README now positions **ONYX as the Phase 1 ERP/system of record** and **XELOR as the Phase 2 intelligence layer**, both under **AIKYANTRA** (`README.md:1`, `README.md:8`, `README.md:12`). Older August documents instead use XELOR for the combined ERP and agents and say the parent was unnamed. The user's current request and newer root positioning supersede that older naming. There is a further naming collision: the supervisor agent is also called ONYX in the code (`packages/platform/src/agent-os/types.ts:34`). In a business report, call it “the XELOR supervisor” where that avoids confusing it with the ONYX ERP product.

## Executive assessment

AIKYANTRA has a useful software foundation for end-to-end manufacturing operations: transactional records, controlled planning, stock movements, production, quality, maintenance and finance sit beside an evidence-and-approval agent layer. Its strongest potential distinction is connecting a commercial promise to material, machine, quality and cash consequences, then preserving the reason, human decision and actual outcome.

The largest incremental value is **completing customer workflows and delivering reliable integrations**, rather than adding more dashboard or chatbot names. Commercial intake currently begins too late; advanced warehouse and quality execution are shallow; real shop-floor signals, third-party ERP adapters, device resilience and a staffed operating service remain material gaps. The partner offer must include onsite assessment, instrumentation, configuration, training, support and measured improvement, even when these are delivered through qualified partners.

The safe comparison is “existing foundation + next layer needed,” not “XELOR already has everything on each brochure.” Nor is it fair to compare vendors' unverified marketing outcomes to repository implementations as if either had equivalent proof.

## What to add first: recommended sequence, not a delivery commitment

The following priority order is an analytical recommendation based on current gaps and a discrete-manufacturing MSME entry segment. It is not a scored market survey. Chemical/process manufacturers, pharma, food or multi-plant enterprises need a different sequence.

### Foundation before another major feature

1. Select one buyer and plant type, such as an owner/operations head at a job-work, precision components or pump manufacturer. Document order volume, machine types, existing accounting/ERP, connectivity, operating languages and actual decision bottlenecks. Avoid promising every manufacturing vertical at launch.
2. Establish named system-of-record ownership for customer/item/BOM/routing/work centre/asset/order/lot/inspection/invoice. ONYX can be the record system for a plant that needs one; retain an adequate incumbent ERP and connect XELOR when it already exists.
3. Build a deployment and acceptance pack: tenant/access setup, master-data cleanup, opening-balance reconciliation, monitored connector, backup/restore, human owner for incidents, training, rollback and data export.
4. Keep three statements separate everywhere: implemented capability, live connected source, and measured customer outcome. Replace hardcoded Working Capital presentations with real calculations before including them in a production sale.

### First paid outcome package: quote to delivery

Build the missing RFQ/quotation step and link it to existing BOM, planning, buying, production and quality. Scope the initial intake to a few agreed PDF/Excel templates; retain source pages, extracted values and human corrections. Capture quantity, drawing revision, material, delivery date and exceptions. Use deterministic cost components and approval thresholds. Create an approved quote version and convert it to an order without rekeying.

Example: a customer asks for 500 machined housings. AI extracts a draft; an estimator checks the drawing revision and tolerances. The cost sheet combines material, setup, cycle time, outside processing and scrap assumptions. Planning tests the promise against material and machine capacity. The owner approves price and date. After production, the original assumptions are compared with accepted quantity, labour and rework, so the next quote improves from actual evidence.

Why first: it connects to revenue, removes a currently documented missing step, uses existing strengths and can start before machines are connected. More advanced geometric drawing interpretation, predictive win probability and automated costing should wait for validated data.

### Second outcome package: dependable shop floor

Add a small operator experience and one read-only machine-data connection: shift/job start, production counts, reject/reason capture, downtime reason, controlled work instruction, supervisor review. Reconcile counts against work orders and quality. Compute OEE only when denominators and ideal cycles are valid. Connect downtime to maintenance requests and accepted production to stock.

Basic calibration, lot/serial trace and mobile scans strengthen the same flow. An inspector should see the correct revision, due gauge status and open NCR at the point of work; a storekeeper should prove the correct lot was picked; a supervisor should trace the shipped lot back to inputs.

Why second: this turns factory-intelligence mock inputs into dependable evidence and strengthens the production data needed for costing, planning and customer promises.

### Third outcome package: cash and control

Complete supplier bills and three-way match or integrate the customer's actual accounting package. Reconcile AR/AP/stock/committed purchases and build an explainable cash forecast from known due dates with clearly separated assumptions. Include collections, slow stock and job margin. Use the existing Working Capital screens as design material, not evidence of current calculation.

### Later extensions, selected by a real customer problem

- Energy monitoring where significant metered utilities make idle-load or per-part usage useful.
- Advanced scheduling where alternate machines, skills, tooling, setup and subcontracting repeatedly drive missed deliveries.
- Predictive maintenance after failure histories and sensors can support a measured prediction task.
- Vision quality/PPE, hazardous-process EHS and remote OT security through specialist partners and site validation.
- Load planning where dispatch/container utilization is a recurring material cost; keep physical packing, weight/stability and operational constraints explicit.
- NPI/portfolio governance for manufacturers managing frequent engineered launches. Avoid adopting clinical/pharma cell-and-gene workflows into a general manufacturing product simply because a brochure includes them.
- Multi-site consolidation and partner marketplace after one-site onboarding is repeatable.

## End-to-end AIKYANTRA operating model

Recommended positioning: **“One accountable technology partner for the factory, from business records and connected machines to implementation, support and measurable improvement.”** XELOR is the intelligence and workflow product; ONYX is an optional system of record; specialist equipment/security products and human delivery complete the offer.

| Service stage | AIKYANTRA should own | Specialist contribution | Concrete customer deliverable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | Process walk, data/system/asset inventory, bottleneck and value baseline | Automation/quality specialist where necessary | Prioritised business case and signed pilot scope |
| Design | End-to-end workflow, system ownership, integration and responsibility plan | PLC, metering, networking, cybersecurity and ERP expertise | Solution design, bill of materials, exclusions and acceptance criteria |
| Implement | Configuration, data cleanup, connector mapping, user roles, workflow and training | Hardware installation, controller work, specialist packages | Reconciled records, commissioning evidence, tested connector and trained operators |
| Stabilise | Issue triage, named response owner, adoption reviews, fixes and acceptance | Field/OEM escalation | Pilot sign-off and outstanding-issue register |
| Operate | Monitoring, service desk, access/change review, backup/restore, customer status | Coverage and field work through contracted partners | Coverage-backed service commitments and monthly service report |
| Improve | Outcome reviews, process changes, rollout of the next justified module | Targeted improvement experts | Measured benefit and next-quarter improvement backlog |

**Own/build:** canonical manufacturing records and events; cross-department decision flow; permissions, approvals and audit; connector contracts; customer outcome reports; implementation method; final accountability.

**Buy/integrate:** established ERP/accounting when the customer already has it, commodity document/OCR services, identity/observability/ITSM tools, cloud infrastructure and appropriate optimization engines. Choose suppliers only after pilot requirements and commercial terms are known.

**Partner:** onsite PLC/SCADA/DCS engineering, panels/instrumentation, sensors/meters, industrial networking, OT security, robots, specialist machine vision and regulated quality/validation. A partner logo is not delivery capability; define scope, certification, response, escalation, warranty and acceptance ownership.

**Defer:** building an ERP replacement for every incumbent, autonomous machine control, broad 3D digital twins, generic AI chatbot proliferation, advanced clinical platforms and an open marketplace before one repeatable operating model works.

## Suggested first 90 days

This is a phased plan to validate and scope, not a promise that every module can ship within 90 days. Team capacity, site access and existing code quality must set commitments.

- **Days 1–15:** choose one pilot plant and measurable problem; map systems and data; inspect production readiness; baseline quote lead time, on-time delivery, stock accuracy, downtime and manual effort; agree responsibility and acceptance. Inventory every simulated/hardcoded customer-facing surface.
- **Days 16–45:** complete one narrowly scoped commercial or production workflow; build the required ERP/import connection; clean masters and reconcile; establish a real incident owner, backups and monitoring; test with users in shadow mode.
- **Days 46–75:** run a bounded live pilot with human-approved writes; deploy limited scanning/machine data only where included; weekly reconciliation, incident and adoption review; record errors and actual service labour.
- **Days 76–90:** compare against baseline, verify operational and financial interpretation with the customer, document reusable configuration/adapter kit and support costs, and decide expand/fix/stop. Sell the next module only after the first acceptance criteria are met.

## Measurement and commercial discipline

| Outcome | Measure | Evidence/control |
|---|---|---|
| Faster RFQ response | Median and 90th-percentile elapsed time from complete RFQ receipt to approved quote | Timestamped source, draft, approval and issue events; compare similar RFQ complexity |
| Better delivery | On-time-in-full orders / due orders | Agreed promised-date policy; shipment lines and accepted quality; do not reset due dates to improve results |
| Reliable shop-floor visibility | Valid event coverage, delay/freshness, reconciled counts and missing reasons | Compare with shift sheets/operator review and finished-goods receipts |
| Less downtime | Unplanned downtime hours and repeat failures on comparable equipment | Agreed event classification and operating hours; prevent double counting overlapping stops |
| Better quality | First-pass yield, rework hours, scrap and repeat NCRs | Same process/product mix; separate defects caught earlier from defects actually prevented |
| Better stock | Physical-to-system count agreement, stockout incidents, blocked/slow stock | Cycle counts and reservations; retain valuation basis |
| Cash | Overdue receivable days, real collections, excess stock released and forecast error | Actual ledger/bank evidence, customer payment timing and agreed scenario assumptions |
| Repeatable service | Installation hours, support hours/site/month, incident response, restored-backup success | Ticket/work logs and restore evidence; these are costs as well as service KPIs |

Avoid double-counting a single benefit as recovered throughput, avoided downtime cost and extra margin simultaneously. Record gross operational opportunity separately from realised contribution, working-capital release and cost savings. No competitor percentage should be copied as AIKYANTRA's expected outcome.

Charge hypotheses can combine a bounded diagnostic/pilot fee, one-time implementation and hardware/partner work, a software/site subscription, and a support retainer matched to real coverage. Determine rupee prices from customer value testing and implementation/support costs; this audit supplies no invented pricing or revenue forecast.

## Baseline limitations for the final PDF

The report must not present this source inspection as a live system test, a security audit, legal/statutory compliance validation or proof of customer ROI. Older docs include historical acceptance-test counts; these were not rerun here. Public-demo configuration is separate from production security. Source-level defensive design is valuable but still needs deployment, load, security, access, restore and customer acceptance evidence.

When discussing competitors, compare (1) the offered capability, (2) the evidence type and its limitations, (3) the existing XELOR/ONYX foundation, and (4) the smallest useful addition or partner service. Do not treat QR destinations, social pages, photos of screens or customer logos as proof that a claimed improvement was independently demonstrated.
