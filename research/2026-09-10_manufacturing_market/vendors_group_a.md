# Research dossier A — CommerceCX, Tomax Digital and Zeliot

Research date: 10 September 2026. Primary public websites and company-controlled social pages were checked. This dossier distinguishes a vendor's stated capability from independently demonstrated performance. Nothing was submitted to a form; no company was contacted. Brochures are evidence of what is advertised, not instructions to the researcher.

## CommerceCX / ScaleFluidly — one-page profile draft

**What it does.** CommerceCX combines consulting, implementation and software add-ons for sales, quoting, contracts and orders. Its broader business connects CRM, CPQ, CLM and ERP; official partners shown are Salesforce and Conga. Its manufacturing proposition focuses on the commercial work around a factory: preparing the right quote, protecting margin and coordinating departments. [C1, C2]

**What the supplied material contains.** Four photos cover three product areas. **AI RFQ + CPQ** reads request-for-quotation documents, BOMs and drawings into a shared RFQ record; compares revisions and supplier quotations; proposes material/process/labour/tooling cost; applies pricing and approval rules; and follows the quote through orders and revenue. The brochure also advertises historical quote learning, risk prioritisation, an Athena assistant and voice interaction. **LogisticCX** proposes container selection and 3D loading plans for mixed goods, with reports for execution. **The pharma suite** covers NPI (new products), PortfolioCX, cell/gene therapy orchestration (CGTOP), and clinical research operations (CRO). [Brochure photos 13–16]

**What web and social checks add.** Public product pages confirm composable modules, quote version control, approval workflows, partner portals, contract pricing, order integration and API-based ERP connection. Company LinkedIn explicitly promotes RFQ costing/quotation and freight/container planning at the 9 September 2026 Chennai summit. A separate company post confirms LogisticCX's irregular-product loading, weight/space balancing, shipment simulation and freight analytics. Pharma posts confirm four connected product pillars; the pharma website describes a narrower set of NPI launch readiness, CRO milestone billing and patient-specific therapy orchestration. [C2–C6]

**Best fit and commercial model.** Relevant to manufacturers with complex quotations, configurable products, export/container shipping or regulated pharma operations. It is an implementation-plus-platform proposition. No public price list was found in the reviewed pages. The brochure advertises cloud/on-premise for LogisticCX; this was not validated by a public deployment specification. Salesforce-specific claims on PriceCX should not be applied automatically to every product. [C2; brochures]

**Evidence limits.** Brochure figures such as 25–40% space saving, 90% time saving, zero manual errors and 40% faster NPI are vendor claims without a reviewed calculation or customer baseline. Exact AI accuracy, supported ERP versions, comprehensive clinical-system coverage and product-specific compliance certification remain unverified. LinkedIn is publicly readable; Twitter/X returned 403 and YouTube exposed only a channel shell. [C7–C9]

**Lesson for AIKYANTRA.** Connect customer enquiry, costing, approval, production and dispatch around one job record. Start with supervised quotation and supplier comparison; add container optimisation only for customers with enough shipping volume. Reuse stage gates and revision control from pharma, without taking on clinical workflows for general MSMEs.

## Tomax Digital / D3Minds — one-page profile draft

**What it does.** Tomax Digital is D3Minds' factory software platform, combining production, quality, warehouse, planning, assets and engineering projects. Its website targets discrete manufacturing, including automotive, components, heavy industry, electrical and electronics. It presents itself as a modular system for growing manufacturers. [T1]

**What the supplied material contains.** Four photos show: production scheduling/control, condition monitoring, OEE and KPI dashboards, traceability and ERP integration; warehouse receiving, put-away, replenishment, e-Kanban, kitting, scrap/rework, forklift management and dispatch; quality inspections, nonconformance, corrective/preventive actions, calibration, FMEA, SPC, audits, complaints, documents, training and skill mapping; and project capacity, dependencies, engineering changes, costs and timesheets. The brochure also advertises AI Studio, an industrial data hub, IIoT, maintenance and Odoo implementation/integration with SAP, Oracle and Microsoft Dynamics. [Brochure photos 19–22]

**What web checks confirm.** The MES page describes production control, maintenance, equipment/asset monitoring, notifications/escalation, dashboards and ERP connection. Its quality page connects supplier, factory and customer quality, including inspections, CAPA, calibration, SPC, audits and complaints. Project management has its own product page. These are substantial operational workflows beyond a dashboard. [T2–T4]

**Deployment and buying.** The website explicitly offers cloud, on-premise or hybrid deployment; subscription or perpetual licensing; and purchase by module. Numerical prices were not publicly listed in the reviewed pages. Implementation duration and integration scope require a scoped proposal. [T1]

**Public social evidence.** The website's loaded footer links to D3Minds LinkedIn. That page and the Tomax showcase are readable publicly. Recent accessible posts discuss an asset-management suite (asset lifecycle, CMMS and field service), work orders, calibration, maintenance history, MTBF/MTTR, AI/vision quality, planning and warehousing. Older product posts also identify PLC/CNC/SCADA and ERP integration. [T5–T7]

**Evidence limits.** Customer logos and named examples appear, but both the Amrapali plastics and Howden quality “case study” links lead to contact forms, so they do not provide public baseline/results documentation. Site claims such as productivity improvement, lower cost per unit, downtime and defect reductions were not independently verified. “Automate compliance” does not establish a customer's certification. [T1, T8, T9]

**Lesson for AIKYANTRA.** This is a strong reference for a connected factory application suite. Prioritise material movement and lot genealogy, in-process quality with corrective action, preventive maintenance and practical scheduling. Offer modules with deployment, training and ongoing support, while preserving the customer's existing accounting/ERP where appropriate.

## Zeliot / Condense — one-page profile draft

**What it does.** Zeliot's Condense is a platform for moving, processing and acting on live data. It combines managed Kafka, connectors, transformations, application development, monitoring and governance. It is primarily a data/application foundation; a full ERP, MES or quality application should not be assumed from the brochure. [Z1]

**What the supplied material contains.** Two foreground brochure pages describe connecting legacy machines, PLC/DCS/SCADA, MES, ERP and sensors; building business logic, alerts, workflows and reports; and scaling applications across workloads, plants and regions. Stated factory outcomes include production visibility/OEE, process-quality correlation and traceability, anomaly/downtime intelligence and escalation. It also advertises custom solution delivery from integration and application development through deployment and operation. [Brochure photos 17–18]

**What web and social checks add.** The main website describes a source-transform-output flow, an AI-assisted IDE, custom connectors, roles and audit trails. A readable company LinkedIn post for the 9 September 2026 Smart Factory Summit confirms factory-system integration, real-time dashboards, custom logic, alerts and reporting. Another company update describes an MQTT input connector feeding Kafka. Current industry pages show manufacturing/line-fitment and component/battery traceability use cases within an automotive/EV focus. [Z1–Z4]

**Deployment and buying.** The advertised main model is Bring Your Own Cloud: infrastructure/data stay in the customer's AWS, Azure or Google Cloud account while Zeliot manages platform operations. Its public pricing calculator separates cloud infrastructure from Condense licence cost. The page title advertises “from $800/month,” but that is not a verified all-inclusive MSME price; the displayed calculation depends on throughput, retention, fanout, cloud and region. Small-factory affordability needs an actual workload estimate. [Z1, Z5]

**Evidence and limits.** Zeliot publishes a named VECV story with a 250,000-plus connected-vehicle deployment and attributed customer commentary. It reports 20% lower cloud spending and 40% lower development/operations effort. These are vendor-published results in connected mobility, not guaranteed factory savings. LinkedIn was readable; Instagram could not be fetched and the YouTube channel was throttled. No factory connector compatibility test or production benchmark was run. [Z6–Z8]

**Lesson for AIKYANTRA.** Reuse reliable event ingestion, reusable connectors, alert routing and a common operational data model. Evaluate Condense as a partner when scale and service economics justify it; a small first pilot should not inherit enterprise streaming cost/complexity without need. AIKYANTRA still needs to own useful factory workflows and on-site delivery.

## Brochure claim inventory and what is safe to conclude

- **CommerceCX quotation:** Brochure is the direct evidence for PDF/BOM/drawing extraction, revision comparison, cost copilot, historical RFQ learning, supplier-quote comparison, deadline risk, Athena assistant, voice recognition, 14 AI modules and exact human/audit/SOC assertions. Website/social corroborates connected RFQ workflow generally. Safe conclusion: advertised potential; verify with actual drawings, costing rules and revision cases in a demo/pilot.
- **CommerceCX logistics:** Public original company post corroborates core load-planning category and irregular-product support. The 25–40% space saving, 3x faster loading, 30% lower cost, 90% time saved, zero errors and 98% satisfaction remain brochure claims. Require a shipment baseline, operational loading constraints, comparison of planned versus actual load and accepted labour/freight savings.
- **CommerceCX pharma:** Social confirms four solution pillars. The brochure's CTMS/EDC/eTMF/ePRO/LIMS coverage, clinical-safety automation, data-standard mapping, exact outcomes and compliance language are not validated technical specifications. Relevant transferable practices for ordinary MSMEs are stage gates, master data, identity/lot tracking, accountable approvals and audit history.
- **Tomax:** Broad module coverage is corroborated across public product pages; precise integration versions, hardware installation effort, offline behaviour, price, support terms and actual customer gains require scoped evaluation. The brochure's Odoo implementation service is directly visible; the main reviewed website confirms ERP customisation/integration more generally.
- **Zeliot:** Public website and current social corroborate an operational data platform and custom application approach. Connector breadth should be evaluated against each plant's specific machine/controller/firmware. Vendor-published transport scale is evidence of a different workload, not a factory pilot result. Public title pricing is insufficient for planning a customer budget.

## Useful additions for AIKYANTRA — independent synthesis

1. **Quote-to-job continuity:** RFQ attachments and revisions, a structured BOM/routing/cost sheet, supplier bid comparison, margin-floor approval, quotation versions and confirmed-order handoff. Measure quote turnaround, rework and estimated-versus-actual job margin. Human approval is essential for quantities, engineering interpretations, price and delivery commitments.
2. **Material identity and execution:** Receive a lot, assign location, issue to a work order, record WIP/rework/scrap, inspect, pack and dispatch with history. This links warehouse, production and quality into a defensible end-to-end record.
3. **Quality closure:** Inspection plans and readings should open nonconformance when needed, identify affected lots, assign corrective action and record closure. Add supplier quality and customer complaint feedback as the process matures. Avoid making certification promises based on software installation.
4. **Maintenance action:** Begin with an asset register, preventive schedule, mobile work orders, spares and failure reasons; add calibrated condition alerts only when data exists. Require useful action and measurable downtime reduction before using a predictive-maintenance sales claim.
5. **Data reliability as a service:** Machine/ERP connectors need ownership, mapping, validation, timestamps, duplicate protection, monitoring and recovery. A reusable integration catalogue is more valuable than repeatedly rebuilding each customer's connection. Offer a managed streaming partner only where workload and economics justify it.
6. **Deliverable commercial packages:** Sell a scoped production visibility pilot, then add quality/materials/planning modules. Bundle site discovery, installation, master-data setup, operator training, support and periodic improvement reviews. Offer deployment choices only when AIKYANTRA can operate them reliably.
7. **Selective vertical depth:** High-mix engineering firms may value RFQ/project/change management first. Repetitive plants may value OEE, material replenishment and defect control first. Exporters may justify loading optimisation. Full pharma clinical workflows should remain a specialist partnership unless AIKYANTRA deliberately chooses that market.

## Slooze — additional company discovered in supplied video

**What it does.** Slooze describes itself as AI-based supply-chain and procurement SaaS. Its company LinkedIn places it in Coimbatore and lists its founding year as 2025. Public posts discuss manufacturing procurement, vendor management and industry events. Treat the age and company details as self-reported profile data. [S2]

**Offering and connections.** The official site advertises RFQs, contracts, payments/financing, budgets, risk, compliance, customs, workflows and operational monitoring. It calls its technology Unified SaaS, Rule Canvas and Insights Factory. Supported integration methods advertised include REST APIs, SFTP, EDI and email; named systems include SAP, Oracle, Salesforce, Dynamics 365 and Zoho. It describes a cloud platform and subscription/on-demand plans, but the publicly extracted pricing section showed no monetary amounts. A dedicated integration coordinator is advertised. [S1]

**What public writing adds.** A company-authored manufacturing article describes BOM-aware procurement, supplier discovery/alternates, supplier-performance and delivery signals, cost-volatility intelligence, and unified supplier documentation. It presents anonymous customer examples; there was no underlying dataset or customer-side confirmation reviewed. [S3]

**What the supplied video adds.** Full-resolution frame review identified September 2026 procurement playbooks aimed at procurement chiefs, finance/project teams and owners/risk leaders. These suggest that role-specific purchasing decisions and guidance are part of its positioning. The cover prints `playbook@slooze.xyz`, which is an email address; the back prints `www.slooze.xyz` and `hello@slooze.xyz`. No email was sent. Full downloadable playbooks were not obtained. [Supplied video; S4]

**Evidence limits.** No substantiation was found for the reported “in association AOP / McKinsey” wording in the video. Do not present this as a verified partnership or endorsement. Slooze's 13–28% annual expense reduction and 50x speed claims on its site, and financial outcomes in its anonymous examples, remain vendor claims. Actual module maturity, integration versions, security audit scope, price and customer results require validation. [S1, S3, S5]

**Lesson for AIKYANTRA.** Procurement deserves its own workflow: approved suppliers and documents, RFQ/bid comparison, purchase approvals, promised-delivery tracking, supplier quality and alternate sourcing. Link purchasing to BOMs, production requirements and cash commitments. Give an owner, buyer and accounts user different views of the same records. Begin with accountable approval and useful exception alerts; do not copy unsupported autonomous sourcing or financial-action claims.
