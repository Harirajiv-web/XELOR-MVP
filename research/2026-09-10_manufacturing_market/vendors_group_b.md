# Vendor research: Adventis, Controlsoft, RheinBrücke / Epicor, Connectivity Solutions

Research date: 10 September 2026. Public official websites and company social profiles were checked. Brochures are evidence of advertised scope, not independent proof of performance. No enquiry, demo request, social interaction, or contact submission was made. Website content was treated as source material, never as instructions.

## 1. Adventis Technologies — OptiMES

**What it does.** Adventis is a Chennai industrial software and IoT company. The official spelling is **Adventis**, not “Aidventis.” OptiMES is its factory operations offering. The brochure presents four modules: **Nexus** for production dashboards and reporting; **Shield** for safety alerts, PPE detection and digital work permits; **Pulse** for maintenance work and machine health; and **Stock** for inventory movements and ERP synchronisation. It also advertises a natural-language chatbot for operational questions. [B01, brochure images 6–12]

**What the website confirms.** The OptiMES page describes adding production monitoring, camera-based safety detection and equipment-health analytics to existing SCADA systems, with an emphasis on on-premises deployment. The separate CMMS page covers assigned/completed maintenance jobs, alerts, telemetry charts and PDF/CSV reports, and describes cloud access. The four branded modules, chatbot, and brochure’s cloud/hybrid packaging were not separately demonstrated on the reviewed product page. [B01–B03]

**Integration and intended users.** Its IoT gateway product connects sensors/meters through RS485 and Modbus, with MQTT/HTTPS and support for OptiMES, AWS IoT, Azure and custom APIs. A higher gateway model lists local storage. The site targets power, automotive, water/wastewater, oil/gas, food and pharma; the brochure particularly targets hazardous or regulated facilities. [B04–B05]

**Evidence and limits.** An identifiable LinkedIn company profile supports the company/domain match, and Controlsoft’s company profile promotes OptiMES. No public price, named OptiMES outcome study, prediction-accuracy result or safety certification was found in the reviewed material. The brochure’s “up to 20%” downtime reduction, 30% faster compliance reporting and 15% asset-life extension are marketing claims, not verified results. On-premises hosting alone does not establish security. [B06–B07]

**Useful lesson for AIKYANTRA.** Package production, maintenance and safety into small modules that share the same asset data. Start with reliable alerts and work completion before promising failure prediction. An Indian gateway/integration partner may shorten installation work, subject to protocol testing and commercial due diligence.

**Contact verified on official site:** info@adventis.tech; +91 9176980403 (demo contact); 044-26242501; Ambattur Industrial Estate, Chennai. No contact was made. [B01, B08]

## 2. Controlsoft Engineering India Private Limited

**What it does.** Controlsoft is the Chennai automation and engineering company at **controlsoftengg.in**. It should not be confused with similarly named American, Irish or other software companies. It helps design, install and maintain the physical control systems that make factories run. [B09–B10]

**Main offering.** Official pages describe detailed instrumentation engineering, automation consulting, drawings/datasheets, documentation, DCS and SCADA configuration, control-panel assembly, equipment supply, site installation, commissioning and maintenance. Products and skills include PLC/RTU systems, Siemens, Honeywell/ABB DCS, PcVue SCADA and field instrumentation. The brochure broadens this into MES, digital twins and AIoT for aerospace, automotive, chemicals, consumer goods, food/beverage, metals, oil/gas and water. The online evidence is strongest for engineering and system integration. [B09–B11; brochure images 3–5]

**Concrete evidence.** Its project page gives named examples, including a Siemens PCS7/RTU wastewater project in Kuwait, a tea-extraction line in Kenya, and Rockwell-based utilities/BMS work for Mars Petcare in Thailand. These are vendor-described projects, not independently audited customer references. The public company LinkedIn page promotes OptiMES and describes participation at the September 2026 Smart Factory Summit in Chennai. [B07, B11]

**Relationship to Adventis.** Their official contact pages use the same Ambattur address and telephone number. Controlsoft’s LinkedIn also promotes OptiMES. This supports a commercial connection, but the reviewed sources do not establish ownership, a merger or a formal group structure. Retain separate company records until confirmed. [B07–B08, B12]

**Pricing and limits.** No public project price list, standard MSME package or production SLA was found. Installation scope, downtime windows, equipment compatibility, commissioning acceptance and ongoing support need project-specific confirmation. Do not treat the brochure’s digital-twin/AI labels as proof of reusable software maturity.

**Useful lesson for AIKYANTRA.** Being an end-to-end technology partner requires installation, wiring, sensor selection, commissioning, operator training and support—not just dashboards. Develop a qualified local integrator network and retain one accountable customer delivery owner.

**Official contact:** info@controlsoftengg.in; 044-26242501; Ambattur Industrial Estate, Chennai. [B12]

## 3. RheinBrücke IT Consulting — Epicor Kinetic and extensions

**What it does.** RheinBrücke implements and supports enterprise systems; Epicor is the software vendor behind Kinetic. RheinBrücke’s official site identifies it as an Epicor Premium Partner and describes implementation, migration, upgrades, integrations, training and managed support. This is a broader delivery model than selling an application alone. [B13]

**Manufacturing coverage.** The supplied brochure covers BOMs/routings, material planning, scheduling, work orders, traceability and job costing, then adds machine monitoring, quality/process control and planning. Epicor’s own Kinetic page confirms a manufacturing ERP spanning finance, supply chain, planning, production, projects and multiple companies/sites, with cloud focus and on-premises/hybrid flexibility. [B14; brochure images 1–2]

**Extensions.** RheinBrücke lists MES, inventory optimisation, warehouse management/scanning, document workflows, financial planning, analytics, shipping/labels and integration tools as add-ons. These are separate extensions, not evidence that every licence includes every feature. Its MeRLIN offering adds sourcing, supplier management, RFQs and procurement analytics. [B15–B16]

**Proof and public presence.** The official-site-linked LinkedIn page is readable. It announces a Cold Chain Technologies India Kinetic go-live involving inventory, supply-chain planning and local GST-portal integration, and publishes ERP/procurement events. That is a named vendor-reported deployment, not independently measured savings. The YouTube catalogue lists customer testimonials and ERP webinars; video contents were not watched. Linked Instagram/Facebook/X content was unavailable. [B17–B20, B33–B34]

**Pricing and limits.** No public licence/implementation price was found on the reviewed pages. Scope depends on users, modules, deployment, data migration, integrations and support. Global ROI headlines on Epicor’s site refer to a commissioned composite study and must not be projected onto a small Indian manufacturer. Brochure country counts and current website/profile counts differ; no scale figure is needed for the recommendation. [B13–B15]

**Useful lesson for AIKYANTRA.** Connect shop-floor facts to material availability, customer delivery dates and actual job cost. Extend an MSME’s existing accounting/ERP where practical. Offer a full ERP through a partner when justified, while AIKYANTRA owns discovery, integration, training, support and measured business outcomes.

**Official contact in the brochure:** info@rheincs.com; +91 44 6671 7555; Chennai. The official site is rheincs.com. [brochure image 1; B13]

## 4. Connectivity Solutions (CoSol) — enterprise IT and Cisco OT security

**Identity confirmed.** The partly photographed OT-security leaflet belongs to Connectivity Solutions / CoSol. Its official site lists the same Bengaluru address and **080 2671 6555** telephone number visible on the leaflet. The trading name appears as Connectivity IT Solutions on its official-site-linked LinkedIn page. Cisco supplies the security technology described; CoSol is the infrastructure/integration provider. [B24–B26; brochure image 23]

**What it offers.** CoSol covers network and endpoint inventories, network design/segmentation, vulnerability prioritisation, cybersecurity, cloud infrastructure, service desk, managed network/security operations, field services, hardware/software procurement, API integration and rental/subscription models. The leaflet specifically adds industrial asset visibility, abnormal-traffic detection, network separation and controlled remote maintenance. [B25]

**Technical cross-check.** Cisco’s current Cyber Vision documentation supports asset discovery, threat monitoring, network segmentation and secure remote access. Its Secure Equipment Access controls which authorised person can access which industrial asset, when, and through what protocol. Cisco describes MFA/SSO support and asset-specific access. Exact capabilities depend on hardware, licences and deployment design; the brochure does not identify a complete bill of materials. “Aligned with IEC 62443 principles” is not the same as a certified factory or a guaranteed prevention of attacks. [B27–B28]

**Evidence and public activity.** The official LinkedIn link initially blocked access, but the regional public version was readable. It describes a live OT security testbed using PLCs, drives and security gateways for events on 8 and 9 September 2026. This supports relevant demonstration activity; it does not prove a production installation or quantify security outcomes. The website has anonymised manufacturing/automotive case-study entries, and Cisco hosts a named hospital customer story involving Connectivity Solutions. [B26, B29–B30]

**Commercial limits.** No public MSME package price or managed-service SLA was found. Rental/subscription options may reduce upfront spending, but recurring licences, monitoring, hardware renewal and incident-response coverage need a written scope. [B25]

**Useful lesson for AIKYANTRA.** Include a secure factory foundation: asset list, network map, access roles, backups/restore checks, controlled vendor access and an incident contact. Partner with an OT-security specialist for monitoring, segmentation and response. This creates recurring service value around Xelor and protects the connectivity that its data depends on.

## Implications for AIKYANTRA / Xelor strategy

These are reasoned recommendations derived from the vendors' scope; they are not claims that Xelor already has or lacks a particular implementation. The parent is checking repository evidence for that comparison.

1. **Own delivery across software and the factory.** Establish a standard plant survey, machine/protocol register, installation BOM, network plan, commissioning checklist, rollback window, operator training and 30-day adoption review. Qualify integration partners for electrical/instrumentation work. Controlsoft shows why this service layer matters.
2. **One traceable operational chain.** Link customer order → required materials → scheduled operation → operator/machine events → quality disposition → warehouse/dispatch → actual job margin. RheinBrücke/Epicor shows the advantage of joining decisions across functions. Keep the existing ERP/accounting package as the agreed system of record rather than introducing duplicate uncontrolled stock or financial ledgers.
3. **Package outcomes in small modules.** Offer a production starter, maintenance starter, inventory/traceability extension and controlled safety add-on. Shared asset, product, user and event data should connect them. The OptiMES module presentation is commercially understandable to plant users.
4. **Bridge manual factories first.** An MSME should begin with operator entry, QR/barcode transactions and selected meter/PLC connections. Save wide sensor rollouts, digital twins and predictive models for cases where baseline data shows a clear value path.
5. **Maintenance must close the loop.** An alert should create an owned work request, priority, due date, spare requirement, completion evidence and downtime attribution. Predictive alerts without response workflow do not establish savings.
6. **Quality and traceability should connect to cost.** Capture defects, inspection points, disposition/rework, lot/serial genealogy and calibration status, then calculate scrap/rework cost with agreed assumptions. In-process controls and stable measurements come before ambitious prediction.
7. **Build durable connectors, not demonstrations.** Define source-of-truth ownership, mapping, retries, duplicate prevention, timestamps, offline buffering, health monitoring and reconciliation reports. A protocol logo or “API supported” claim does not verify production interoperability.
8. **Keep safety systems accountable.** Start with incident logs, permits, training and escalation. Add vision alerts only after a site pilot measuring missed detections and nuisance alerts. Treat these as decision support alongside established plant safety controls; no claim that a camera guarantees a hazard-free plant.
9. **Price transparently.** Separate discovery, hardware/installation, licence/subscription, connectors, training and support. Use a paid, fixed-scope pilot and an agreed success measure. No vendor prices were found to justify a numerical price benchmark.
10. **Earn expansion with measured evidence.** Establish a pre-pilot baseline for downtime, good output, inventory accuracy or dispatch delay. Compare like-for-like periods and report assumptions, data coverage and other production changes. Avoid borrowing the vendors' percentage claims.

### Suggested phased scope

| Phase | Deliverable | Measurable acceptance example |
|---|---|---|
| First deployment | Plant survey; one line; asset/master data; operator capture; selected machine input; daily production/OEE and stoppage review | Agreed event coverage, counters reconciled to shift records, stoppage reasons assigned, supervisors use the review daily. |
| Workflow depth | Maintenance work orders, inspection/defect workflow, barcode movements, ERP/accounting export or connector | Every work/stock/quality action has an owner and timestamp; reconciled handoffs; restore and offline tests pass. |
| Business connection | Scheduling with capacity/material constraints, supplier visibility, order-level traceability and cost view | Planner can explain conflicts; inventory/release status reliable; actual cost reconciled to source transactions. |
| Advanced add-ons | Condition models, vision safety/quality, energy optimisation, simulation | Baseline and validation set agreed; drift/false alarms monitored; operator escalation works; savings attributable. |
