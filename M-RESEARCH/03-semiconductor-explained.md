# Semiconductors explained: what India makes and where a new project fits

Research status: **22 September 2026**. This is a simplified technical and commercial explanation, not process-engineering instructions. Operational claims below come from dated government or company reports; forecasts and investment plans are identified separately.

## From a chip design to a working product

A semiconductor business can participate at several different stages. These stages have different equipment, skills, customers and investment needs.

1. **Design:** Engineers decide what a chip does and develop the circuit and physical layout. A fabless company owns or licenses a design and buys manufacturing services. Designing an Indian chip does not establish that its wafers were made in India.
2. **Making blank wafers:** Silicon is purified, melted and grown into a large crystal. The crystal is sliced into thin discs, then flattened, polished, cleaned and inspected. These blank wafers have no working chip circuits yet. This material-making business operates alongside chip design. [SUMCO's wafer-production explanation](https://www.sumcosi.com/english/products/process/)
3. **Building circuits on wafers:** A fab repeatedly adds thin layers, prints tiny patterns and removes selected material to build circuits. A 300 mm wafer describes its diameter; 28 nm describes a technology generation. [ASML's manufacturing explanation](https://www.asml.com/en/company/stories/2021/semiconductor-manufacturing-process-steps)
4. **Wafer testing and separation:** Electrical tests identify working chips, called dies. The wafer may be thinned, receive additional connections and then be separated into individual dies. The exact sequence varies.
5. **Packaging and final testing:** The die receives electrical connections, protection and a way to remove heat. Advanced packaging can connect several dies through closely spaced connections. ATMP means assembly, testing, marking and packaging; OSAT means an independent assembly-and-test service provider.
6. **Final systems:** Packaged chips are mounted on circuit boards and incorporated into servers, vehicles, phones and industrial controls. Building a server is a different business from fabricating its processor.

Packaging is real manufacturing. It can add substantial technical value without making the transistors inside the incoming die. Equally, an advanced-packaging plant does not automatically have the processes, licences, customer qualification or memory supply needed to package a leading GPU with high-bandwidth memory, or HBM.

## What is actually operating in India

On 17 September 2026, MeitY reported **five commercial units within twelve approved Semicon 1.0 projects**, including assembly/test/packaging operations and CDIL's discrete-device line. This is a programme count, not a census of every historical Indian semiconductor facility. Tata's Dholera fab and Jagiroad OSAT facility remained under construction. [MeitY, 17 September 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2311726&lang=1&reg=3)

| Project | Published status | What the evidence supports |
|---|---|---|
| Micron, Sanand | Commercial production by 28 February 2026 | Assembly and test of DRAM and NAND products using wafers from Micron's global manufacturing network |
| Kaynes | Included in the five commercial programme units on 17 September | Operating backend manufacturing; the programme count does not establish GPU or HBM capability |
| CG Semi, Sanand | Commercial production commenced on 4 July 2026 | OSAT serving automotive and industrial applications, with Renesas as partner |
| CDIL, Mohali | New commercial line inaugurated on 17 September | Discrete-device production line |
| Suchi Semicon, Surat | Commercial line inaugurated on 17 September | Packaging, including the announced commercial QFN product |
| Tata, Dholera and Jagiroad | Under construction on 17 September | Planned wafer fabrication and OSAT capacity, respectively |

Micron explicitly describes its Indian operation as conventional assembly and test. Its first shipment went to Dell for laptops made in India. This does not establish Indian fabrication of DRAM wafers, HBM manufacturing or GPU manufacturing. [Micron, 28 February 2026](https://micron.gcs-web.com/node/50101)

CG Semi's announcement identifies automotive, scooter and industrial uses and intended exports to Japan, the United States and Europe. These are useful examples of a commercial manufacturing route beyond leading-edge AI processors. [MeitY, 4 July 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2281174&lang=1&reg=3)

## Where demand pressure is real

Company reports identify particular constraints; they do not demonstrate a shortage of every semiconductor product.

- **Memory:** Micron reported DRAM and NAND demand exceeding supply in June 2026. Its expectation that tightness would extend beyond 2027 is a forecast, not an observed future outcome. [Micron prepared remarks, 24 June 2026](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/Q3-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)
- **Advanced packaging and selected power chips:** TSMC reported packaging capacity constraining customer growth. It also distinguished tight supply for particular AI-related power-management chips and sensors, including mature-node products, from weaker consumer-oriented mature-node demand. [TSMC earnings transcript, 16 July 2026, pages 10 and 21](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-08/3e494f0c14dd0890f897aa044415e21d93486cc4/TSMC%202Q26%20Transcript.pdf)
- **Package substrates:** IBIDEN described industry supply shortages, rising prices and requests for large, high-layer-count substrates, alongside GPU, networking and high-end CPU demand. A package substrate is the specialised connection platform inside a package; it is not an ordinary motherboard. [IBIDEN results Q&A, 5 August 2026, pages 3–4](https://www.ibiden.com/ir/items/en_QA_FY2026Q1.pdf)

These reports justify investigating specific products with prospective customers. They do not guarantee that an unqualified new supplier can immediately sell into the constrained segment.

## Market size and government support

NITI Aayog reports **US$23.8 billion of Indian integrated-circuit imports under HS 8542 in calendar 2024**. That is a defined trade category, not all electronics imports, total semiconductor consumption, or the value of chips embedded in imported finished equipment. [NITI Trade Watch, February 2026, printed pages 17–18](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-02/Trade-Watch-Quarterly-July-September-Q2-FY-2025-26.pdf)

The government's September backgrounder describes a semiconductor-market forecast of approximately US$110 billion by FY2030 and Semicon 2.0's approved ₹1,27,500 crore outlay. A forecast is potential market demand; an approved scheme outlay is a programme envelope. Neither is money already paid to a project or a customer's purchase commitment. [MeitY backgrounder, 17 September 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2311220&lang=1&reg=48)

For context, the **31 August 2026 Gazette** specifies these central support rates on eligible capital expenditure for large manufacturing facilities. They are not automatic grants for suppliers serving those facilities.

| Manufacturing category | Central support |
|---|---:|
| Silicon wafer fab | 40% |
| Compound, discrete, MEMS or photonics fab | 35% |
| Advanced packaging or advanced substrates | 35% |
| Legacy packaging | 25% |

Support is on a pari-passu basis, subject to eligibility, appraisal and approval. Advanced packaging requires at least ₹1,000 crore capital investment, qualifying group/JV revenue of ₹200 crore and owned or licensed production-grade technology. The earlier programme's 50% rate should not be applied automatically to a new proposal. State support requires separate eligibility and confirmation. [Official Gazette, pages 15–17](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/275860.pdf)

## What this means for the current business search

The current focus is businesses supplying semiconductor factories: testing and automation equipment, inspection systems, metal leadframes that support and connect chips, vacuum equipment, and carriers that protect wafers or parts during handling. These are distinct product businesses with different technical requirements. Specialised package substrates remain an ambitious option conditional on a suitable technology partner, substantial funding and customer qualification.

A strong network can help obtain introductions and specific customer requirements. The next step is to compare defined products, technical partners and realistic qualification routes, as discussed in [04 — Network questions and findings](04-network-questions-and-findings.md). This explanation does not select a final investment; the large-factory policy background above does not change the current scope into building a wafer fab.
