# Bengaluru manufacturing opportunity: electrical subassemblies, EVs and AI infrastructure

## Recommendation

For a first manufacturing venture in Bengaluru with ₹1–30 crore available, my provisional choice is **custom power-distribution subassemblies for industrial equipment makers**: start with formed, drilled/punched, plated and insulated copper/aluminium conductor kits and busbars for UPS, variable-frequency drives, electrical panels and power converters. Add matching mechanical mounting kits and assemblies only when customers request them. Industrial and energy customers should support the initial business; EV and data-centre demand should expand it.

This is an investment hypothesis, not demonstrated local unmet demand. It becomes investable only after procurement interviews, sample drawings, successful prototypes and commercially viable customer orders. Avoid buying a full busbar, sheet-metal, wiring-harness and cooling factory simultaneously.

Bengaluru provides unusually relevant nearby customers and engineers. ABB inaugurated its second Nelamangala campus in March 2026 and is expanding Peenya. Its release says power-management production for data centres will expand nearly tenfold and that its LV/MV systems already serve more than 30% of Indian hyperscale data centres. These are company statements, not independent market-share verification. They support prospecting into an existing ecosystem, not an assumption that ABB will buy from a new supplier.[1]

The counterargument is substantial: Mersen already offers sophisticated laminated busbars and has a Bommasandra presence; Rittal has a Doddaballapur location; ABB and Schneider possess strong engineering, brand and procurement systems. A new entrant must win a specific combination of delivery time, minimum order quantity, design responsiveness, cost and documented quality. “EV/AI growth” alone is no competitive advantage.[2][3]

## What the evidence establishes

**Power and electrification provide a broader demand base than EVs alone.** The government's 2025 power-sector summary describes a ₹9.15 lakh crore transmission plan through 2032. This is a planned national infrastructure outlay, not a market-size estimate for LV components or guaranteed spend accessible to a startup.[4] Existing industrial equipment, renewable generation, UPS systems, storage and replacement demand offer exposure across different capital-expenditure cycles.

**AI demand is real, but orders are concentrated and construction can slip.** IEA's April 2026 central projection takes global data-centre electricity use from 485 TWh in 2025 to 950 TWh in 2030, while stressing equipment bottlenecks and financing sensitivity. This is global demand, not Indian demand. Google reported groundbreaking for its Visakhapatnam AI hub on 28 April 2026; its approximately $15 billion commitment covers several kinds of infrastructure and cannot be treated as a local component procurement budget.[5][6]

**Cooling is not unoccupied territory.** Schneider launched a Bengaluru Motivair liquid-cooling factory in February 2026. Vertiv's Chakan thermal-management factory opened in January 2024. These investments create possible supplier relationships while making direct competition in complete cooling systems considerably harder.[7][8] Schneider's August 2026 practitioner article explicitly stresses integrated commissioning, spares and lifecycle support, not merely component availability.[9]

**EV growth and supply-chain dependence coexist.** IEA reports Indian electric two/three-wheeler sales grew over 30% year on year in Q1 2026. However, global electric-car sales fell 8% in that quarter, showing policy and geography matter. China supplied over 80% of battery-cell production in 2025. Global lithium-ion nameplate capacity exceeded 4 TWh; battery manufacturers face difficult economics and slow production-yield ramps. A battery-cell factory is inappropriate for this budget and first-time-operator profile.[10][11]

**Subsidy details have changed.** The PM E-DRIVE portal identifies a 10 August 2026 amendment extending electric two-wheeler support to 31 March 2028 and shows a revised ₹11,900 crore total scheme outlay. Older introductory text on the same site still contains the original end date. Use the dated amendment and updated tables, and check category-specific availability; subsidy-supported volumes are not the entire EV market.[12]

## Candidate products and entry barriers

| Product | First-venture assessment | Main commercial and execution constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Formed conductor/busbar kits for UPS, drives and panel OEMs | Preferred starting hypothesis | Copper financing, entrenched vendors, rejection control and commodity-like pricing |
| Custom industrial enclosures and mechanical mounting kits | Good if there are paid drawings/orders; secondary to one core process | Local fabricators and Rittal; low differentiation without precision, finish, sealing or fast delivery |
| Industrial cable assemblies and small-batch harnesses | Viable lower-capital alternative | Connector sourcing, crimp consistency and labour productivity; avoid assuming easy passenger-car OEM access |
| Complete LV switchboards under a proven system architecture | Conditional on experienced electrical team and partner channel | Design/routine verification, commissioning, warranty and receivables |
| EV high-voltage harnesses, battery interconnects | Attractive later after qualification | Automotive quality systems, programme dependence, tooling and field-failure exposure |
| Cooling manifolds, frames and specified pipe assemblies | Second-stage option with a cooling OEM sponsor | Leak cleanliness, fluid/material compatibility and project qualification |
| Chip cold plates or complete coolant distribution units | Do not launch speculatively | Thermal R&D, joining processes, leak performance, controls, qualification and service obligations |
| Battery packs/BESS integration | Only with contracted application, cell source and warranty capability | Imported cells, safety, degradation, BMS integration and warranty cash exposure |
| Solar modules, battery cells, rare-earth magnets | Reject as the default first venture | Scale economics, technology, trade and policy risk exceed the apparent import-substitution opportunity |

These are analyst judgments. Capex and qualification times depend on exact drawings, volumes, equipment sourcing and customer standards. There is no credible single “busbar factory margin” or “EV-component capex” that applies to every row.

**Busbar quality is more than bending copper.** Mersen's engineering material describes dielectric selection, plating control, dimensional accuracy and controlled lamination. Begin with conventional formed and insulated conductor assemblies; laminated high-voltage designs require a higher process and validation standard. Treat thin dielectric laminates and high-frequency power electronics as a separate capability upgrade.[2]

**Panels carry system responsibility.** IEC 61439-1:2020, together with the applicable product part, specifies construction and verification requirements for LV assemblies; its official listing incorporates 2021 and 2023 corrigenda. A collection of individually certified breakers and enclosures does not itself prove the final panel's conformity. Clarify design verification, routine checks, temperature rise and short-circuit responsibilities with the OEM/testing laboratory before pricing.[13] Customer-specific Indian requirements and export-destination standards must be checked for the exact assembly, not guessed from this general reference.

**Harnesses are competitive, not an overlooked simple assembly business.** Motherson describes itself as the Indian passenger-car harness market leader and has integrated design, testing, engineering and components capability. Competing first in entire passenger-vehicle harness programmes is less attractive than industrial machinery, selected commercial-vehicle subassemblies or paid development work. Automotive buyers may demand APQP/PPAP documentation and IATF-related quality requirements; obtain each buyer's actual vendor manual.[14]

## Bengaluru customer access and a phased plant

Target buying functions in established UPS, drive, switchgear, industrial-machine and power-converter businesses. ABB's Nelamangala/Peenya ecosystem and Schneider's Bengaluru power/cooling operations are relevant prospect clusters, not confirmed open tenders. Include medium-size equipment makers so that procurement access does not depend on one multinational.

A practical initial proposition is: manufacture to the customer's released drawing; provide documented material traceability, dimensional and electrical inspection, agreed plating/insulation, rapid sample iterations and repeatable delivery. Compete for a defined part family that is troublesome for the incumbent, rather than asking buyers to replace an entire established vendor.

**Indicative planning allocations, not quotations:**

| Available total capital | Sensible scope |
|---|---|
| ₹1–3 crore | Rented small unit, engineering/quality/assembly, selected crimping or fabrication equipment; outsource substantial cutting, plating and finishing. Main priority is working capital and paid prototypes. |
| ₹4–10 crore | Focused conductor/busbar operation with selected CNC punching/bending or machining, fixtures, deburring, insulation/assembly, inspection and electrical testing; outsource plating and specialist finishes. |
| ₹10–20 crore | Add automation, capacity and one validated adjacent process after repeat orders; retain meaningful working-capital and ramp-loss reserve. |
| ₹20–30 crore | Scale a proven product family or acquire a qualified small supplier after diligence; do not spend the whole amount on land and machinery ahead of orders. |

For example, an **illustrative ₹8 crore envelope** could allocate ₹3 crore to equipment/fixtures/test capability, ₹0.75 crore to lease fit-out/utilities/IT, ₹0.75 crore to development/qualification and initial operating losses, ₹2.5 crore to working capital, and ₹1 crore contingency. This excludes land purchase and is a feasibility placeholder; collect vendor quotations, GST timing, lease terms and staffing estimates before relying on it. A new entrant may need less machinery but more operating runway than an established manufacturer.

Plan an initial 6–12 months for industrial customer development and qualification as a management assumption, with longer stress scenarios; automotive and hyperscale-related approvals can extend beyond that. Neither location near a buyer nor an ISO certificate guarantees approval.

## Unit economics that can invalidate the recommendation

Use contribution after direct materials, subcontract work, variable labour, energy, rejection, freight and warranty provision. Treat owner salary, maintenance, compliance, quality personnel and selling expense as real costs. EBITDA is not distributable cash; interest, tax, depreciation and replacement capex also matter.

For illustration only, ₹12 crore annual revenue at 20% contribution produces ₹2.4 crore before fixed operating costs. With ₹1.5 crore fixed cash expense, EBITDA would be ₹0.9 crore, or 7.5%. Break-even revenue is ₹7.5 crore. Revenue falling to ₹8.4 crore at the same contribution gives only ₹0.18 crore EBITDA. A 25% contribution mix would produce very different results. Do not assume premium margins just because end demand is labelled AI.

Copper needs contractual treatment. If copper represents 60% of selling value and its cost rises 15% without repricing, the margin impact is approximately 9 percentage points of revenue before behavioural changes. Metal-index adjustment, agreed scrap accounting, customer-supplied material or short quote validity can reduce exposure. Aluminium substitution changes thermal/mechanical design and joint validation; it is not an automatic drop-in cost saving.

Working capital is equally important. At ₹12 crore sales, 90 receivable days, 60 inventory days and 30 supplier-credit days, assuming annual material purchases of ₹7.2 crore, approximate receivables plus material inventory less payables total ₹3.55 crore. This simplified illustration excludes GST, work-in-process conversion costs and cash balances. Profitability can coexist with an urgent cash shortage.

## International and policy risks

China's advantage is a dense supplier, equipment and materials ecosystem, not just low wages. Insulation systems, specialised connectors, power semiconductors, battery cells and manufacturing equipment can remain imported even when final assembly is Indian. Obtain dual-source qualifications where possible; record foreign-currency and lead-time exposure at bill-of-material level.

Rare-earth localisation is a strategic priority, not proof that small entrants can manufacture magnets economically. A February 2026 government note describes a ₹7,280 crore scheme for 6,000 tonnes per year of integrated capacity. Reuters reported in June that major conglomerates were exploring Andhra Pradesh facilities; another June report describes IREL pursuing overseas resources and longer production timelines. Different projects have different dates, so announced year-end targets should not be treated as industry-wide production readiness.[15][16]

Solar is a warning about policy-dependent economics. The US announced preliminary 125.87% countervailing subsidy rates on in-scope Indian solar cells/modules in February 2026. A May notice scheduled the final anti-dumping determination for 10 September 2026; the final outcome was not verified in this memo, and the February rate is not represented as the current all-in duty. Domestic ALMM cell rules also have exemptions: the July 2026 ministry notice extended a limited window for net-metering/open-access projects to December 2026.[17][18][19] Do not build an export business model from a generic assumption that Indian origin avoids trade barriers.

Export initially through established OEM customers or a defined overseas equipment buyer, using written technical and landed-cost requirements. Direct exports may improve diversification but introduce certification, logistics, warranty, currency and collection costs. Bulky low-value enclosures face a different freight problem from compact high-value assemblies.

## Investment gates

Before releasing major capex, obtain roughly 20–30 structured buyer interviews, several actual drawings/RFQs, three paid prototype engagements and at least two credible routes to repeat business. These are proposed diligence gates, not sourced industry norms. Ask each buyer why an additional supplier is needed, what annual volumes are credible, who pays tooling, what approval tests apply and how prices adjust.

Reject or redesign the plan if buyers want only lowest-price commodity parts, require unfinanced long credit, cannot explain a supplier gap, or demand a full certified product/service network beyond the team's competence. Stress a six-month qualification delay, 30% volume shortfall, copper inflation, one lost customer and slow collections. Require the model to survive without subsidy and without speculative land appreciation.

**Final judgment:** diversified industrial electrical subassemblies offer the best combination of Bengaluru fit, manageable staged capex and exposure to electrification, EVs and AI. The moat must be engineering-assisted execution and customer qualification. If buyer discovery does not confirm a specific opening, an experienced operator and an existing qualified business are worth more than buying a fashionable product line.

## Appendix: critique of the ₹8 crore model under consideration

A proposed ₹8 crore allocation of machinery ₹1.90 crore, quality/tooling ₹0.70 crore, lease/fit-out ₹0.60 crore, qualification engineering ₹0.45 crore, ramp losses ₹0.75 crore, working capital ₹2.60 crore and contingency ₹1.00 crore sums correctly. It is a discussion envelope, not a vendor-quoted project budget. Keep specialist plating and laser-cutting outsourced initially; thermal/short-circuit validation can also be bought from appropriate laboratories where needed.

The scenario arithmetic is correct: ₹13.5 crore sales at 28% contribution less ₹2.2 crore fixed expense yields ₹1.58 crore EBITDA (11.7%); ₹8 crore at 24% less ₹2.2 crore yields a ₹0.28 crore loss; ₹18 crore at 30% less ₹2.6 crore yields ₹2.8 crore. However, calling 28% the “base” may wrongly suggest market evidence. For pure cut-and-bent copper, that contribution assumption needs proof, especially if direct labour, plating, rejects, packing, outbound freight, sales commissions and warranty provision have not all been included.

At ₹13.5 crore revenue, a 20% contribution instead produces only ₹0.50 crore EBITDA with the same fixed expense. A 15% copper-cost increase where copper costs initially equal 60% of revenue reduces contribution by nine percentage points without repricing—approximately ₹1.215 crore of EBITDA on that revenue. Report copper-indexed selling-value growth separately from value-added growth.

Working capital is a possible hidden shortfall. With ₹13.5 crore sales, material purchases assumed at 60% of revenue, 90 receivable days, 60 inventory days and 30 payable days, the simplified requirement is about ₹4.0 crore, before GST and conversion costs in work in process. At 60 receivable days, 45 inventory days and 30 payable days, it is approximately ₹2.55 crore. Thus ₹2.6 crore works only with disciplined commercial terms and cash-cycle performance; it is not automatically adequate because annual EBITDA is positive. Ramp losses and contingency must not be double-counted as permanently available working capital.

The stated ₹18 crore capacity is not a physical engineering estimate until linked to a SKU mix, price per part, metal weight, cycle/setup time, accepted yield, shifts and utilisation. One large copper-price change can alter turnover “capacity” while the factory makes exactly the same number of parts. Track bottleneck hours, accepted kits and contribution per machine hour.

A more coherent first SKU family is **build-to-print insulated formed conductor kits for industrial UPS/VFD/power-converter cabinets**. Variants can share the same operations: straight/offset/riser busbar sets; battery-to-rectifier cabinet links; and small-batch retrofit or model-change conductor kits. Operations are cut/punch/bend/deburr, outsourced plating, sleeve/insulation application, captive hardware or standoff assembly, serialisation and inspection. This does not require launching a complete cabinet line, automotive harness line or cooling factory. Avoid starting with cell-level battery interconnects, high-frequency laminated HV busbars or a proprietary complete distribution system.

The proposed contribution should therefore be presented as a customer-validation threshold or upside scenario. Require several priced RFQs to prove conversion value, rejection allowance and willingness to pay for engineering/fast-turn service before adopting it as the investment base case.
