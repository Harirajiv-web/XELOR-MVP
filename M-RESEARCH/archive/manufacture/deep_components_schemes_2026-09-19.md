# India Electronics Component Incentives & Component-Level Demand
**Deep research report — as of 19 September 2026**
Prepared for: prospective manufacturer, Bengaluru. Ranking basis = market potential + strength of government-driven demand pull. Capex is NOT a ranking factor. Order-winning ability is NOT assessed — this judges the market only.

**Currency note:** DGCI&S trade data below is reported in US$ million. Where I convert to INR I use ₹84.6/US$ for FY2024-25 and ₹88/US$ for FY2025-26 and label the result "derived".

**Employment linkage:** of the total turnover-linked incentive rate, **1 percentage point** is disbursed only on meeting the employment threshold. Of the 25% capex incentive, **5 percentage points** are disbursed only on meeting the employment threshold. (Notification clauses 6.1, 6.2.)

- **Notified and operating:** the scheme itself, all 5 approval tranches, ₹22,919 cr outlay.

- **Disbursed:** *no ECMS disbursement figure has been published in any source I could find.* Incentive claims only become due 9 months after the end of the relevant FY, and the first claim year is FY2025-26. **Treat ECMS as ₹0 disbursed as of Sep 2026.** Expected FY2026-27 outcome per PIB: ₹11,156 cr investment, ₹29,024 cr production, 19,240 jobs.
- Implementation status Aug 2026: 38 plants operational, 16 under construction, 52 fresh/greenfield.

### 7.3 **Location warning for a Bengaluru-based client**

This is a material siting decision, not a detail. (Confirm against Annexure 6 zone classification of the policy — I read this from the guidelines' operative clauses, not the zone annexure itself.)

## CANDIDATE MANUFACTURING OPPORTUNITIES

Ranked by **total India market size × growth to 2030 × force of the government scheme × how much of the product is genuinely bought and fitted inside India**. Existing Indian manufacturers are listed as market intelligence and are treated as proof the product is really bought here — they are **not** used to lower any rank. Capex is not a ranking factor.

### 1. Multi-layer, HDI and flexible printed circuit boards (bare boards)
**What it is:** the rigid or flexible fibreglass-and-copper board that every electronic product is built on, in 4-16+ layers.
**Who buys it in India:** contract manufacturers and OEMs — Dixon, Foxconn/Bharat FIH, Tata Electronics, Syrma SGS, Kaynes, VVDN, Amber/Ascent, plus defence PSUs (BEL, HAL), automotive tier-1s, meter and inverter makers. Bought as a bare board and populated in India.
**Market now:** imports **US$1,676.53 m (FY2025-26, HS 8534, DGCI&S)**, ~₹14,750 cr, +11.05% YoY. Domestic production additional.
**Market ~2030:** PCB market **₹1.8 lakh crore by 2032** (found, Communications Today); PCBA **₹12 lakh crore by 2030** at 30% CAGR (found). Straight-line interpolation to 2030 gives a bare-PCB market around **₹1.3-1.5 lakh crore** (derived).
**Import dependence:** approved ECMS plants were said to cover 20% of PCB demand after tranche 1 and **~50% after March 2026 approvals** — i.e. roughly half still imported, and that is against today's demand, not 2030's.
**Scheme support:** **Very strong but the front door is shut.** ECMS Segment B multi-layer PCB **10/8/7/6/5/5%** turnover-linked for ≥8 layers (6/6/5/5/4/4% for ≤6 layers), min ₹50 cr; Segment C HDI/MSAP/flexible **8/7/7/6/5/4% + 25% capex**, min ₹1,000 cr. **+1% extra for domestically sourced laminate.** Windows closed 30 Sep 2025. Entry now is via **Segment D (25% capex, open to 30 Apr 2027)** on PCB inputs, or by waiting for a reopening.
**Who already does this in India:** AT&S India, Shogini Technoarts, CIPSA-TEC India, Epitome Components, Sierra Circuits (India), Micropack, Hi-Q Electronics, Secure Circuits, India Circuits, Vital Electronics, Signum Electronics, BPL Ltd, Ascent-K Circuit, Zetfab India, Ehoome IOT, Meena Electrotech, Infopower Technologies; Syrma Strategic Electronics for flexible PCB. Seventeen-plus ECMS-approved names is the clearest possible signal that Indian buyers buy this in India.

### 2. Lithium-ion cells for digital applications (<10,000 mAh)
**What it is:** the rechargeable cell inside phones, laptops, earbuds, power tools and wearables — explicitly excluding grid storage and EV traction.
**Who buys it in India:** mobile and laptop assemblers and their pack makers, wearables and TWS brands, power-tool and appliance makers.
**Market now:** imports **US$4,696.64 m (FY2025-26, HS 8507.60)**, ~₹41,330 cr, **+56.22% in one year** — the fastest-growing large component line in the entire customs dataset. Plus US$560.98 m of accumulator parts.
**Market ~2030:** at even half the current growth rate the digital-cell slice alone runs to **₹1.0-1.3 lakh crore by 2030** (derived from the FY25-26 base and a 20-25% CAGR).
**Import dependence:** approved ECMS projects expected to cover **~61% of Li-ion battery demand**; the balance plus all growth is imported.
**Scheme support:** **Strong, window closed.** ECMS Segment B: **6/6/5/5/4/4%** turnover-linked, min ₹500 cr investment, **+2% additional for domestic Cathode Active Material**. Cell chemistry inputs — cathode material, anode material, electrolyte, separator — are **explicitly named in ECMS Annexure 1 Segment D supply chain, which is OPEN at 25% capex, min ₹10 cr**.
**Who already does this in India:** ATL Battery Technology (India), Munoth Lithium Battery; NPSPL Advanced Materials (anode material). Thin domestic field against a ₹41,000 cr import bill.

### 3. Connectors, relays and switches (ECMS "electro-mechanicals")
**What it is:** board-to-board and wire-to-board connectors, electromechanical and latching relays, tactile and rocker switches, terminals.
**Who buys it in India:** every EMS company and OEM; plus — and this is the distinguishing feature — **the power sector**. Smart meters, ring main units, MV switchgear and distribution panels are assembled in India by Indian firms from imported relays.
**Market now:** HS 8536 total imports **US$3,582.46 m (FY2025-26)**, ~₹31,500 cr, **+24.69%**. Within it: connectors ~US$1,556.58 m (+41.13%), plugs/sockets US$575.21 m, switches US$474.61 m, **relays US$475.15 m combined (+21.8%)**.
**Market ~2030:** applying the 2023→2030 component-demand CAGR of ~28% to the FY25-26 import base gives **₹1.0-1.3 lakh crore by 2030** for the 8536 family (derived).
**Import dependence:** high and **rising** — the +41% growth in HS 8536.90 means domestic supply is losing ground, not gaining.
**Scheme support:** **Strong on paper, two doors.** ECMS Segment B electro-mechanicals **8/7/7/6/5/4%** — **the joint-highest opening rate in the scheme** — at a low **₹50 cr** minimum investment; window closed. **Second door:** the **CEA 12 Dec 2025 consultation** names relays, relays ≤60V, relays for 230V meter load switching, C151 copper strip and relay alloys, and **relay production automation** among the 73 critical imported items, with a stated intent to take a localisation proposal to the Ministry of Power — announced intent, **no money notified yet**.
**Who already does this in India:** Molex (India), Amphenol (FCI / High Speed / Interconnect), TE Connectivity India, Rosenberger Interconnect India, Ennovi Mobility, SFO Technologies, Terminal Technologies, O/E/N India, Permanent Magnets Ltd, BG Electrical & Electronics India, Gruner India. Eleven ECMS approvals in this family — the densest cluster in the scheme — which says Indian buyers place real purchase orders for these here.

### 4. Capital equipment for electronics manufacturing (and its sub-assemblies and parts)
**What it is:** the machines that make components and assemble boards — SMT handling, test and inspection rigs, winding and coil machines, automation cells, precision assembly lines, plus their sub-assemblies and parts.
**Who buys it in India:** the 106 ECMS-approved component plants now being built, the PLI mobile and IT-hardware plants, semiconductor ATMP/OSAT fabs under ISM, and the EMS majors.
**Market now:** HS 8486 (semiconductor/FPD manufacturing machines) **US$1,383.71 m FY2025-26, +29.41%**; HS 8479 machines n.e.s. US$4,131.45 m; HS 8462 press/forming machine tools US$1,029.92 m; HS 8477.10 injection moulding machines US$286.29 m.
**Market ~2030:** ECMS alone has ₹69,548 cr of committed plant investment plus REPM ₹7,280 cr and ISM 2.0; a large share of that is equipment purchase in the next 3-4 years. **Derived equipment spend from ECMS-approved projects alone: order of ₹35,000-50,000 crore** (taking equipment at roughly half of non-land capex).
**Import dependence:** near-total for the higher-value lines.
**Scheme support:** **STRONGEST AVAILABLE — this is the only ECMS window still open.** Segment D item 11: **25% capex incentive, minimum investment only ₹10 crore, open until 30 April 2027.** Employment condition 20 persons per ₹1 cr. Reinforced by the Minister's 30 Mar 2026 statement favouring **buyer-seller arrangements and "possible preference for indigenous capital equipment manufacturers"**, and by ISM 2.0's stated focus on semiconductor equipment made in India.
**Who already does this in India:** Titan Engineering & Automation, NeST Advanced Technology Development Centre, ASM Technologies, Indo-MIM, Bharat FIH, IFB Industries, Jyoti CNC Automation, VVDN Technologies, Accurate Gauging and Instruments, Mitsubishi Electric India, Ind Sphinx Precision — all ECMS-approved for capital goods. Several are Bengaluru-based.

### 5. Precision tooling — moulds, dies, press tools, jigs and fixtures
**What it is:** the hardened steel tooling that stamps, moulds and forms every plastic housing, metal shield, connector shell and enclosure.
**Who buys it in India:** enclosure and mechanical makers (Yuzhan/Foxconn, Motherson, Tata Electronics, Aequs), connector and relay manufacturers, auto tier-1s, appliance makers. Tooling is specified, bought and consumed at the Indian plant.
**Market now:** imports **US$1,464.31 m FY2025-26** across HS 8480 + 8207.30 + 8207.20 ≈ **₹12,886 crore (derived)**, up **+21.4%** in one year. Domestic industry **₹23,800 crore** (TAGMA). Total market ≈ **₹36,700 crore** (derived); **imports ≈ 35% of consumption** (derived).
**Market ~2030:** at the observed ~20% import growth and the component sector's build-out, **₹75,000-90,000 crore total market by 2030** (derived).
**Import dependence:** ~35% overall, but concentrated at the high-precision end — the CEA list names **"high-precision injection molding tools"** as a critical imported item, and 8480.20 mould bases doubled (+114.78%).
**Scheme support:** **Good, and the door is open.** Tooling for electronics manufacturing qualifies under **ECMS Segment D item 11, 25% capex, min ₹10 cr, open to 30 Apr 2027**. Separately, **tools, dies, moulds, jigs and fixtures are explicitly eligible capital expenditure under both ECMS and the REPM scheme**, so every approved component plant is being subsidised to buy tooling. MHI Capital Goods Scheme Phase II (₹1,207 cr) supports CEFCs and import-substitution accelerators. **No dedicated moulds-and-dies scheme exists.** The ₹9,400 cr MHI parliamentary figure is **unverified** — but DGCI&S puts the same basket at ₹10,206 cr for FY2024-25 (derived), so the order of magnitude holds.
**Who already does this in India:** Indo-MIM, Ind Sphinx Precision, Micromax Precision Moulding, Brandworks Precision (all ECMS-approved), Craftsman Automation, Sansera Engineering, Godrej Tooling, Mutual Engineering, S&T Engineers, Devu Tools, plus the Indo-German Tool Rooms and Central Tool Room & Training Centres. TAGMA has 665 members with a Bangalore chapter.

### 6. Sintered rare-earth permanent magnets (NdFeB) and magnetic assemblies
**What it is:** high-strength neodymium-iron-boron magnets, sintered from rare-earth oxide, used in speakers, vibrator motors, sensors, actuators, EV and appliance motors, and MV switchgear actuators.
**Who buys it in India:** speaker and microphone makers, vibrator-motor and haptics suppliers, EV and appliance motor makers, switchgear manufacturers (the CEA list names NdFeB for MV actuators as a **prioritised** critical import), defence and aerospace.
**Market now:** imports HS 8505.11+8505.19 **US$332.28 m FY2025-26** (~₹2,924 cr), +14.24%; plus electromagnets/chucks US$110.15 m. **India imports 100% of its sintered NdFeB demand** (MHI gazette, clause 1.6).
**Market ~2030:** the REPM scheme itself targets **6,000 MTPA** of domestic capacity. India's current oxide base supports ~1,500 MTPA. At scheme scale and current magnet prices this is a **₹8,000-12,000 crore domestic market by 2030** (derived), with EV and wind driving the upside.
**Import dependence:** **100%.** The single cleanest import-substitution case in this entire report.
**Scheme support:** **Very strong and freshly notified — arguably the strongest per-rupee pull available.** **REPM Scheme, ₹7,280 crore, Gazette S.O. 5800(E), 15 December 2025.** 15% capital subsidy (capped ₹75-150 cr per beneficiary) **plus** a bid-determined sales-linked incentive capped at 40% of net sales turnover and ₹645-1,290 cr per beneficiary. Only **5 beneficiaries** will be selected via Global Tender Enquiry — a genuinely scarce, high-value slot. Assured NdPr oxide supply from IREL for the 3 lowest bidders. **Additionally**, rare-earth permanent magnets are already an approved **ECMS Segment D supply-chain** product at 25% capex.
**Who already does this in India:** Lohum Cleantech (India's 1st REPM plant from rare-earth oxide, ECMS-approved Mar 2026), Quantum Magnetics (ECMS-approved Aug 2026), Permanent Magnets Ltd (long-established, also ECMS-approved for relays). IREL (India) Ltd is the sole domestic oxide producer. The field is forming right now.

### 7. SMD and non-SMD passive components — capacitors, resistors, inductors, ferrites
**What it is:** the highest-volume parts on any board: MLCCs, electrolytic and film capacitors, chip and wirewound resistors, inductors, ferrite cores and beads.
**Who buys it in India:** every EMS company and OEM, by the billion. Also power-sector panel builders — the CEA list names **electrolytic capacitors** and **H-class ferrite cores** as critical imports.
**Market now:** capacitors **US$1,281.02 m**, resistors **US$495.01 m**, inductors (HS 8504.50) **US$584.02 m** — **US$2,360 m combined FY2025-26 ≈ ₹20,770 crore**, growing 5-16% by line. MLCCs alone US$425.42 m (+15.17%).
**Market ~2030:** at the sector's ~28% component CAGR, **₹70,000-90,000 crore by 2030** (derived); at a conservative 15%, ~₹45,000 crore (derived).
**Import dependence:** very high, especially SMD/chip formats. India's **first** SMD tantalum capacitor plant was only approved in March 2026.
**Scheme support:** **Strong, window closed.** ECMS Segment B non-SMD passives **8/7/7/6/5/4%** at a low ₹50 cr minimum; Segment C SMD passives **5/5/4/4/3/3% + 25% capex** at ₹250 cr. Passive-component raw materials — metallised film, specialty ceramics, laminates — sit in **Segment D, open at 25% capex**.
**Who already does this in India:** Vishay Components India (1st SMD tantalum plant), Vishay Precision Transducers India (resistors, transducers), Deki Electronics (capacitors), TDK India (capacitors and inductors), Globe Capacitors, Syrma SGS, Centum Electronics, Sensata Technologies India; supply chain — Dhruv Industries and Capfilm India (metallised film), Epsilon C2GR and PCBL Chemical (materials). A dense, real domestic buyer-supplier network.

### 8. Enclosures and mechanicals — metal and plastic housings, aluminium extrusion, heat sinks, shielding
**What it is:** the phone/laptop chassis, mid-frames, back covers, EMI shields, heat sinks and extruded aluminium sections.
**Who buys it in India:** mobile and IT-hardware assemblers directly — Foxconn/Bharat FIH, Tata Electronics, Dixon, Aequs; also data-centre and telecom equipment integrators.
**Market now:** HS 7616.99 other aluminium articles **US$822.82 m FY2025-26, +50.67%** (~₹7,240 cr); plus housings embedded in HS 8517.79 (US$16.59 bn of telephone parts) and HS 8473.30 (US$3.72 bn of ADP parts, +38.15%).
**Market ~2030:** enclosures typically run 8-12% of a phone's BOM; against ₹5.45 lakh cr of mobile production in FY2024-25 scaling toward the $500 bn 2030 target, the addressable Indian enclosure market is **₹60,000-90,000 crore by 2030** (derived).
**Import dependence:** **aluminium extrusion for mobile phone enclosures was "100% imported"** as of January 2026 (PIB). Finished enclosures are already made here in volume.
**Scheme support:** **Good, window closed for the finished product.** ECMS Segment B enclosures **7/6/5/4/4/3%**, min ₹500 cr, with a condition that the enclosure be made in India from metal, plastic or glass **and finished/decorated in India**. **Aluminium extrusion and other enclosure inputs sit in Segment D, open at 25% capex, min ₹10 cr.** Heat sinks are separately covered under electro-mechanicals at 8/7/7/6/5/4%.
**Who already does this in India:** Yuzhan Technology (India) (Foxconn), Motherson Electronic Components, Tata Electronics, Aequs Consumer Products (Belagavi, Karnataka — laptop and smartwatch enclosures), Brandworks Precision, Micromax Precision Moulding; Hindalco for aluminium extrusion; VVDN for heat sinks.

### 9. Display and camera module sub-assemblies
**What it is:** the bonded display stack (Film-on-Glass and Chip-on-Glass done in India) and the complete camera module (surface mount, front-of-line and end-of-line in India).
**Who buys it in India:** Apple's and Samsung's Indian supply chains, Dixon, Bharat FIH, Lava, plus automotive, drone, medical-device and security-camera makers.
**Market now:** the biggest single pool in the dataset — most of it sits inside HS 8517.79 parts of telephone apparatus, **US$16,593.86 m FY2025-26 (~₹1.46 lakh crore)**, plus HS 8529.90 **US$1,446.41 m (+19.11%)**. Display and camera together are conventionally 30-40% of a smartphone BOM.
**Market ~2030:** against a 2030 electronics production target of ₹42.5 lakh crore with mobiles the largest slice, the display+camera module market is plausibly **₹3-5 lakh crore by 2030** (derived) — the largest of any item here.
**Import dependence:** after tranche 1, domestic plants were expected to meet only **15% of camera module demand**. Display module localisation is earlier still.
**Scheme support:** **Present but the weakest rates in ECMS, and the window is closed.** Segment A: display module **4/4/3/2/2/1%**, camera module **5/4/4/3/2/2%**, both at ₹250 cr minimum investment and ₹250 cr revenue thresholds. Module inputs — lenses, glass cover, back light, polarizer film, contrast film, protective film — are **named in Segment D Annexure 1 and are OPEN at 25% capex**, which is the more attractive entry today.
**Who already does this in India:** Samsung Display Noida, Dixon Display Technologies, Wangda Technologies (display); Kunshan Q Tech Microelectronics (India), ASUX Safety Components India, Uno Minda, Syrma Mobility, TXB Optics, SkyQuad Electronics, Minda Instrument (camera and optics).

### 10. Copper clad laminate and PCB base materials (laminate, prepreg, copper foil)
**What it is:** the copper-faced fibreglass sheet that a PCB is etched from, plus prepreg bonding layers and electrodeposited copper foil.
**Who buys it in India:** the PCB makers listed at #1 — and there are now 17+ of them approved and building.
**Market now:** laminate is **~30% of a PCB's bill of materials** (PIB). Against a bare-PCB market whose import leg alone is ₹14,750 cr and with domestic PCB output rising fast, the Indian CCL market is on the order of **₹8,000-12,000 crore today** (derived).
**Market ~2030:** at 30% of a ₹1.3-1.5 lakh crore 2030 PCB market, **₹40,000-45,000 crore by 2030** (derived).
**Import dependence:** India had **no** CCL plant before ECMS tranche 1 in October 2025 — it was fully imported. The Minister stated domestic CCL demand "will now completely be domestically met" once approved plants run, which makes this the fastest-closing gap on the list.
**Scheme support:** **Good and OPEN.** Laminate, pre-preg and copper foil are **named explicitly in ECMS Annexure 1, Segment D supply chain — 25% capex, minimum ₹10 crore, open until 30 April 2027.** Plus the **+1% additional turnover incentive** paid to any multi-layer PCB maker who sources laminate domestically — a subsidy pointed directly at Indian CCL buyers.
**Who already does this in India:** Syrma Components, Ratnaveer Precision Engineering, Wipro Global Engineering and Electronic Materials (US$108 m project). Three approved projects against a market that was 100% imported eighteen months ago.

### Cross-cutting observations

1. **Segment D is the only open door, and it is wide.** ₹10 crore minimum investment, 25% capex, open until 30 April 2027. It covers *both* the inputs to every Segment A/B/C product *and* the machines and tooling used to make them. For anything in categories 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, 9 and 10 above, the practical route today is to enter as a **supply-chain or capital-equipment supplier**, not as the finished-component maker.
2. **Nothing has been disbursed yet.** ECMS claims are filed up to 9 months after the relevant financial year; the first claim year is FY2025-26. ₹69,548 cr of investment is *committed*, not spent, and ₹0 of the ₹22,919 cr (or ₹40,000 cr) has been paid out on any published record.
3. **The scheme is oversubscribed.** 249 applications against an expectation of 50-55; committed investment ₹1.15 lakh cr against a ₹59,350 cr target; projected incentive outgo ₹41,468 cr against a ₹22,805 cr original estimate — which is precisely why the Budget raised the outlay to ₹40,000 cr. Incentives are **first-come-first-served**, so approval order matters.
4. **Two distinct demand pulls exist and they do not overlap.** MeitY/ECMS pulls consumer-electronics and IT-hardware components. The CEA/Ministry of Power track pulls power-sector components — relays, switchgear parts, PCB assemblies, capacitors, magnets — and has 73 identified items but **no notified money yet**. A product that sits in both (relays, magnets, capacitors, PCBAs) has two independent buyers.
5. **Siting matters in Karnataka.** Bengaluru Urban appears to draw no state capex subsidy or state PLI under Industrial Policy 2025-30. Bengaluru Rural draws 10%/1.0%; Zone 1 and 2 districts draw 25%/2.5% and 20%/2.0%.
