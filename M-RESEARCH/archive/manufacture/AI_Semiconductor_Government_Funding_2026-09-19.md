# AI and semiconductor government funding: verified position on 19 September 2026

Research for a Karnataka-based company, assuming access to an experienced team, strong introductions and investment partners. This is a policy evidence ledger, not an application approval or a forecast of money receivable. No applications or external messages were submitted. Existing research and the HTML deck were not modified.

## The material change

Semicon India 2.0 has progressed beyond the February 2026 announcement: the India Semiconductor Mission (ISM) records Cabinet approval on **15 July 2026**, a **31 August 2026** notification, and manufacturing guidelines dated **16 September 2026**. Its approved fiscal outlay is **₹1,27,500 crore**. Current manufacturing support rates and thresholds differ substantially from the legacy 50% offer. Use the current category, eligible-cost definition and application documents when modelling a new project. [Official ISM](https://ism.gov.in/)

## Semicon 2.0: direct manufacturing beneficiaries

The following are **minimum capital investments**, not yearly incremental investments. Revenue criteria use the specified applicant/group/JV basis and one of the preceding three financial years. The official category pages state an initial three-year application window through the nodal portal. Manufacturing guidelines are listed as issued; a logged-in application submission was not tested.

| Category | Minimum capital investment | Minimum revenue | Stated fiscal support |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Semiconductor equipment R&D | ₹300 crore | ₹120 crore | 30% of eligible capex |
| Semiconductor-grade raw materials | ₹50 crore | ₹20 crore | 30% of eligible capex |
| Semiconductor test and characterisation facility | ₹100 crore | ₹40 crore | 30% of eligible capex |
| Equipment, refurbishment, subassemblies or components for fabs/packaging | ₹300 crore | ₹120 crore | 30% of eligible capex; conditional domestic-BoM production incentive also specified |

The equipment incentive uses rates of 10%, 8%, 6%, 4% and 2% on the stipulated domestic-manufacturer BoM base over five years starting FY2028–29, with a stated overall ceiling tied to 50% of eligible capex. Do not treat it as an unconditional extra grant on turnover. Technology ownership/licensing is required, with the page's exception for test/characterisation facilities. A ₹10–30 crore supplier should **not** claim direct eligibility for the ₹300 crore equipment category. Its opportunity may instead be supplying a qualifying beneficiary. [Official machines and materials terms](https://ism.gov.in/schemes/semicon2.0/machines-and-materials)

| Category | Minimum capital investment | Minimum revenue | Support / technical threshold |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Advanced ATMP/OSAT | ₹1,000 crore | ₹200 crore | 35% eligible capex; covers qualifying advanced packaging categories |
| Legacy ATMP/OSAT | ₹1,000 crore | ₹200 crore | 25% eligible capex |

Advanced packaging includes qualifying 2.5D/3D, wafer-level chip-scale, heterogeneous integration and advanced substrate approaches. A small burn-in/test-services business is not automatically an eligible ATMP facility. Both categories require production-grade technology ownership or a licence and the specified financial eligibility. [Official ATMP/OSAT terms](https://ism.gov.in/schemes/semicon2.0/atmp-osat)

| Category | Minimum capital investment | Minimum revenue | Support / technical threshold |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Silicon wafer fab | ₹20,000 crore | ₹7,500 crore | 40% eligible capex; 300mm and at least 40,000 wafer starts/month |
| Compound, photonics, MEMS or discrete fab | ₹500 crore | ₹200 crore | 35% eligible capex; at least 500 wafer starts/month; specified wafer-diameter conditions |

The category specifies 200mm for MEMS, 150mm for compound/discrete and 100mm for photonics. These are consortium/technology-partner projects, not a plausible extension of assembling an AI server or designing a chip. [Official fabs terms](https://ism.gov.in/schemes/semicon2.0/fabs)

**Legacy comparison:** the original modified silicon-fab offer was 50% of project cost; its current page explicitly says **Application Closed**. Legacy rates must not be copied into a new Semicon2.0 business case. [Legacy fab page](https://ism.gov.in/schemes/semicon1.0/semiconductor-fab)

## Semiconductor design: the relevant direct startup route

Semicon2.0 commercial design provides qualifying startups/MSMEs milestone-linked **advance seed funding up to 50% of project cost, capped at ₹15 crore per application**. It also sets out equity co-investment beyond that threshold for companies with qualifying VC/PE investment; this is not an automatic matching grant. Deployment support is **9% of net sales for five years**, capped at **₹30 crore per application** and **₹120 crore per group company**. Product, ownership and timing conditions apply.

Commercial applicants must be incorporated/headquartered in India, with substantial operations/manpower and IP/design files in India; specified Indian-citizen/OCI ownership or control is required. Strategic design procurement is a separate route with C-DAC RFPs and IP terms. The current policy page establishes these mechanisms, but every detailed call and online workflow was not independently tested. A focused industrial/edge-AI chip or reusable semiconductor IP business is a credible growth candidate with an experienced CTO, tape-out plan and anchor customer. It does not establish domestic wafer-fabrication independence. [Official Semicon2.0 design terms](https://ism.gov.in/schemes/semicon2.0/)

**Legacy DLI:** product-design reimbursement was up to 50%, capped at ₹15 crore; deployment incentive was 6%, 6%, 5%, 5%, 4% over five years, capped at ₹30 crore. The current legacy portal explicitly invites **design infrastructure support** applications, while describing an initial three-year scheme application period from 1 January2022. This is not evidence that the original cash-incentive window remains open in September2026. EDA/IP/MPW support and the new design route should be distinguished. [DLI portal](https://chips-dli.gov.in/DLI/HomePage), [official legacy thresholds](https://chips-dli.gov.in/DLI/AbstractFilePathCoded?FileName=VGhyZXNob2xkX2FuZF9DZWlsaW5nX0xpbWl0cy5wZGY%3D&FileType=RQ%3D%3D&PathKey=RE9DVU1FTlRfVEVNUExBVEU%3D)

## ECMS: a smaller manufacturing entry route, with a narrow open window

The live portal states that applications for segments **A, B, C and E closed on 30 September2025**, while **segment D remains open until 30 April2027**. Segment D covers the supply chain of specified electronic subassemblies/bare components and capital goods used in electronics manufacturing, including qualifying subassemblies/components. It has a **₹10 crore minimum investment** and **25% capex incentive**, subject to product and expenditure eligibility. An attractive category is not proof that a particular machine, precision part, connector or material falls within it. [Live ECMS portal](https://ecms.meity.gov.in/), [official April2025 launch and incentive table](https://negd.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Press-Release_Press-Information-Bureau-1.pdf)

For HDI/multilayer PCBs or other closed-window categories, do not tell a new entrant that an application is presently available; consider a qualified existing beneficiary/JV or independently viable investment. The portal's lithium-ion cell category concerns digital applications and excludes mobility/storage. Obtain written product classification before spending against expected support.

The **17 September2026** official factsheet reports an expanded **₹40,000 crore** outlay, **106 approved projects across 15 states**, **₹69,548 crore approved investment**, and **38 plants in production** as of August2026. The reported projected output is not actual startup revenue or available government cash. A separate revised legal notification for the expanded outlay was not retrieved. [Current ECMS factsheet](https://www.pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?ModuleId=16&NoteId=151016&id=151016&lang=1&reg=3)

**ISM2/ECMS interaction:** this review did not establish a blanket entitlement to claim both on the same equipment or costs. Model a single route per eligible cost until the relevant authorities confirm treatment in writing. That is a conservative modelling rule, not a claim that all programme combinations are legally prohibited.

## IndiaAI: usable compute and demand, not a domestic GPU-fab subsidy

The mission's approved five-year outlay is **₹10,371.92 crore**. The **6 August2026** parliamentary reply records **15 empanelled compute providers**, **237 projects approved for subsidised compute**, and **93.18 lakh GPU-hours sanctioned**. Sanctioned GPU-hours are not necessarily consumed hours. A purchase order for about **1.1 exaflops** at NIC Shastri Park is not evidence that this additional capacity was commissioned. [Parliamentary reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295477&lang=1&reg=3)

The **13 August2026** official factsheet reports **45,000+ GPUs of shared capacity as of June2026**. Use that dated reported capacity instead of adding a February announcement of another20,000 to the earlier38,000 and declaring58,000 installed. The evidence does not establish a real-time September count of idle or instantly allocable GPUs. Access is through providers/programme conditions; model, memory, interconnect, location and price differ. [Official capacity factsheet](https://www.pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?Id=150860), [February expansion announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2229171&lang=1&reg=1)

Practical implications: eligible AI businesses can reduce compute-access barriers; Indian suppliers of liquid cooling, rack power, interconnects and qualified servers can pursue the underlying infrastructure market. Neither point means IndiaAI automatically reimburses their factories. Imported GPUs remain imported even when integrated into a domestically manufactured server or operated in India.

## Karnataka: actual support exists; calculate the package project by project

The Karnataka industrial operative guidelines require selection of the applicable state policy rather than mixing policy benefits indiscriminately (printed p12). The MSME capital-subsidy provision describes differential treatment where capital/fixed-asset/project-cost grants were already received under other state or central schemes (p97); do not universalise that provision to every project category. Peenya is within Bengaluru Urban; Dabaspet/Nelamangala lies in Bengaluru Rural **Zone3** (p19); Tumakuru taluk **Zone2** (p20). These location classifications alone do not establish a payable incentive. [Official operative guidelines](https://investkarnataka.co.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Karnataka.pdf)

A **2 June2026** Aequs stock-exchange disclosure confirms a sanction dated1June2026 under the **Karnataka Special Incentives Scheme for ESDM2020–2030** for its Dharwad facility. This establishes actual implementation of that policy, not a blanket percentage for a new applicant. [Primary company regulatory disclosure](https://www.aequs.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Intimation-on-ACPPL-Incentive-Order.pdf)

For an early factory, Bengaluru engineering/customer access plus a manufacturing location selected for process utilities, workforce and logistics is more defensible than choosing land solely for an assumed subsidy. A ministerial introduction can help reach technology partners, scheme administrators and buyers; MeitY/ISM, IndiaAI and Karnataka retain their respective approval roles. Customer qualification and procurement remain separate from introductions.

## How this changes opportunity ranking

These are commercial judgments inferred from the eligibility and demand evidence, not official rankings:

1. **Fabless industrial/edge-AI silicon or reusable IP** has a relevant startup funding route and potentially high IP-led growth, provided an experienced founding technical team and paying use case exist. Funding does not resolve tape-out, software ecosystem or sales risk.
2. **AI cooling, rack power, interconnects, semiconductor equipment assemblies and test fixtures** can begin as customer-led manufacturing businesses. Treat government programmes primarily as demand catalysts; prove direct scheme eligibility product by product.
3. **A chip-test/characterisation centre** may qualify directly only at the specified ₹100 crore capital scale and financial criteria. A smaller test-interface/fixture company is a different, potentially attractive supplier business.
4. **Advanced packaging/OSAT** is a large technology-JV opportunity: ₹1,000 crore minimum investment for the current category. Do not label an inexpensive assembly line as equivalent to advanced GPU packaging.
5. **Materials or equipment platforms** can seek ISM2 support at the relevant ₹50 crore/₹300 crore thresholds; below them, supplying beneficiaries or a qualifying ECMS route can be more realistic.
6. **HDI PCB manufacturing** can benefit from the broader ecosystem, but the current ECMS application closure is material for a new company. An existing beneficiary partnership is distinct from a fresh subsidy application.
