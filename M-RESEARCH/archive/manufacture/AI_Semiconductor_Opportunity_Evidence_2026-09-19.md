# AI and semiconductor growth opportunities — independent evidence

Research date: 19 September 2026. Scope: a Karnataka-based company pursuing growing AI/semiconductor demand, with strong technical partners and access to capital. No fixed INR 30 crore ceiling is assumed in this screen. This is an opportunity comparison, not a costed investment proposal. No outreach was performed; named organisations are ecosystem participants or prospective counterparties, not confirmed customers. Earlier reports and attachments were not modified.

## Main finding

The strongest accessible exposure to semiconductor growth is through qualified equipment assemblies and increasingly demanding test interfaces. A well-backed fabless product company is a credible, more ambitious third route, but belongs to a different business model from a manufacturing workshop. UHP systems and packaging inputs provide tangible Indian localisation opportunities when an established technology owner and an anchor customer participate. A new leading-edge GPU/HBM fab is not an extension of these businesses.

The current demand boom is concentrated. It should not be applied equally to mature discrete packages, leadframes, commodity trays, every Indian fab, or every local supplier.

## Fresh growth evidence and its limits

- **WSTS, 2 June 2026:** its original Spring 2026 forecast PDF projects semiconductor revenue of USD 1.511 trillion in 2026 and USD 1.914 trillion in 2027. Its 2026 segment forecasts differ sharply: memory +249.5%, logic +37.3%, analog +10.2%, discrete +8.0%. These are revenue forecasts, not physical-unit or supplier-order growth rates; the release does not decompose memory pricing versus shipment volume. Do not project the headline 89.9% revenue growth onto a small Indian supplier. Original dated PDF: https://www.wsts.org/esraCMS/extension/media/f/WST/7618/WSTS_FC-Release-2026-May.pdf
- **SEMI, 14 July 2026:** forecast 2026 equipment sales of USD 165.9 billion (+23.2%), with test equipment USD 15.3 billion (+31.0%) and assembly/packaging equipment USD 6.7 billion (+9.6%). Its 2028 total reaches USD 229.5 billion. The release links the expansion to AI logic, advanced memory and increasingly complex test/packaging. China, Taiwan and Korea remain its largest equipment-spending destinations. This supports export-oriented Indian supplier development, but is not an estimate of accessible Indian demand. https://www.semi.org/en/semi-press-release/global-semiconductor-equipment-sales-forecast-to-reach-a-record-229-billion-dollars-in-2028-semi-reports

## Ranked growth candidates

Ranking weighs growth exposure, a Karnataka entry route, commercial accessibility and localisation depth. It is judgment, not a numerical market model. Capital classes are qualitative: **focused supplier** means a bounded product/process operation; **patient deep-tech** means sustained engineering, qualification and several funding stages; **industrial JV** means an experienced process/technology partner, substantial facilities and committed demand. They are not project quotations.

### 1. Precision assemblies for semiconductor equipment

**Entrance unit:** one approved family of precision machined/cleaned mechanical assemblies, progressing toward more demanding vacuum/thermal/fluid subsystems only after qualification. Sell to equipment OEM sourcing organisations or established Tier-1 suppliers, not directly to chip-design offices.

**Why it grows:** global fab-equipment expansion is a direct driver; the market is geographically broader than Indian fab construction. Bengaluru has relevant engineering, manufacturing suppliers and qualification capability. Applied Materials' 5 February 2026 announcement explicitly identifies component qualification, cleanroom inspection, prototyping and wafer-processing validation. These are not a commercial high-volume wafer fab.

**Localisation:** supplying India-based tool users can replace imported parts. Parts exported for overseas tool assembly build Indian manufacturing exports; do not count that automatically as current Indian import displacement.

**Capital/gate:** focused supplier initially, industrial scale for critical subsystems. Need an OEM-approved drawing and process specification, repeatable metrology, material traceability, approved surface treatment/cleaning and a paid qualification programme. Existing competitors include TEAM D and Millworks; proximity does not guarantee purchase orders.

Sources: https://www.appliedmaterials.com/us/en/newsroom/blogs/a-new-chapter-for-applied-materials-india.html ; https://www.teamd.in/ ; https://millworksindia.com/semiconductor/ ; https://www.lamresearch.com/company/environmental-social-and-governance/responsible-supply-chain/

### 2. Semiconductor test interfaces and thermal test hardware

**Entrance unit:** application-specific load/burn-in board assemblies, handler interface kits, mechanical contactors and controlled-temperature fixtures for one device/package family. Include electrical/thermal engineering; avoid starting with a complete general-purpose tester.

**Why it grows:** more complex chips create more demanding test and thermal requirements; SEMI's test-equipment outlook is especially strong. Karnataka has real packaging at Tata Vemgal and an established test ecosystem. STPI's Bengaluru SMART Lab lists 64 users as of 31 August 2026, and Tessolve provides custom silicon/productisation/test services.

**Localisation:** boards, mechanical structures and engineering can become local, while precision contacts, sockets, high-end PCB fabrication and instruments may remain foreign. The OSAT/fabless test-engineering team controls specifications; the ultimate package owner may approve the supplier.

**Capital/gate:** focused supplier to patient deep-tech. Start with a paid co-development project and prove signal integrity, temperature uniformity, contact life and device protection. An AI chip-test claim requires an actual matching device programme; a generic burn-in oven is insufficient. Tessolve/ChipTest are meaningful incumbent competition.

Sources: https://stpi.in/en/smart-lab-services ; https://www.tessolve.com/system-level-test/ ; https://c2s.gov.in/Post_Silicon.jsp ; Tata Vemgal source above.

### 3. Fabless application-specific chips and reference modules

**Entrance unit:** one customer-backed edge-AI/vision SoC, mixed-signal/PMIC product, or specialised embedded processor, plus its software and reference module. These are alternatives, not one initial product programme. A RISC-V instruction set is an architectural choice, not a complete product moat.

**Why it grows:** local system makers need product differentiation and dependable supply. Bengaluru has relevant design talent. BigEndian's own page describes a surveillance SoC under development in Whitefield. Netrasemi's May–June 2026 updates describe A2000 silicon bring-up and customer trials, with commercial production targeted for 2027. This demonstrates both feasibility and the difference between working samples and mature revenue.

**Buyer:** camera, industrial-vision, robotics, communications, power-system or vehicle-electronics OEM with specific performance/price/software requirements. Government GPU procurement does not automatically buy a new local edge chip.

**Localisation:** Indian IP and product control are substantial, but foreign fabrication, EDA/IP and often packaging remain. MosChip explicitly describes a completed edge-AI ASIC engagement using TSMC 22 nm, illustrating this division. Tata/L&T's 18 September partnership explores Indian fabrication/packaging for suitable designs; it does not promise that every advanced-node design can be made at Dholera.

**Capital/gate:** patient deep-tech. Requires a proven silicon team, licensed IP and foundry access, verification, tape-out/re-spin runway, developer tools and customer design-ins. For a founder whose priority is operating a factory immediately, this is a separate venture/JV or acquisition route.

Sources: https://bigendiansemi.com/solutions/ ; https://www.netrasemi.com/news ; https://www.netrasemi.com/technology ; https://www.mindgrovetech.in/ ; https://moschip.com/silicon-engineering-services/ ; https://www.tataelectronics.com/w/l-t-semiconductor-and-tata-electronics-partner-to-expand-indigenous-manufacturing-of-market-leading-chip-products-for-global-customers

### 4. UHP gas-delivery assemblies and tool hook-up

**Entrance unit:** qualified inert-gas tube spools, manifolds/panels and installation packages under an experienced EPC/process-systems partner. This is semiconductor infrastructure, not chip fabrication.

**Why it grows:** India's fab build-out creates domestic installation and maintenance demand. Tata's 18 September Kelington announcement establishes a concrete named tool-hook-up/infrastructure relationship and potential local capability development.

**Buyer/localisation:** fab facilities engineering and the approved EPC; Karnataka can host engineering/fabrication, while the anchor fab is in Gujarat. Local assemblies and installation replace imported systems/services partly; valves, mass-flow controllers and high-spec components may remain imported. Joscotec's own component listings illustrate this.

**Capital/gate:** focused supplier within a technical partnership, expanding toward industrial JV. Material purity, traceable orbital welds, cleaning, contamination control, helium leak testing and customer approval are essential. Cooling-metalwork capability does not automatically satisfy UHP requirements. Construction demand is project-driven; establish service/consumable revenue separately.

Sources: https://www.tataelectronics.com/w/tata-electronics-and-kelington-collaborate-to-accelerate-operational-readiness-of-dholera-fab ; https://www.joscotec.com/gasvacuum-system/ ; https://www.swagelok.com/en/industries/semiconductor/cleanliness-chain

### 5. Specialised power-semiconductor packaging and modules

**Entrance unit:** licensed/partnered SiC power-module packaging for an identified inverter, storage or industrial converter programme. Choose die attach, interconnect and thermal architecture around that customer; a full SiC wafer fab is a different project.

**Why it grows:** power conversion benefits from electrification and AI infrastructure, but not every module is appropriate for a server supply. L&T Semiconductor/Newen's April 2026 agreement covers design, qualification and supply of SiC modules for renewable-energy converters, providing a concrete Indian commercial pattern.

**Localisation:** assembly process, substrate/baseplate, interconnect and testing can shift locally, while qualified bare dies and some ceramic substrates/materials may stay imported. Qualification must establish power-cycling, thermal and insulation reliability.

**Capital/gate:** patient deep-tech/industrial JV. Start only with process expertise, die supply and an anchor converter maker. Existing CDIL, Kaynes and major global device makers mean this is not an empty market. Direct AI attribution requires a specific data-centre power design, not the word SiC.

Source: https://www.ltsct.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Press-Release_LTSCT-X-Newen-Systems-Private-Limited_21st-April.pdf ; approved Indian projects above.

### 6. Semiconductor leadframes and related precision package components

**Entrance unit:** an OSAT-approved leadframe family, stamping/etching tools or tightly specified package-metal component, developed with an established material/process supplier. Ordinary component moulds are not equivalent to finished semiconductor leadframes.

**Why it grows:** India's growing packaging output needs recurrent material supply. Tata/Enomoto's 18 September MoU explicitly explores local leadframe manufacture, technology transfer and engineering collaboration for Jagiroad. This is unusually concrete localisation evidence.

**Buyer/localisation:** OSAT package engineering and the chip owner; replace imported qualified package inputs when approved. Alloy strip, plating chemistry and specialist equipment can remain foreign. Conventional leadframes mainly address discrete/analog/mainstream packages; they are not the principal substrate for leading-edge GPU/HBM systems.

**Capital/gate:** focused precision-tooling entry; industrial JV for full semiconductor-grade stamping/etching/plating. Existing Enomoto relationship is evidence of both a partner route and a competitive boundary, not an invitation already granted to this user.

Source: https://www.tataelectronics.com/w/tata-electronics%E2%80%AFand%E2%80%AFenomoto-sign-memorandum-of-understanding-to-strengthen-semiconductor-packaging-ecosystem-in-india

### 7. Electronic-grade wet-chemical purification/blending

**Entrance unit:** a selected high-purity chemical under a licensed process and fab qualification, rather than a broad catalogue of industrial chemicals relabelled semiconductor-grade.

**Why it grows:** consumption recurs as wafer starts rise. Tata/Sumitomo's 18 September announcement identifies photoresists, functional chemicals and high-purity hydrogen peroxide, IPA, ammonia water and sulfuric acid; local manufacturing is being explored, not declared operational.

**Buyer/localisation:** fab process/material engineering and approved supply-chain partners. Domestic purification, analysis, filling and eventually production can replace imports; importing and warehousing finished chemicals is distribution, not substantive manufacture. Tata's separate Sojitz/NRS agreement concerns supply/storage/logistics, illustrating that distinction.

**Capital/gate:** industrial JV. Requires contamination analysis, stable feedstock, validated packaging, consistent batches, waste treatment and customer process qualification. Established Japanese suppliers are formidable incumbents; locating near actual fabs may outweigh a Karnataka preference. Do not infer an available Tata contract.

Sources: https://www.tataelectronics.com/w/tata-electronics-and-sumitomo-chemical-sign-mou-for-high-quality-semiconductor-materials ; https://www.tataelectronics.com/w/tata-electronics-sojitz-corporation-and-nrs-corporation-announce-partnership-to-develop-semiconductor-materials-supply-chain-for-dholera-fab

### 8. Advanced-package co-design, pilot integration and qualification

**Entrance unit:** customer-specific package design, thermal/power-integrity modelling, test development and outsourced pilot assembly; expand to a selected manufacturing process with a technology partner and demonstrated demand. Own physical processing must be specified before describing the business as manufacturing.

**Why it grows:** chiplets and heterogeneous integration create technical complexity and qualification needs. Tata's portfolio and the approved 3D Glass Solutions project show Indian intent beyond wire bonding. They do not establish freely available CoWoS-equivalent capacity, HBM assembly rights or customer die allocation.

**Buyer/localisation:** fabless product teams and established OSATs. Early value is Indian engineering/IP; imported dies and outsourced assembly remain. Manufacturing localisation follows only after a qualified line is established.

**Capital/gate:** patient deep-tech for services/pilot engineering; industrial JV for commercial integration. Known-good-die procurement, interposer/substrate access, bonding yield, inspection and failure analysis must work together. Start-up yield losses can destroy the economics of expensive multi-die products.

Sources: https://www.tessolve.com/ ; https://www.tessolve.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Owen-Bateman.pdf ; Tata package and 3DGS approval sources above.

### 9. Advanced substrates/interposers — strategic JV reserve

Glass-core substrates, fine-line organic substrates or interposers offer strong advanced-package exposure, but are not a routine extension of PCB fabrication. India's approved 3DGS project establishes a credible strategic direction, not proven accessible orders for a new entrant. Consider only with proprietary process access, a substrate/package technology partner and customer commitments. Capital class: large industrial JV. Qualification, panel/wafer yield, metallisation and thermal-mechanical reliability are decisive. Do not describe a normal PCB shop or leadframe line as an AI GPU substrate plant.

Source: government 3DGS project description in the February 2026 annexure above.

## What to exclude from a near-term first-company recommendation

- A new leading-edge GPU wafer fab or HBM memory fab without a proven technology owner, very large consortium capital and committed customers.
- Generic legacy OSAT solely because AI chip revenue is surging. Package type, customer relationship, die availability and utilisation matter more than the broad label.
- Commodity carrier tape/trays presented as a uniquely high-growth AI semiconductor business without specific imported SKUs and differentiated specifications.
- Broad photoresist invention or an unlicensed electronic-grade chemical plant without process IP and qualification access.
- Ordinary cold plates presented as semiconductor manufacturing. They can be a strong AI infrastructure business, but their connection is cooling computers; UHP fabs require different processes and approval.

## How the user's network can change the ranking

Use introductions to obtain a paid sample-development programme, access to a technology owner and the buyer's supplier-qualification team. For growth-focused Karnataka entry, the first discussions should be with equipment OEM sourcing/qualification teams, Tata Vemgal packaging engineering, established test engineering firms and an identified fabless/product customer. An introduction to a minister does not establish an RFQ, technical licence, subsidy entitlement or exemption from customer qualification.

A signed technology/JV route and anchor programme could move power-module packaging, high-purity chemicals or substrates up substantially. Without those, equipment assemblies and test interfaces remain better-supported entry points. For direct AI infrastructure rather than semiconductor production, server cooling/power businesses should be compared separately with these candidates.

## Additional comparison: advanced PCB manufacture versus materials

**For the final slot in an AI-growth top ten, advanced HDI/high-layer-count low-loss PCB manufacture is stronger than generic leadframes or electronic-grade chemicals for a Karnataka-based venture, conditional on an experienced technology partner and an anchor server/networking OEM or ODM.** This is a large industrial JV candidate, not the same process as semiconductor ABF/glass substrates and not ordinary PCB assembly.

AT&S's own Nanjangud page establishes high-frequency and HDI PCB manufacturing in Karnataka, including automotive, industrial and infrastructure applications. It establishes relevant process capability and labour experience in the state, not proof of current AI-server motherboard manufacture at that plant. Source: https://ats.net/en/campaign/nanjangud/

The initial ECMS approval table illustrates the industrial scale and growing domestic supply: Kaynes HDI in Tamil Nadu, Syrma multilayer PCBs in Andhra Pradesh and Ascent multilayer PCBs in Tamil Nadu. These are competitors and possible technology/customer ecosystem participants, not reasons to exclude the opportunity. Source: https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/oct/doc20251027678301.pdf

The new venture needs a specified layer stack, high-speed loss/impedance targets, thermal reliability, via/plating yield, materials supply and accepted qualification samples. Domestic fabrication can replace imported bare boards while low-loss laminates, specialised process chemicals and manufacturing equipment may remain foreign. Current customer imports and the intended board family must be established before sizing the opportunity. Participation in an existing approved JV or acquisition may differ from obtaining a new scheme approval; incentives must not be assumed.

Leadframes retain strong direct Indian packaging-localisation evidence through Tata/Enomoto but weaker direct exposure to GPU/HBM packaging. Chemicals offer recurring fab-consumption revenue, with a geographically more distant customer base and demanding purity/process qualification. Advanced organic/glass substrates have greater long-term compute relevance but require a more specialised and capital-intensive process partnership than PCB manufacture.

**Competition is evidence of an established market and execution standard, not an automatic exclusion.** The user's network can materially improve customer access, technical partnerships and anchor purchase commitments. Under the stated best-case partnership/funding scenario, keep advanced packaging, PCB fabrication and power-semiconductor modules in consideration. Change the order when a credible partner and paid customer programme are secured; do not substitute political access for qualification evidence.

For the broader final list, when **funding, credible technology transfer and anchor customer programmes are assumed to be achievable**, my growth-oriented comparison order is: (1) semiconductor equipment assemblies; (2) test/burn-in interfaces; (3) advanced HDI/high-layer-count PCBs; (4) advanced-package pilot-to-production JV; (5) application-specific fabless chips; (6) UHP delivery assemblies; (7) specialised power-module packaging; (8) electronic-grade chemicals; (9) semiconductor leadframes. This order is not an assertion that all partner/customer arrangements already exist. Advanced IC substrates remain an additional strategic reserve. Cooling, high-speed interconnect, AI-server integration and power systems require comparison in the broader AI-infrastructure study.

## Research limitations

No product-level customs dataset, buyer annual purchase ledger, current RFQ, plant audit or binding technology licence was obtained. Import displacement is therefore identified by process/product and residual foreign content, not invented niche percentages. Forecasts and announcements are dated; proposed capacity and MoUs are not booked revenue or operating production. Capital classifications describe business complexity only. Original policy-scheme eligibility requires separate project-specific verification.
