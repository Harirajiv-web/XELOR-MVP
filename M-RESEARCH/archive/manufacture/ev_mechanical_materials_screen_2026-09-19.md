# Independent EV mechanical and materials opportunity screen

Prepared 19 September 2026. Scope: Bengaluru-based entry, an experienced technical partner and access to industry assumed, with an initial ₹1–30 crore envelope. Semiconductor opportunities are deliberately outside this screen. This is research and a commercial screen, not a feasibility study or a supplier quotation. No company or public official was contacted.

## Decision

The strongest four candidates to carry into an EV-only comparison are **specialised battery production/test stations; engineered battery/inverter interconnects; selective battery-material recovery; and a technically validated rare-earth-free motor programme**. The last two have a stronger strategic import-reduction argument but more severe entry conditions. They should not inherit a high execution score merely because a technical partner is assumed.

For an immediately executable manufacturing investigation, I would start with **one difficult battery-process/test station or one engineered interconnect family**, selected from an actual OEM localisation request. For the most consequential import substitution, I would investigate **recovering saleable lithium-bearing material** and **eliminating imported rare-earth magnets from a defined motor application**. Neither presently has enough verified commercial information for an unconditional factory recommendation.

India already manufactures substantial EV mechanical content. JMK Research/IEEFA's June 2026 component-localisation study estimates chassis/structural localisation at 95–100%, identifies mature mechanical capability within thermal systems, and identifies persistent gaps in magnets and advanced motor materials. These are researcher estimates, not a product-level customs census. They argue against using total EV sales to justify a generic metalworking factory. [JMK/IEEFA original research](https://jmkresearch.com/electric-vehicles-published-reports/beyond-battery-packs-localisation-in-manufacturing-ev-components/)

**Import evidence standard:** a foreign supplier's product catalogue proves a competing product exists; it does not prove Indian import volume or manufacturing origin. A domestic supplier's product page proves claimed capability, not the absence of imports. Before investment, the proposed customer must identify the imported part or machine, specification, annual purchases, price, origin and qualification route. This screen found stronger official evidence of dependence in critical materials than in the particular busbar, gear or cold-plate niches.

## What would actually be manufactured and sold

### 1. Specialised pack production and test machinery

Start with one station family: for example, joining prismatic-cell terminals to interconnects, verifying joint resistance and weld integrity, then recording traceable results. Alternatively, specialise in coolant-circuit leak testing and electrical end-of-line acceptance. The business supplies the machine, fixtures, controls, process recipe and service, rather than simply reselling a complete imported line.

Competition is real. Jendamark documents an Indian Livguard battery-module welding installation and its EV offering includes assembly and test operations. This validates demand and local capability; it also eliminates the claim that all such machines are imported. A new entrant needs a particular process improvement, model-change flexibility, service advantage or cost reduction. [Jendamark's Livguard case study](https://jendamark.io/case-study/ensuring-safe-and-accurate-ev-battery-module-assembly/), [EV assembly offering](https://jendamark.io/component-assembly-solutions/electric-vehicles/)

**Target buyer roles:** battery-pack process engineering and supplier-development teams, module manufacturers and established line integrators needing specialist stations. Livguard is an evidenced customer of the category, not an available order for this venture. Vehicle makers and pack integrators are prospects only.

**Entry gates:** customer sample parts; agreed cycle time and quality metrics; measurement-system repeatability; weld/process destructive correlation where relevant; factory and site acceptance; uptime/service commitments; safe handling of live cells. Bought-in lasers, drives and instruments must be deducted when calculating domestic value addition. Do not count a complete imported station inside a local frame as meaningful indigenisation.

### 2. Engineered electrical interconnects

Choose between two distinct product families initially: a laminated low-inductance DC link for an inverter, or a flexible battery interconnect qualified for its cell terminal, vibration and temperature cycle. They have different process and validation details and should not be treated as one generic busbar SKU. The value lies in engineering, controlled joining, insulation and repeatable performance.

Tata AutoComp has a dedicated Indian busbar operation covering rigid, flexible and hybrid designs, with forming/joining, plating and inspection capabilities; its site names customers including JLR, PACCAR and Tata Gotion. Exxelia SVM also offers laminated busbars. Mersen supplies EV inverter/distribution busbars internationally. These are genuine competitors; none establishes an uncovered Indian import share. [Tata AutoComp busbar division](https://www.tataautocomp.com/tevcs/busbar-division/), [its process capabilities](https://www.tataautocomp.com/tevcs/busbar-division/technology/), [Exxelia SVM product](https://exxelia.com/en/products/svm-bus-bars/laminated-bus-bars/partially-laminated-bus-bar), [Mersen EV portfolio](https://www.mersen.com/en/all-markets/ev-driving-ahead-performance-and-sustainability)

**Target buyer roles:** inverter designers, pack/module manufacturers and e-powertrain Tier 1 sourcing teams. **Entry gates:** a nominated drawing/design brief, current-temperature rise, joint resistance, isolation/partial-discharge requirements where applicable, insulation ageing, mechanical endurance and production process approval. Special films, sensing electronics and plating chemistry may remain imported even when the assembly is Indian.

### 3. Battery-material recovery

This is a chemical-process manufacturing business with working-capital and feedstock risk. Its import-substitution value depends on the final recovered material being usable by a domestic buyer. Shredding batteries and exporting black mass does not achieve the same result as producing buyer-accepted lithium or metal compounds.

**Concrete accessible technology evidence:** C-MET's April 2026 rolling, non-exclusive pyro-route EOI quotes ₹2.20 crore indicative plant CAPEX for 500 kg batteries/day including lithium extraction, ₹250–300/kg operating cost excluding batteries, and a ₹4.64 lakh plus tax technology fee. It lists approximately 20,000 sq ft and 480 kVA. Products are lithium carbonate and cobalt–nickel alloy, not automatically battery-grade cathode salts. Demonstration includes only 250 g black-mass smelting batches; commercial scale-up needs proof. The document also says hydrometallurgical transfers continue. Figures are guidance at April 2026 prices, not a turnkey quotation, and admission is subject to selection. [C-MET EOI, especially printed pages 4–7](https://cmet.gov.in/sites/default/files/eoi/EOIforLIB-recyclingTechnology-PyroRoute_28042026.pdf)

**Operating-cost arithmetic:** at the indicated throughput and an assumed 300 operating days, 500 × 300 = 150,000 kg/year. At ₹250–300/kg, the implied processing expense is **₹3.75–4.50 crore/year before buying batteries**. Low machinery cost does not imply low cash requirements. This is an arithmetic illustration, not a profitability forecast.

There is also a government-reported 2024 precedent: Remine's C-MET-based project had a stated total cost of ₹15 crore, with ₹7.5 crore TDB assistance. That is evidence that a project in this envelope has been financed, not proof of its present throughput or commercial success. [PIB, 2 April 2024](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2016912&lang=2&reg=48)

Competition includes BatX and Lohum. TDB's June 2025 BatX announcement describes commercialising domestic hydrometallurgical recovery, including lithium carbonate and cobalt sulphate. CSIR-NML reports a pilot-scale recycling process and transfer activity. Do not assume a private competitor will license its technology. [TDB on BatX](https://www.tdb.gov.in/tdb-supports-batx-energies-commercialisation-indigenous-battery-recycling-technology), [CSIR-NML pilot achievement](https://www.csir.res.in/en/latest-achievements/latest-achievements-1), [Lohum's description of its recycling business](https://lohum.com/news/125)

The Ministry of Mines has explicitly discussed limited domestic refining and black-mass exports when announcing support for critical-mineral recycling. The existence of a scheme does not establish eligibility or a currently open application window for this project. [Ministry of Mines/PIB, October 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2182108&lang=2&reg=3)

**Entry gates:** contracted, assayed feedstock by chemistry; buyer-signed output specification and sample acceptance; throughput/yield and mass-balance demonstration; reagent, energy and waste-treatment quotations; site and operating permissions; metal-price sensitivities; battery-purchase credit terms. LFP does not contain the nickel/cobalt value of NMC feedstock: do not use one economic model for both. C-MET's pyro option should be compared with the hydromet route for the intended feed and buyer. An alloy customer is different from a cathode-material customer.

### 4. Rare-earth-free motor industrialisation

The attractive product is a complete, application-proven motor/controller combination with defensible IP rights and a route to manufacturing, not a motor housing business. Start with one vehicle class and continuous/peak power requirement. A rare-earth-free design can still contain ferrite magnets; it is not necessarily magnet-free. The government has acknowledged India's rare-earth magnet import dependence. [PIB, December 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2202967&lang=1&reg=3)

**Material correction to the partnership assumption:** Greaves' official April 2025 announcement says its Chara motors and controllers will be exclusively manufactured by Greaves at Shendra. Chara is therefore evidence of domestic technology and competitive activity, not a verified licence available to this new venture. Greaves' current portfolio describes ferrite-magnet-assisted synchronous reluctance technology. [Greaves exchange release](https://greavescotton.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Greavescot_09042025_chara.pdf), [current e-powertrain portfolio](https://greavescotton.com/mobility-solutions/e-powertrain/)

A public technology route exists for investigation, but its limitations matter. A C-DAC EVSS EOI includes IIT Delhi's **reduced-rare-earth**, 5 kW IPMSM/controller. It combines NdFeB and ferrite magnets; field trials and certification were pending in the product annex. The published application deadline was 15 January 2026, so current availability is unverified. The broad programme's TRL description must not override the individual product's stated maturity. [C-DAC EOI, annex pages 17–19](https://www.cdac.in/index.aspx?id=lu_EVSS01_EOI_5ProjectsV1)

ARAI's current technology catalogue lists an MHI-supported 3W e-axle powertrain kit and its powertrain division lists motor and e-axle design capability. These are relevant technical discussions to seek, but do not prove a ready, open rare-earth-free motor licence. [ARAI technologies](https://www.araiindia.com/research-development-and-technologies/technologies), [ARAI powertrain capability](https://www.araiindia.com/centre-of-excellence/centre-of-excellence-powertrain)

**Entry gates:** written IP/licence terms; engineering team with motor and controller experience; complete efficiency map and Indian drive-cycle validation; torque ripple/NVH; thermal derating, overspeed and endurance; controller calibration and faults; nominated vehicle programme and warranty responsibility. A laboratory peak-efficiency number is not proof of vehicle efficiency or production readiness.

### 5. Thermal cold plates/manifolds

A defensible entry is a qualified cooling assembly whose thermal resistance, pressure drop, cleanliness and lifetime leakage are difficult to reproduce. A simple pipe-bending shop provides less differentiated import substitution. Basic plates, hoses and structures should not be confused with a complete BTMS containing compressor, pump, valves, chiller and controls.

Indian alternatives are already visible. Tata AutoComp Air International offers integrated battery thermal systems; Alkraft offers BTMS products; Pricol and UCAL list electric coolant pumps. Their presence weakens a blanket import-dependence claim while leaving room for individual advanced parts. [Tata AutoComp Air International technology](https://www.tataautocomp.com/taco-air-international/technology/), [Alkraft BTMS](https://alkraft.com/btms/), [Pricol electric coolant pump](https://pricol.com/solution/electrical-coolant-pump/), [UCAL pump](https://ucal.com/electric-coolant-pump/)

**Entry gates:** customer cooling model and interface; selected joining process; flatness and distortion control; pressure/leak inspection, coolant compatibility, corrosion and thermal-cycle durability. Decide whether to manufacture a plate, a fluid assembly or the complete controlled thermal system: they require different expertise and investment. No product-specific import volume was verified here.

### 6. Stator/rotor subassemblies

Pitti's annual report documents established electrical lamination, stator/rotor and related manufacturing capability. Competing on a customer-specific assembly is plausible, but a new plant cannot claim to solve motor dependence while continuing to import the critical magnetic material or all high-grade steel. [Pitti FY2024–25 annual report](https://www.pitti.in/api/investor-relation/download/HS_Pitti%20Engineering%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf?disposition=inline&id=847)

The practical initial product is one controlled lamination/stack or wound-stator family with precision tooling, burr/insulation control, winding and impregnation competence, electrical testing and traceability. A broad high-volume lamination-plus-hairpin-plus-diecast-rotor factory is a different capital proposition. Obtain a sourcing nomination and material qualification first. This candidate could supply a successful motor programme rather than be launched independently on speculative EV demand.

### 7. Traction shafts/reduction gears

Sona Comstar's disclosures show Indian capability in EV drivetrain products, including precision gear/shaft development and supply. Consequently, ordinary machining is not a sufficiently strong reason to select this category. [Sona technology disclosure](https://sonacomstar.com/annual-report-23-24/pdf/s4-technological-powess.pdf), [FY2025–26 annual report](https://sonacomstar.com/annual-report-25-26/index.html)


A viable narrow entry would have a nominated hard-to-localise shaft/gear, controlled heat treatment and grinding, gear metrology and a proven noise/endurance programme. A complete reducer adds bearings, seals, housing, lubrication and system validation; a complete e-axle additionally adds the motor/inverter. The estimated machining-cell capital cannot be reused as a complete e-axle factory budget.

### 8. Enclosures and crash structures

Hindalco announced 10,000 battery enclosures supplied to Mahindra in April 2025 and an approximately ₹500 crore Chakan facility initially sized for 80,000 enclosures/year. This establishes serious domestic competition and the scale of one integrated project. It does **not** establish that every enclosure supplier needs ₹500 crore. Smaller welded sections, brackets and machining/fabrication subassemblies remain possible within the user envelope. [Hindalco release, 25 April 2025](https://www.hindalco.com/media/press-releases/hindalco-delivers-10000-aluminium-battery-enclosures-to-mahindra-from-its-new-state-of-the-art-facility-in-chakan)

However, a ₹5–15 crore subassembly operation should not be described as an equivalent complete structural enclosure factory. Require an OEM/Tier 1 drawing, nominated annual volume and explicit allocation of crash, sealing and corrosion-validation responsibility. This is a reasonable contract-manufacturing opportunity when a contract exists; it is a weak standalone import-reduction thesis.

## How the network changes the decision

The user's direct H. D. Kumaraswamy connection is an assumed route to relevant conversations, not a verified purchase commitment, licence or funding sanction. It is most commercially useful when it produces introductions to OEM engineering/supplier-development teams, a specific localisation problem, an existing sample/drawing, and access to the right technical/test institution. ARAI's documented MHI relationship makes its technology and validation work particularly relevant.

Recycling technologies cited here sit within MeitY/C-MET, CSIR and other government bodies, and critical-mineral support involves the Ministry of Mines. Access to MHI should not be described as control over these organisations' technology licences or approval decisions. A steel-sector relationship could help discussions about electrical-steel supply, but does not make a steel mill or magnet plant feasible inside this initial envelope.

The next useful network request is: **identify two imported parts/processes per candidate, introduce the engineer responsible for qualification, and establish whether that buyer will sponsor sample validation or a pilot order.** That provides a reason to choose one factory. General introductions to senior people do not resolve the remaining product-level gap.

## Minimum evidence before selecting the winner

1. A customer-specific imported reference and annual requirement, or a contracted domestic-material offtake in recycling.
2. A technically qualified cofounder/partner with rights to the required design/process; executed licence terms where applicable.
3. A process flow and vendor quotations separating equipment, tooling, laboratory work, utilities, launch engineering and working capital.
4. Prototype/sample acceptance criteria agreed with the customer; an honest estimate of the qualification lead time and who pays for it.
5. Unit economics including rejected output, warranty/service, purchased imported subcomponents, finance and cash collection. No blanket margin or payback estimate is justified by this research.

**Recommended disposition:** carry the first four into the EV shortlist with their stated conditions; keep qualified thermal assemblies as reserve. Do not choose commodity enclosures, ordinary shafts or generic motor laminations merely because they carry an EV label. The strongest strategic candidate and the fastest executable candidate need not be the same business.
