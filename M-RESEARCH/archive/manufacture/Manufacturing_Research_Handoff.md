## 3. Method: the 4 buying-chain checks (use for every product)

1. **Who actually buys it?** (data-centre operator, EPC/MEP contractor, OEM, rack builder, pack assembler, machine maker…)
2. **Where does it get fitted?** (in a factory abroad, or at an Indian plant/site)
3. **How much arrives PRE-INSTALLED inside imported equipment?** (e.g., cold plates inside imported AI racks)
4. **How much is bought/made locally in India today?**

**Ranking criteria used after the checks:** AI/EV/semiconductor linkage; India local market size & growth (2026 → 2030); feasibility within ₹1–30 cr; competition; Bengaluru fit; resilience (other industries can buy the same product).

### 7.2 Liquid cooling — technology, market, players
- **Why needed:** GB200 module up to 1,200 W; NVL72 rack ~130 kW; NVIDIA roadmap toward ~1 MW racks; air works to ~700 W/chip. All chip power ends up as heat.
- **Adoption (TrendForce, 17 Aug 2026):** liquid-cooled share of AI chips **33% (2025) → 53% (2026) → ~60% (2027)**; Google >80% of AI servers.
- **Global market:** Dell'Oro ~$3B (2025) → ~$7B (2029) (narrow definition); broader estimates $4–14B for 2026. **Base estimate: ~$8B (2026) → ~$20B (2030)**; precision-metal-parts slice ~$2.4B → ~$6B.
- **India liquid cooling:** ~$110M (2026) → ~$700M (2030) (base est.); India cooling overall $2.99B (2026) → $9.28B (2031) (Mordor). S&P: ~₹23,500 cr cooling investment over 4–5 years.
- **Per-rack BOM (GB300 NVL72):** Morgan Stanley $49,860 = **tray-level only** (18 trays × $2,260 + 9 switch trays × $1,020). Full rack cooling est. **~$77K**: cold plates ~47% (~$300/GPU), QDs & in-tray tubing ~18% (GB200: 198 QDs at ~$70), rack manifold ~10% ($6–10K), CDU ~25% ($18–20K). ~30% is machined/welded metal.
- **How a pre-plumbed rack gets cooled:** rack (cold plates + tubes + rack manifold) → hoses to **CDU** (pump + liquid-to-liquid heat exchanger; secondary loop, PG25 coolant) → **facility water loop** → dry coolers/chillers/cooling towers. Site steps: build facility loop & CDUs → run pipes to rack positions → roll in rack → connect supply/return → flush, fill, bleed air → pressure/leak test → start pumps → power servers. GB200 inlet coolant **30–45°C** (warm water → dry coolers possible), **~2 L/s** per rack, manifold branch flow ±5%, some residual air load. No facility water → liquid-to-air sidecar CDU.
- **Global players & results:** Vertiv (2026 guide ~$14B, +37%); nVent (organic +47%); AVC (Q2 2026 revenue +66%, gross margin 32.6%); Auras (Jan–Jul +83%); Fositek (QDs); Delta; Cooler Master; CoolIT (10,000s of cold-plate loops/month).
- **Consolidation:** Eaton–Boyd Thermal $9.5B (closed 12 Mar 2026); Ecolab–CoolIT $4.75B (closed 2 Jul 2026); Schneider–Motivair 75% $850M; Vertiv–CoolTera, Vertiv–PurgeRite (~$1B), Vertiv–ThermoKey; Samsung–FläktGroup (~$1.6B); Trane–LiquidStack; Flex–JetCool; Daikin–Chilldyne.
- **Counts:** ~40 CDU vendors globally (Dell'Oro; expects <10 to dominate); ~6 NVIDIA-scale cold-plate makers (mostly Taiwan); ~10 manifold makers; ~10–12 QD makers; ~8–10 immersion. ### 17.2 The real signal: PMP localisation mandates (notified 3 Sep 2026)
- **From 1 Sep 2026 (e-bus traction motor):** rotor & stator assembly, bearing, enclosure, connector and cable fitment must be done in India. Import relaxation for rare-earth motors ended 31 Aug 2026.
- **From 1 Apr 2027 (e-bus + e-truck):** magnet and shaft fitment; for e-trucks the full motor list; **motor controller/inverter: PCB component & semiconductor assembly, HV connector, cable, heatsink and enclosure fitment, firmware flashing**.
- SIAM has asked for the 1 Sep 2026 date to be deferred (magnet supply) — watch for slippage.
- **PMP component lists (v9 PDFs, pmedrive.heavyindustries.gov.in):** e-2W/e-3W and e-bus must locally assemble battery pack, BMS, DC-DC, VCU, on-board charger, traction motor, motor controller+inverter, instrument cluster; **e-buses add HVAC/VAC unit (#1), AC Type-2 charging inlet (#2), DC CCS2 charging inlet (#3)**. Annexure-1 adds gearbox, axles, suspension, brakes+ABS, chassis, body, wheels, harness, telematics, lighting, seats, glass.
- **Key nuance (Annexure-2):** partly-imported child parts + Indian assembly still counts as indigenous. The rule forces the *finished named part* into India, not every sub-part.

## 18. Deep dives: charging inlets (item 1) and e-bus thermal parts (item 4) — 19 Sep 2026

### 18.1 Vehicle-side charging inlets — CORRECTION + detail
- **Market correction:** the "$280–350M / 55–65% imported" figure is the **whole HV interconnect pool** (battery, inverter, MSD, DC-DC), NOT inlets. **Vehicle-side inlets alone ≈ ₹270 cr (FY26) → ~₹900 cr (2030)** (agent derivation).
- **Value split:** e-cars ~45% of value; e-2W/3W 87% of units but ~40% of value; e-bus/truck ~7% of value on 0.8% of units.
- **Prices [est.]:** e-2W ₹250–600; e-3W ₹400–900; car AC ₹1,800, car CCS2 ₹6,000–9,000; **bus AC Type-2 ₹2,500–4,000; bus DC CCS2 ₹9,000–15,000**.
- **Standards:** IS 17017 Part 2 — Sec 2 (AC Type-2 = IEC 62196-2), Sec 3 (CCS2), Sec 6 (LEVDC/Type 6), **Sec 7 (LECCS/Type 7, 2023, Ather-originated, works with >90% of Indian e-2Ws)**. Homologation via ARAI/ICAT/GARC; CPRI Bengaluru has an EV charger test lab (charger-side scope). Type-test dossier ≈ **₹8–20 lakh, 4–8 months per variant [est.]**.
- **Test severity (TE spec 108-94907):** ≤10,000 mating cycles; contact temp ≤90°C at 350–500A; IP6K9K/IPX9K; vibration 5g; shock 50g; thermal shock −40/+85°C.
- **Policy nuance (important):** the PMP **inlet mandate is e-BUS ONLY** (items #2 AC Type-2 and #3 DC CCS2). The e-2W/e-3W PMP has 8 items and **no inlet**; sockets fall under the weaker Annexure-1 harness/connector line. The reported DVA relaxation applies to **chargers (EVSE)**, not vehicle inlets.
- **Competition:** Elcom International (₹100–500 cr), Trinity Touch (₹500–1,000 cr), UKB (mostly cable), IPEC, JT Mobility, Ingar; globally TE (**bought Phoenix Contact's inlet business, Mar 2026**), Amphenol, ITT, Yazaki, Sumitomo, Zhejiang Yonggui.
- **Hard parts:** silver contact-plating uniformity (the exact gap IEEFA names), thermal design at 90°C limit, tooling **₹40–80 lakh [est.]** for moulds + stamping dies. Buy in: stamped contacts, PA66-GF/PBT resin, PT1000 sensors, 12V lock actuators, seals.
- **Time to first serial revenue: 24–36 months** for car/bus (IATF 16949 9–15 months + PPAP 6–12); 12–18 months for 2W.
- **Verdict:** viable as a **narrow technical tier-2**: 3–5% share → **₹25–45 cr revenue by 2030 on ₹8–15 cr capex**. Start with the **e-bus/e-truck CCS2 + AC Type-2 pair**; add LECCS Type-7 later; do NOT start with ₹400 2W sockets.
- **Risks:** many Indian e-buses still use GB/T DC (Switch EiV9 moved to CCS2 only in Jul 2026); plating quality; 24–36 month cash burn; Elcom/Trinity are 100–1,000× bigger; the moat disappears if PM E-DRIVE lapses.

### 18.2 E-bus/e-truck thermal sub-assemblies — DOWNGRADE + detail
- **Value per bus [est.]:** roof HVAC (32–42 kW, e-compressor) ₹4.5–7.0 lakh; battery thermal system ₹1.5–3.0 lakh; **metal/fluid content a small firm can address ₹0.6–1.5 lakh/bus**. E-truck ≈ half.
- **Addressable market [est.]:** **₹30–80 cr/yr today** realistically; ₹110–290 cr/yr if e-buses reach ~10,000/yr and you sell the full kit.
- **Volumes:** FY26 e-bus sales ≈ **5,000–5,500** (Switch 1,166, JBM 1,149, PMI 1,061, Olectra 853, Tata 153). ICRA: 30% e-bus penetration by FY30 ⇒ ~12,000–18,000/yr [est.]. E-trucks tiny (611 in H1 2026).
- **CESL 10,900-bus award (Dec 2025):** PMI 5,210, EKA 3,485, Olectra 1,785, Anthony 420 — at 5–15% below estimate. **Bengaluru 4,500; first award 28 Jan 2026 = EKA Mobility + Chartered Speed, 1,750 buses.**
- **Plants:** EKA Chakan/Pithampur; PMI Dharuhera + Pune; Switch Chennai/Ennore; Olectra Hyderabad; **Tata Dharwad (Karnataka)**; Ashok Leyland Hosur; Veera Vahana Bengaluru; Volvo Hoskote. **The winners of Bengaluru's 4,500 build them outside Karnataka.** No local-content preference found in the BMTC/CESL tender.
- **Who supplies thermal today:** TKT (China; supplies BYD, Olectra, Tata, Switch), Guchen, KingClima; Indian system makers Subros (₹175 cr e-compressor plant, live FY28), Sidwal/Amber, Trans ACNR, Pranav Vikas, Alkraft, Banco, Sanden Vikas, Mahle Anand, Hanon, Valeo.
- **They buy out:** sheet-metal housings/covers, ducting, brackets, tube bends and pipe spools, hose assemblies, **aluminium manifolds and valve blocks**, expansion tanks, condenser/evaporator frames. They keep in-house: compressors, brazed heat exchangers, controls, refrigerant charging, final test.
- **PMP force:** HVAC/VAC is item #1 on the e-bus list (fitment of blowers, e-compressor, chiller, heater, vents, filter, control panel must be domestic); import of a finished PMP component is not permitted; **test agencies may audit the supplier's plant and pull invoices down the chain**.
- **Best entry:** **leak-tested aluminium coolant manifolds + valve blocks sold as a kit with tube/hose assemblies** — lowest competition, best margin, entry **₹2.5–4 cr [est.]**. Cold plates are the bigger prize (₹40k–1.2 L/bus) but need FSW/vacuum brazing + helium leak (₹3–6 cr) and face Alkraft/Pranav Vikas/Banco.
- **Sell to the system makers, not the bus OEMs** — their qualification cycle is 3–4× faster (manifolds 8–14 months vs OEM 12–24).
- **Qualification:** IATF 16949 (12–18 months, ₹3–6 lakh + consultant), PPAP L3 per part; AIS-052/AIS-153 are bus-body codes certified by the body builder, not by you; you must own helium leak, pressure-decay/burst, glycol compatibility, salt spray, vibration testing.
- **Risks:** gross-cost-contract model (operator owns the bus, 12-year uptime liability, brutal price pressure); TKT can satisfy PMP by shipping child parts to an Indian assembler; lumpy volumes across 6 OEMs in 5 states; 90–120 day payments from cash-tight tier-1s; scheme slippage (motor rule already moved to Apr 2027).
- **Cross-sell that saves it:** the same manifold/leak-test line serves e-trucks, tractors, gensets, off-highway **and data-centre cooling (item 9)** — that is how the line stays loaded.

### 18.3 Revised view on these two
- Neither is as big as the Section 17 table implied. Both are **niche tier-2 businesses**, not ₹100 cr markets on their own.
- **For a founder with no technical background and no appetite for a 24–36 month burn: start with the manifold/valve-block route (18.2)** — cheaper entry, faster qualification, and it shares a factory with items 5 and 9.
- **Charging inlets (18.1) are the better long-run product** (higher price per part, named in policy, thin Indian competition at the inlet end) **but only with a connector/plating expert on the team and 2–3 years of funded runway.**
