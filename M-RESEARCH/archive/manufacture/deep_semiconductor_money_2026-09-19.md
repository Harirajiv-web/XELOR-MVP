# India's Semiconductor Money Trail — Deep Research
**As of 19 September 2026** · Prepared for a Bengaluru-based prospective manufacturer
**Ranking basis:** market potential and strength of government-driven demand only. Capex is not a constraint. Whether the client wins orders is explicitly NOT considered.

**Evidence grading used throughout:**
- **[NOTIFIED]** — in a gazette notification or official scheme document. Legally binding.
- **[ANNOUNCED]** — stated by a minister, company or press release. Intention, not money.
- **[DISBURSED]** — money actually paid out, or an order actually placed.

### 1.3 THE KEY CLAUSE FOR THIS CLIENT — the domestic-sourcing PLI

**The client's reported figures are CORRECT and I confirm them verbatim from the gazette.** Category 4, sub-vertical (d):

> "Manufacturing and/or Assembly of Equipment/refurbish Equipment, Sub-Assemblies, Components for use in all semiconductor fabrication and packaging facilities — **30% of Capital Expenditure on pari-passu basis AND Production Linked Incentive (PLI) of 10% / 8% / 6% / 4% / 2% of BoM value sourced from domestic manufactures. The PLI incentive will be applicable for 5 years starting from FY 2028-29 subject to an overall ceiling of 50% of the eligible capex.**"
> — Gazette of India, 31 Aug 2026, para 3.4.3

**Exactly what this subsidises — read this carefully, it is narrower than it first appears:**

- The **recipient** is the company that **builds or assembles semiconductor capital equipment** (or refurbishes it), or its sub-assemblies and components, in India. It is **not** the fab or the OSAT.
- The **subsidised purchase** is that equipment-maker's **bill of materials bought from Indian manufacturers**. BoM is defined in Guidelines para 2.6 as "all raw materials, components, sub-assemblies, parts and components required to manufacture or assemble an identified finished product."
- **"Domestic Sourcing" is defined generously** (Guidelines para 2.11): "the sourcing of assemblies, sub-assemblies, parts and components manufactured within India, **using either domestically sourced or imported raw materials**." → **An Indian component maker may import its raw material (steel, aluminium, specialty alloy) and the part still counts as domestically sourced.** This is a major and under-reported concession.
- The rate **declines** 10% → 8% → 6% → 4% → 2% across the five years FY2028-29 to FY2032-33. It is **not** 10% every year.
- The cap is **50% of the eligible capex** of the equipment maker, aggregated across the whole PLI.
- **Claim mechanic (this creates a formal supplier register):** the equipment maker "shall submit a **declaration from the domestic manufacturer as per Annexure-2**." If sourcing runs through a contract manufacturer or supply-chain partner, the applicant must ensure that partner is *actually producing* the component in India (Guidelines para 9.7).

**Commercial reading:** an Indian firm supplying machined parts, chambers, gas panels, frames or sub-assemblies to an equipment builder in India hands that builder a 2–10% cash rebate on the purchase price that the builder does not get from an import. That is a structural price preference of up to 10 points in the client's favour, written into a gazette, starting FY2029.

### 1.3a Category 4 thresholds — RESOLVED, and the numbers matter a lot

Re-extracting the gazette page in raw reading order binds each threshold to its sub-vertical unambiguously:

| Sub-vertical (Category 4) | Min. capital investment | Min. revenue | Fiscal support |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a)** Setting up **R&D facilities for semiconductor equipment** | **₹300 cr** | **₹120 cr** | 30% capex, pari-passu |
| **(b)** **Manufacturing facilities for semiconductor-grade raw materials** (front-end/back-end) — *"Wafer, Photo mask, Photoresist, **Substrate**, chemicals, gases, materials, glass for display fab etc."* | **₹50 cr** | **₹20 cr** | 30% capex, pari-passu |
| **(c)** **Semiconductor Test and characterization Facilities** | **₹100 cr** | **₹40 cr** | 30% capex, pari-passu |
| **(d)** **Manufacturing and/or Assembly of Equipment / refurbish Equipment, Sub-Assemblies, Components** for all semiconductor fabrication and packaging facilities | **₹300 cr** | **₹120 cr** | 30% capex **+ the 10/8/6/4/2% PLI** |

**Two consequences that change the opportunity ranking:**

1. **Materials manufacturing is the cheapest door into Semicon 2.0 — ₹50 crore capex and ₹20 crore of prior revenue.** That is one-sixth the threshold for equipment manufacturing, and it explicitly includes **substrates, chemicals, gases and materials**.
2. **Test and characterisation (₹100 cr / ₹40 cr) is the only sub-vertical exempted from the "own or possess licensed technologies" requirement** (gazette para 3.4.3). It is the lowest *technical* barrier in the entire scheme.

The **₹300 cr / ₹120 cr** tier that press reporting attached to component and sub-assembly manufacturing ([Business Today, 31 Aug 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/semicon-2-0-notified-rs1-27-lakh-crore-scheme-widens-indias-chip-ambitions-552278-2026-08-31)) **is correct for sub-vertical (d)** — so the PLI-bearing equipment/component route does require ₹300 cr of capex and ₹120 cr of existing revenue. A new entrant below that scale reaches the same customers through **ECMS Segment D (₹10 crore entry, 25% capex — see §4)** or through **Category 4(b) materials at ₹50 crore**.

### 1.4 Eligible capex definition (Guidelines para 2.7) — matters for what gets bought

Eligible capex **includes**: building, plant, machinery, **clean rooms**, equipment and **associated utilities**; tools, dies, moulds, jigs, fixtures **including parts, accessories, components and spares**; packaging, freight, insurance, **erection and commissioning**. "Associated utilities" is expressly defined to include **captive power, effluent treatment, clean rooms, air curtains, temperature and air-quality control, compressed air, water and power supply, control systems, and storage facilities for chemicals and gases and their distribution systems**, plus manufacturing IT/ERP.

It **excludes**: land and land development, temporary structures, technology transfer, interest during construction, and R&D cost. Second-hand equipment is allowed if it has ≥5 years residual life.

**Why this matters:** cleanroom build, gas and chemical distribution systems, and utility hook-up are all **inside** the 30–40% subsidised capex base. The government is co-paying 30–40% of the client's potential customers' spend on exactly those items.

### 1.6 Approval route

For Machines & Materials, Fabs and ATMP/OSAT, the **Union Cabinet** is the competent authority for every approval (Guidelines para 2.10; Gazette para 7.4). ### 3.2 The client's "~0% local now, 20–30% in 3–4 years" claim — PARTIALLY VERIFIED, FLAGGED

I could **not** find a published, attributable source giving a specific current local-content percentage for **OSAT materials specifically** (substrates, lead frames, mould compound, bonding wire), nor a formal government target of "20–30% in 3–4 years". The directionally-consistent published figures are the **85% specialty-chemical import dependence** and **>85% equipment import dependence** above. **Treat "~0% → 20–30%" as a reasonable industry estimate, not a sourced government target.** The one hard localisation target I did find is for finished electronics, not semiconductor materials: domestic value addition in mobile phones is **18–20%** (ICEA, Jul 2026) against a **government target of 35–40%** (Aug 2026) ([Wikipedia industry article, sourced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics_and_semiconductor_manufacturing_industry_in_India)).

### 3.3 Fab capex composition — where the rupees actually go

For a 300 mm fab at mature nodes (28–45 nm), total USD 5–7 bn, the split is ([Bisinfotech](https://www.bisinfotech.com/building-a-chip-fab-from-scratch-infrastructure-costs-and-challenges/)):

| Line | Share of capex | Applied to the ₹1.64 lakh cr approved ISM pipeline (my derivation) |
|---|---|---|
| **Equipment** (tools) | **50–60%** | ₹82,000–98,400 cr — >85% imported today |
| **Cleanroom construction** | **15–20%** | **₹24,600–32,800 cr** — built in India, cannot be imported pre-assembled |
| **Utilities & infrastructure** | **10–15%** | **₹16,400–24,600 cr** — gas/chemical delivery, UPW, HVAC, power |
| Land & permits | 5–8% | ₹8,200–13,100 cr |
| Training & workforce | 2–5% | — |
| Licensing & IP | 2–5% | — |

A fab also consumes **up to 10 million gallons/day of ultra-pure water** and **100–200 MW continuous**, and runs **500+ advanced tools**; cleanrooms are **Class 1 to Class 1000** with vibration-free flooring, laminar flow, humidity/temperature control.

**This is the single most useful derived number in this report: roughly ₹41,000–57,000 crore of cleanroom plus utility/hook-up scope sits inside the already-approved Indian project pipeline, and by its physical nature it is executed in India.**

### 4.3 Application windows — CRITICAL, and this is the live deadline

> **"The application window for Target Segments A, B, C & E is closed on 30th September 2025. For Target Segment D, the window will remain open till 30th April 2027."**
> — [ecms.meity.gov.in](https://ecms.meity.gov.in/), read 19 September 2026

- **Segments A, B, C (and E): CLOSED** since 30 Sep 2025. May be reopened — the notification permits reopening "anytime during its tenure based on the response from the industry and budget availability" — but **no fresh window has been notified.**
- **Segment D: OPEN until 30 April 2027.** Applications are appraised **on an ongoing basis**, and fiscal support is available for investment made **within 5 years of the acknowledgement date**.

**For a Bengaluru manufacturer deciding what to make, Segment D is the only ECMS door currently open, it needs only ₹10 crore of investment, and it pays 25% of capex.** It covers both (a) parts and components feeding other component makers and (b) **capital goods for electronics manufacturing including their sub-assemblies and components**.

**Flagged:** a **"Segment E — telecom sub-assemblies"** is referenced by [startupadvisory.in](https://www.startupadvisory.in/blog-ecms-electronics-component-scheme.htm) and by the ECMS portal banner, but **does not appear in the 8 April 2025 gazette notification**, which lists only A–D. ## 5. State-level policies with actual money

### 5.0 The structural fact that governs every state number

Most states express their incentive as a **percentage of the central assistance you have already won**. That makes the state money worthless unless ISM or ECMS approves you first. Only Gujarat, Odisha and UP offer a meaningful **standalone** capital subsidy that does not require central approval.

### 5.1 KARNATAKA — the client's home state, and the weakest offer of the six

**No dedicated semiconductor policy exists.** Karnataka runs two instruments:
- **Karnataka ESDM Policy 2017-22** ([policy PDF](https://eitbt.karnataka.gov.in/esdm/public/uploads/media_to_upload1726919851.pdf))
- **Special Incentives Scheme for the ESDM Sector 2020-2025 (SIS)** — GO No. ITBT 101 ADM 2020, **7 September 2020** ([operational guidelines PDF](https://eitbt.karnataka.gov.in/esdm/public/uploads/media_to_upload1726920403.pdf))

**SIS money (all verified from the official guidelines PDF):**

| Head | Quantum |
|---|---|
| Capital subsidy on plant & machinery | **20% of eligible P&M capex — available in all districts including Bengaluru** |
| Land subsidy | **25% of land cost — EXCLUDED in Bengaluru Urban and Bengaluru Rural**; up to 50 acres, capped **₹12.5 cr** |
| Stamp duty + registration | 100% reimbursement |
| Land conversion fee | 100% reimbursement |
| **Power tariff** | **₹1.00/unit for 5 years** from commercial production |
| Electricity duty | 100% exemption for 5 years |
| PLI | 1.00% of annual net sales turnover for 5 years |
| **Minimum investment** | **₹15 cr eligible P&M capex (new unit); ₹50 cr (expansion)** |
| Overall cap | all incentives ≤ 100% of value of fixed assets |

**Component manufacture is explicitly in scope** — SIS target segments name "Semiconductor manufacturing & design including manufacturing of Semiconductors, ICs, **PCBs, bare boards, chip components**, chemicals for board manufacture."

**Three serious problems, all flagged:**

1. **Karnataka has NO top-up formula over ISM or ECMS.** Gujarat gives 40%, UP gives a 1:1 match, Tamil Nadu 50%, Assam 40%, Odisha 25%. **Karnataka gives nothing.** This is a verified absence, not a research gap.
2. **The Bengaluru Urban penalty is severe.** ESDM land subsidy excludes Bengaluru Urban and Rural. Under the **Karnataka Industrial Policy 2025-30** ([operative guidelines](https://investkarnataka.co.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Karnataka.pdf)), Zone 3 — "only for Bengaluru Rural" — gets **10% capital subsidy, NIL stamp duty exemption, NIL land conversion reimbursement**, against Zone 1's 25% / 100% / 100%. **A plant inside Bengaluru Urban gets essentially nothing from the Industrial Policy.** Moving to a Zone 1/2 taluk is worth roughly 25% of fixed assets plus stamp duty.
3. **The policy's legal life is contradictory.** Extension order ITBT 11 PRM 2025 dated **15 Sep 2025** ([PDF](https://eitbt.karnataka.gov.in/esdm/public/uploads/media_to_upload1758191925.pdf)) states the *proposal* was to extend "for a period of one year from 7th September 2025 to 6th September 2026" while quoting the Finance Department concurring "to extend... **for a period of five years from 7th September 2025**". **The one-year reading expired 6 September 2026 — thirteen days ago.** Officials describe it as extended to 2030 ([DQ India](https://www.dqindia.com/semiconductors/karnataka-government-provides-details-of-esdm-policies-12022406)). **Get written confirmation from KITS before relying on it.** A replacement electronics policy has been "coming shortly" since September 2025 and is still not notified.

**Also note the mutual-exclusivity trap:** a unit availing SIS **cannot** also avail the Karnataka Industrial Policy 2025-30 for the same project. Pick one.

**Karnataka semiconductor infrastructure:**
- **200-acre Semiconductor Park at KWIN City** near Bengaluru, Phase-1 targeted operational **December 2026** ([Indian Infrastructure, 24 Jul 2026](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2026/07/24/karnatakas-200-acre-semiconductor-park-near-bengaluru-set-for-december-2026-launch/)). **[ANNOUNCED]** — no published land price or park-specific incentive schedule.
- **EMCs:** Kochanahalli, Mysuru (234 acres acquired, 140 earmarked for a semiconductor park; **Kaynes Technology and Würth have taken plots**); Kotur-Belur, Dharwad/Hubballi (224.5 acres, MeitY-approved); Adinarayana Hosahalli, Bengaluru Rural.

**Karnataka has NO ISM-approved fab or OSAT.** What it has is a state-level package cleared by a Cabinet sub-committee on **29 May 2025**, as an explicit exception over and above the ESDM Policy ([Indian Infrastructure](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2025/05/29/cabinet-panel-approves-incentives-for-three-semiconductor-projects-in-karnataka/)):

| Company | Location | Investment | Jobs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Applied Materials** — R&D fab / Innovation Centre | Bengaluru | **₹4,851 cr** | 1,500 |
| **Lam Research** — R&D lab ₹6,790 cr + **silicon component manufacturing ₹9,111 cr** | Karnataka | **₹15,901 cr** | 1,400 |
| **Bharat Semi Systems** — integrated design + SiC/GaN fab | Mysuru | **₹2,342 cr** | 620+ |

**This is the single most important state-level fact for this client.** Applied Materials and Lam Research — the two companies that announced India supply-chain expansion at SEMICON India 2026 (§3.4) — are building their Indian footprint **in Karnataka**, and Lam's ₹9,111 cr line is explicitly **silicon component manufacturing**. The buyers named in opportunity #3 are being built in the client's own state. In May 2026 the Cabinet allotted **140 acres at Bengaluru Signature Business Park near the airport to Applied Materials**. *(The rupee value of the state incentive granted to each is undisclosed — flagged.)*

**Budget FY2026-27:** presented 6 March 2026, total expenditure ₹4,48,004 cr; state **expects ₹45,000 cr of investment** in electronics and semiconductors; **₹1,000 cr to the LEAP programme**. **The ₹45,000 cr is an expected-investment figure, NOT a subsidy budget line.** Cumulative approvals cited by the minister: ₹23,000 cr over two years.

### 5.2 UTTAR PRADESH — materially the best offer in India for a component maker

**UP Semiconductor Policy 2024** — notified **12 February 2024** ([notification PDF](https://invest.up.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Notification_120224.pdf)): **50% additional capital subsidy on top of the central subsidy**; **75% land rebate on the first 200 acres**; 100% stamp duty exemption; **electricity duty exemption for 10 years**; **50% wheeling charge exemption for 25 years**; 5% interest subsidy.

**UP Electronics Component Manufacturing Policy 2025 (UPECMP)** — notified **18 November 2025**, first amendment **7 September 2026** ([English PDF](https://invest.up.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/up-electronuc-componentpolicy_200526.pdf)):

- **If you win central ECMS: UP pays a 1:1 matching top-up** — "additional incentives shall be equivalent to the incentives admissible under the central scheme for each product." Combined central + state capped at **100% of eligible capital investment**.
- **If you DON'T win central ECMS: you still get a 20% capital grant.** Clause 4.6 says units failing the central scheme's turnover/investment tests **remain eligible for the state grant**. **No other state has this.**
- **Land and building count as eligible capex for non-ECMS units** (central ECMS excludes them) — up to 30% of fixed capital investment excluding land/building, or ₹50 cr, whichever is lower.
- **No minimum investment floor.**
- +2% for in-state Cathode Active Material; +1% for in-state laminate for multi-layer PCB (halved if sourced from outside UP).
- Targeted components explicitly include **"production support equipment used in electronics manufacturing, including its sub-units and components"** — equipment makers are in scope by name.
- **100 acres next to Jewar (Noida International) airport dedicated to semiconductor units**; HCL-Foxconn OSAT there received a **75% waiver on additional land costs**.

### 5.3 GUJARAT

**Gujarat Semiconductor Policy 2022-27** (announced Jul 2022, India's first; **expires mid-2027, no successor found**) ([GSEM page](https://gsem.gujarat.gov.in/Home/GujaratSemiconductorPolicy202227)):
**40% of the CAPEX assistance approved by the Government of India** · **75% land subsidy on the first 200 acres at Dholera Semicon City** (50% beyond) · 100% stamp duty reimbursement · **₹2/unit power subsidy for 10 years** · electricity duty exemption · **water at ₹12/m³ for 5 years** · 50% capital subsidy on a desalination plant.

**Gujarat Electronics Policy 2022-28** — *this is the one a component maker uses* ([GSEM page](https://gsem.gujarat.gov.in/Home/GujaratElectronicsPolicy)):
**Up to 20% of eligible capex where capex ≤ ₹1,000 cr, ceiling ₹200 cr** (15% on increments above ₹1,000 cr) · interest assistance up to 7% for 5 years, ₹10 cr/yr · **₹1/unit power for 6 years** · electricity duty exempt · 100% stamp duty reimbursement · **logistics: 25% of freight for 5 years, ₹5 cr/yr** · **equipment relocation: 50% of import cost, capped ₹5 cr** · 100%/75% employer EPF (female/male).

**This 20% capital subsidy has no "must be ISM-approved" gate — the most generous standalone offer of the six states.** Gujarat also hosts 6 of the 12 ISM projects (Dholera + Sanand).

### 5.4 ODISHA

**Odisha Semiconductor Manufacturing and Fabless Policy 2023** ([policy PDF](https://investodisha.gov.in/download/Odisha-Semi-Conductor-Manufacturing-and-Fabless-Policy-2023.pdf)):
**25% additional capex subsidy for ISM-approved projects, OR 30% of overall capex pari-passu on the non-ISM route** · **10% capex incentive on raw materials/components supplementing the central SPECS 25%** · 100% stamp duty exemption · **electricity duty exemption 10 years** · **₹2.00/unit power reimbursed for 10 years** · **water at ₹7.65/m³ for 10 years** · **interest subsidy 5% capped ₹25 cr/year for 7 years** · 100% net SGST reimbursement capped at 200% of P&M cost · 1% PLI for 5 years.

**The 30% non-ISM capex route and the ₹25 cr/year interest subsidy are the most component-friendly terms outside UP.** Projects: SiCSem (₹3,406.25 cr, Info Valley-II, Falta SEZ committee approval **16 Sep 2026**), **RIR Power Electronics ₹620 cr — India's first vertically integrated SiC facility, under construction**, 3D Glass Solutions, plus a ₹452 cr power-electronics hub at IIT Bhubaneswar (Sep 2026) and an announced-only 870-acre Naraj hub.

### 5.5 TAMIL NADU

**TN Semiconductor and Advanced Electronics Policy 2024** (released at GIM 2024, Jan 2024; **3-year validity → lapses ~Jan 2027**) ([policy PDF](https://icea.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Tamil-Nadu-Semiconductor-and-Advanced-Electronics-Policy-2024.pdf)):

- **Semiconductor track (ISM-approved only):** **up to 50% of the GoI CAPEX assistance** via a SIPCOT escrow · land at 10% concessional rate in A/B districts, 50% in C districts · 100% stamp duty exemption · electricity tax exemption 5 years · 5% interest subvention for 6 years.
- **Advanced Electronics track (the component/equipment route):** **20% fixed capital subsidy + 1.5% of annual turnover for 5 years** — **but requires a minimum ₹200 cr investment and 150 jobs, and is offered only to the first 20 applicants.** Out of reach for a ₹1–30 cr founder.
- **TN Semiconductor Mission 2030, ₹500 cr allocation.** TN accounted for **65% of ECMS proposals** by one state minister's count.

### 5.6 ASSAM — irrelevant to a component maker

**Assam Electronics (Semiconductor etc.) Policy 2023**, notified **29 August 2023**, in force 5 years ([notification PDF](https://industries.assam.gov.in/sites/default/files/swf_utility_folder/departments/industries_com_oid_4/portlet/level_2/notification_assam_electronics_semiconductor_etc_231211_101812.pdf)):
**Additional 40% of the GoI CAPEX assistance** (20% per year over 5 years) · land at nominal prices case-to-case · 100% stamp duty exemption · **up to 50% of power tariff for 10 years** · water at ₹5.00/m³ for 10 years · SGST reimbursement up to 15 years · **20% of net wages for local employees**.

**Critical gate (§4.2.1–4.2.2): only projects already qualified under an ISM scheme are eligible. There is no component-maker, equipment-maker, ancillary or MSME track in this policy at all.**

| **Karnataka** | **NONE — no top-up formula exists** | **20% on plant & machinery only** | **₹15 cr of P&M capex** | Yes (ICs, PCBs, bare boards, chip components) |
| **Assam** | 40% of GoI capex assistance | **None** | ISM approval required | **No** |

**Bottom line for a Bengaluru founder.** Karnataka gives a real 20% on plant and machinery, ₹1/unit power for 5 years, electricity duty exemption and a 1% PLI, and component manufacture is squarely in scope. But it is the **only one of the six states with no top-up to ISM or ECMS**, its land subsidy and the entire Industrial Policy 2025-30 capital subsidy **exclude Bengaluru Urban**, and the instrument sits on an extension order whose duration is internally contradictory and nominally expired on 6 September 2026.

**Two practical moves follow.** First, siting the plant in a Zone 1/2 taluk rather than Bengaluru Urban roughly doubles the Karnataka package. Second, **Karnataka's real advantage is not its subsidy schedule — it is that Applied Materials (₹4,851 cr) and Lam Research (₹15,901 cr, including ₹9,111 cr of silicon component manufacturing) are building in Karnataka.** For a supplier of machined parts, chambers, gas hardware or sub-assemblies, proximity to those two buyers is worth more than any state's percentage points.

*(State research: notified entitlements only. **No state in this set publishes actual disbursed rupees.** Karnataka's historic ~₹5 cr under the prior ESDM policy and ₹21 cr from KITVEN are the only documented disbursements found anywhere.)*

### 6.6 HS codes that do NOT exist — flagged

**There is no India HS line isolating lead frames, bonding wire, mould compound, IC substrates, ultra-high-purity gases, or semiconductor photoresists.**
- IC substrates fall inside HS 8534 and cannot be separated.
- Mould compound proxies to HS 390730 epoxide resins (USD 281.71 m) — overwhelmingly non-semiconductor.
- Bonding wire: the gold proxy HS 71081300 shows USD 270.37 m in FY2024-25 then **zero** in FY2025-26 because the code was dropped or re-allocated.
- Lead frames: HS 8548 is only USD 5.19 m, clearly not capturing them.
- Photoresist: HS 3707 totals just **USD 23.49 m** for the entire chapter — too small and too generic. Either India genuinely imports almost no wafer photoresist (consistent with having no operating logic fab), or it is buried in HS 3824.
- UHP gases hide inside 2804 / 2811 / 2812 / 2853 / 3824.

**Any figure quoted anywhere for India's imports of these specific items is a market-research estimate, not customs data. Treat accordingly.**

### 6.7 Three things that jump out

1. **HS 848610 went from USD 6.91 m to USD 252.70 m in one year — 36× — and 96% came from Malaysia and Singapore.** That is Tata, Micron and CG Power tooling up, bought through SE-Asian channels.
2. **The gap between the government's materials wish-list and what actually crosses customs is enormous.** Semicon 2.0 Pillar 2 names wafers, photomasks, photoresists, substrates, chemicals and gases — but India today imports only USD 392 m of wafers, ~USD 45 m of rare gases, ~USD 24 m of photo-chemicals and essentially zero electronic-grade polysilicon. **The market to substitute does not exist yet; it arrives with the fabs in 2027–29.** The ₹50 crore threshold in Category 4(b) is the policy's deliberate invitation to get in before it does.
3. **Spares and consumables are where the recurring money already is, and they are China-dominated today.** HS 848690: China supplies USD 46.86 m of USD 90.43 m.

## 7. CANDIDATE MANUFACTURING OPPORTUNITIES

**Ranking basis (per client instruction):** total India market size · growth to 2030 · strength of the notified government scheme or deadline forcing the purchase · how much of the product is genuinely bought and physically fitted in India rather than arriving pre-installed inside imported equipment.

**Existing Indian producers are NOT a negative.** They are listed under "Who already does this in India" as market intelligence, and their presence is read as **positive evidence that the product is genuinely purchased in India by Indian buyers**. Competition affects a rank only where it demonstrably changes the economics — flagged explicitly in the one case where it does (#9).

---

### #1 — Cleanroom systems and fab/OSAT facility hook-up (MEP)

**What it is in one line:** the Class 1–1000 cleanroom shell and everything that makes it work — wall and ceiling systems, FFUs and laminar-flow hardware, vibration-isolated flooring, HVAC and humidity control, air curtains, and the mechanical/electrical/plumbing hook-up that ties tools to utilities.

**Who buys it in India:** every one of the 12 ISM projects plus the ~106 ECMS plants. Directly: Tata Electronics (Dholera, Jagiroad), Micron, CG Semi, Kaynes Semicon, HCL-Foxconn, SiCSem, 3D Glass Solutions, Suchi, Crystal Matrix.

**India market now → 2030:** **derived.** Cleanroom construction is **15–20% of fab capex**; applied to the approved ₹1.64 lakh cr ISM pipeline that is **₹24,600–32,800 crore** of one-time scope, spending out across roughly 2026–2030. Add ECMS's ~₹69,548 cr of approved plant investment and the addressable cleanroom/controlled-environment scope rises materially further. No published India-specific market figure found — **this is my derivation from the published capex split.**

**How much is imported:** low for the built scope. A cleanroom is erected on site in India; the imported content is specific hardware (FFUs, high-end filtration, some control systems). This is the product category with the **highest genuine India-fitted share** in this report.

**Government pull: VERY STRONG.** Cleanrooms are named explicitly inside the Semicon 2.0 eligible-capex definition (Guidelines para 2.7) and inside Category 4's scope, so the government co-pays **30–40%** of the customer's spend. The same spend is eligible under ECMS Segment D.

**Who already does this in India:** a mature Indian cleanroom-contracting industry exists serving pharma and biotech, which is the natural adjacency — pharma cleanrooms and semiconductor cleanrooms share most of the supply chain. **I could not verify a specific list of named semiconductor-qualified Indian cleanroom contractors from primary sources and have not guessed.**

### #2 — Ultra-high-purity gas and chemical delivery systems

**What it is in one line:** gas cabinets, valve manifold boxes and panels, orbital-welded electropolished stainless tubing, high-purity valves, regulators, filters, purifiers and the chemical distribution skids that carry process gases and wet chemicals from the bulk yard to the tool.

**Who buys it in India:** fabs and OSATs directly, and the industrial-gas majors building the supply — **Linde** and **Air Liquide** are establishing dedicated purification plants adjacent to the **Dholera and Sanand** clusters, and **Inox Air Products** signed an MoU with Tata Electronics at SEMICON India 2026.

**India market now → 2030:** **derived.** "Utilities and infrastructure" is **10–15% of fab capex** → **₹16,400–24,600 crore** across the approved ISM pipeline, of which gas and chemical delivery is a major slice alongside ultra-pure water and HVAC. A fab needs **150–500 distinct chemicals and gases**, each requiring its own delivery path. **No published India-specific market size found.**

**How much is imported:** high for the precision hardware (valves, regulators, mass-flow controllers, purifiers), lower for the fabrication and installation of the tubing runs and skids. The gazette explicitly names "storage facilities for chemicals, gases, and their distribution system" as eligible capex — meaning it is bought as part of the plant, in India.

**Government pull: VERY STRONG.** Named in eligible capex under Semicon 2.0 (30–40% co-paid). "High purity gas piping" and "special valves" are explicitly identified as **MSME localisation targets** by the equipment-market analysis. Also squarely inside ECMS Segment D.

**Who already does this in India:** Linde India, Air Liquide India, Inox Air Products (all gas suppliers building adjacent capacity — they are buyers of delivery hardware as much as competitors). **Positive signal: these companies are physically building in Gujarat, which proves the demand is fitted in India.**

### #3 — Precision machined parts, vacuum chambers and sub-assemblies for semiconductor capital equipment

**What it is in one line:** the heavy and precision metalwork inside a semiconductor tool — vacuum chambers, chamber liners, flanges, frames, weldments, precision-machined aluminium and stainless parts, and the sub-assemblies that equipment builders bolt together.

**Who buys it in India — and this is the Bengaluru point:** **Applied Materials** and **Lam Research**, both of which are building **in Karnataka**. Karnataka's Cabinet sub-committee cleared **Applied Materials ₹4,851 cr** (Bengaluru R&D fab / Innovation Centre, 140 acres allotted at Bengaluru Signature Business Park near the airport in May 2026) and **Lam Research ₹15,901 cr — of which ₹9,111 cr is explicitly "silicon component manufacturing"** (29 May 2025). Nationally, Applied Materials committed **USD 5 bn over a decade** to expand **India-based supply-chain capacity tenfold by 2035** plus a 140-acre research park; Lam committed ~**₹10,000 crore** to its **first silicon-component manufacturing facility in India**. Also **BESI** and **L&T Semiconductors** (Tata MoUs), and **Bharat Semi Systems** (₹2,342 cr, Mysuru).

**The two global equipment makers most likely to buy Indian machined parts are building their Indian plants in the client's own state.** That is the strongest single locational argument in this report.

**India market now → 2030:** **official customs data.** India imported **USD 1,383.71 m (₹12,277 cr) of HS 8486 semiconductor/FPD manufacturing machines in FY2025-26**, up **29.4%** — and within that, **HS 848690 "parts and accessories" doubled to USD 90.43 m (+135%)**. That parts line is the directly addressable, recurring, repeat-order market **today**. Market-research forecasts for the whole India equipment market range **USD 2.6 bn → 4.9 bn by 2032** (P&S) or **USD 3.81 bn → 5.65 bn by 2030** (Bisinfotech) — **both are 2–4× larger than the observable customs lines, so treat them as aspirational and use the TRADESTAT figures.**

**How much is imported:** **>85% of equipment used in India is imported.** The revealing detail: **China supplies 52% of the HS 848690 parts and accessories line (USD 46.86 m of USD 90.43 m)**. That is a live, China-dominated, today-sized repeat import to displace — far more attainable than front-end tools. Overall, **China + Malaysia + Singapore supply 87% of HS 8486**, while the Netherlands, USA and Japan together supply barely USD 42 m.

**Two policy statements that bear directly on this.** MeitY Secretary S. Krishnan (30 Mar 2026): opportunities remain open "particularly in **capital equipment and upstream supply chains**." Minister Vaishnaw, same release: called for "**coordinated buyer-seller arrangements, suggesting possible preference for indigenous capital equipment manufacturers**." ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2247040))

**Government pull: VERY STRONG AND UNIQUELY TARGETED.** This is the **exact** category the Semicon 2.0 PLI was written for. An Indian equipment builder claiming the PLI gets **10/8/6/4/2% of the BoM value it buys from Indian manufacturers**, for 5 years from **FY2028-29**, capped at 50% of eligible capex — and must file **Annexure-2 declarations naming its Indian suppliers**. "Heavy steel vacuum chambers" is named as an MSME target. Critically, **"domestic sourcing" permits imported raw material** — the client can import specialty alloy and still qualify the part.

**Who already does this in India:** India has a deep precision-engineering base in aerospace, defence and energy that is the natural adjacency, much of it around Bengaluru and Hyderabad. **I did not find primary-source confirmation of which Indian firms are already qualified as semicap suppliers and have deliberately not guessed names.** The absence of a visible incumbent here is a gap in my research, not evidence that none exist.

### #4 — Electronic-grade and ultra-high-purity chemicals and gases

**What it is in one line:** the consumable chemistry a fab and OSAT burns continuously — process and dopant gases at 5N–6N purity, wet chemicals (sulfuric-peroxide, ammonia-peroxide, HF), CMP slurries, etchants and photoresists.

**Who buys it in India:** every operating and under-construction fab and OSAT, forever — this is **recurring revenue**, unlike the capex categories above.

**India market now → 2030:** market research puts India's semiconductor **materials** market at **USD 5.2–5.3 bn (2025) → USD 7.7 bn (2032) / USD 8.3 bn (2034)**. **But official customs data tells a very different and more useful story:** India imported only **USD 392.50 m of silicon wafers (HS 3818, +143.7%, 97.6% from China)**, **USD 44.82 m of rare gases**, **USD 23.49 m of photographic chemicals (HS 3707)** and **essentially zero electronic-grade polysilicon (USD 40,000)** in FY2025-26. **The materials market to substitute does not exist at scale yet — it arrives with the fabs in 2027–29.** That is a timing argument for entering early, not against entering.

**How much is imported:** **85%** specialty-chemical import dependence for India's new facilities. Contamination tolerances run to **parts-per-billion or -trillion** — the barrier is qualification, not volume.

**One customs finding that should reshape the plan:** India already imports almost **no bulk hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen or argon** — under USD 3 m combined — because **bulk gas production is already localised**. **The opportunity is therefore in purity, blending, cylinder/ISO-container handling and delivery, and in specialty gases (HS 2812 non-metal halides doubled to USD 14.50 m) — not in bulk gas generation.**

**Government pull: VERY STRONG — AND THIS IS THE CHEAPEST DOOR INTO SEMICON 2.0.** Named in the gazette's own Category 4(b) target list: "Wafer, Photo mask, Photoresist, **Substrate**, chemicals, gases, materials, glass for display fab" at **30% of capex**. **Category 4(b)'s threshold is only ₹50 crore of capital investment and ₹20 crore of prior revenue** — one-sixth the ₹300 cr / ₹120 cr required for the equipment route (§1.3a). Odisha adds **10% on top for raw materials/components**; Gujarat adds a standalone 20%. Analysts identify **process gases, wet chemicals, slurries and etchants** as the **first priority** for localisation because they are high-volume, constantly consumed and single-sourced today.

**Who already does this in India:** **Fujifilm** (₹800 cr, Dholera, production FY2028), **JSR** (Tata MoU), **Linde**, **Air Liquide**, **Inox Air Products**, **Stallion India Fluorochemicals** (commissioned a 1,200 MT Khalapur plant, Sep 2026). **Strong positive signal — global materials majors are committing Indian plants, which confirms Indian buyers exist.**

### #5 — Multi-layer PCB, and HDI / MSAP / flexible PCB

**What it is in one line:** the printed circuit boards themselves — 6-layer and 8-layer rigid multilayer, and the higher-density HDI, modified semi-additive and flexible variants.

**Who buys it in India:** the entire Indian electronics assembly base — over 300 mobile manufacturing units, IT hardware, automotive, industrial, defence. Named ECMS approved applicants already making multi-layer PCBs.

**India market now → 2030:** **official customs data — India imported USD 1,676.53 m of printed circuits (HS 8534) in FY2025-26**, up 11.1%, of which roughly three-quarters comes from China. Note HS 8534 is **bare boards and IC substrates only** — populated boards (PCBA) sit in HS 8517/8529/8473 and are far larger. ECMS projects **₹10,34,751 crore of production** across all segments over six years.

**How much is imported:** the overwhelming majority, but **this is the one category with a published localisation trajectory**: PCB projects already approved under ECMS will meet **27% of total domestic demand**, and approved **copper-clad laminate and polypropylene film projects will meet 100% of domestic demand**. Copper-clad laminate is **~30% of PCB manufacturing BoM**. Domestic value addition in mobile phones is **18–20%** against a **35–40% government target**.

**Government pull: STRONG, but the door is shut for now.** Multi-layer PCB carries the **highest turnover-linked rate in ECMS — 10/8/7/6/5/5% for 8-layer** — plus **+1% for domestically sourced laminate**, on only **₹50 crore** of investment. HDI/MSAP/flex gets **8/7/7/6/5/4% plus 25% capex** but needs **₹1,000 crore**. **However: Segments B and C closed on 30 September 2025 and no fresh window is notified.** Entry today depends on a reopening, which the notification permits but has not scheduled.

**Who already does this in India:** **Dixon Technologies, Syrma SGS, Foxconn, Hindalco** are among named ECMS approvals; **AT&S** operates in India. **This is the strongest possible positive signal — a dense field of Indian producers proves Indian buyers are placing real PCB orders in India rather than importing finished boards.**

### #6 — Capital goods for electronics manufacturing, and their sub-assemblies

**What it is in one line:** the machines that make electronics — placement, dispensing, inspection, test, handling and automation equipment, plus the sub-assemblies and components that go inside them.

**Who buys it in India:** the ~106 ECMS-approved plants (38 already operational, 16 in advanced construction as of Aug 2026), the 12 ISM projects, and the 300+ mobile manufacturing units.

**India market now → 2030:** **derived.** ECMS-approved investment alone is **~₹69,548 crore**, a large fraction of which is plant and machinery. India's semiconductor equipment market is **USD 3.81 bn → 5.65 bn (2030)**; the broader electronics capital-goods market is larger again but **I found no published India figure.**

**How much is imported:** the great majority. **>85%** for semiconductor tools specifically.

**Government pull: STRONG, AND THIS IS THE ONLY OPEN DOOR WITH A LOW THRESHOLD.** **ECMS Segment D-11 covers "capital goods used in electronics manufacturing including their sub-assemblies and components" at 25% of capex on a minimum investment of just ₹10 crore, and the window is OPEN until 30 April 2027.** Fiscal support covers investment made within 5 years of acknowledgement; the only performance trigger is commencement of commercial production (plus 5 of the 25 points tied to jobs at 10–20 persons per crore). **No other scheme in this report combines an open window, a ₹10 crore entry, and a 25% capex grant.**

**Who already does this in India — now named.** MeitY's tranche 4 (30 Mar 2026) approved capital equipment "with special emphasis" to six firms: **Titan Engineering & Automation, NeST Advanced Technology Development Centre, ASM Technologies, Indo-MIM, Bharat FIH and IFB Industries.** **Two of the six — ASM Technologies and Indo-MIM — are Bengaluru companies, and Indo-MIM is a precision metal-injection-moulding manufacturer.** The government has therefore already funded a Bengaluru precision-parts maker under exactly this route. **Strong positive signal on every count: the segment is real, the money is flowing, and the precedent is local.**

### #7 — IC substrates, advanced substrates and lead frames

**What it is in one line:** the carrier the chip is mounted on — organic laminate and build-up IC substrates, advanced 2.5D/3D substrates, glass substrates, and stamped/etched metal lead frames.

**Who buys it in India:** every OSAT. Kaynes Semicon explicitly makes "wire-bond and **substrate-based** packages"; CG Semi makes **FC-BGA**, which is substrate-intensive; Micron, Suchi, ASIP and TSAT all consume substrates and lead frames continuously.

**India market now → 2030:** **not published.** Derivable from packaging volume: the approved OSAT pipeline alone is **48 m chips/day (TSAT) + 15.07 m/day (CG Semi) + 14 m/week (Micron) + 6.33 m/day (Kaynes) + 1,033 m/yr (Suchi) + 96 m/yr (ASIP) + 96 m/yr (SiCSem) + 50 m/yr (3D Glass)**. Every one of those units needs a substrate or lead frame. **This is the largest recurring-consumable volume in the entire Indian semiconductor build-out.**

**How much is imported:** effectively all of it — **but it cannot be measured, because India's tariff has no HS line for IC substrates or lead frames.** Substrates fall inside HS 8534 (USD 1,676.53 m total, not separable); lead frames are not captured by HS 8548 (only USD 5.19 m). **Any published figure for India's substrate or lead-frame imports is a market-research estimate, not customs data.** I found no evidence of an Indian producer of either at scale, and could not verify the "~0% local" figure from a published source — though nothing contradicts it.

**Government pull: STRONG.** "Substrate" is named explicitly in the gazette's Category 4(b) materials list (**30% capex**), and **"Advanced Substrate" is named inside Category 8 advanced packaging at 35% capex** — an unusually high rate. **3D Glass Solutions' ₹1,943 cr Bhubaneswar plant for glass substrates is already under construction**, which proves the government will fund substrate capacity.

**Who already does this in India:** **3D Glass Solutions / HIPS** (glass substrates, Bhubaneswar, groundbreaking Apr 2026) is the only approved substrate project found. For organic substrates and lead frames, **no Indian incumbent identified.** Under the client's ranking rule this is neither a plus nor a minus — but it does mean the client would be qualifying a supply chain from a standing start, which lengthens the sales cycle.

### #8 — Ultra-pure water and process-water systems, and industrial effluent treatment

**What it is in one line:** the UPW plant, polishing loops, distribution ring and the chemical-waste neutralisation and recycling systems that a fab cannot legally or physically run without.

**Who buys it in India:** every fab and OSAT. Dholera already has **desalination and tertiary water treatment plants with a 90% recycling rate** built into the cluster.

**India market now → 2030:** **derived.** Part of the **10–15% "utilities and infrastructure"** share → **₹16,400–24,600 crore** across the approved pipeline, shared with gas/chemical delivery and HVAC. Scale driver: a single fab consumes **up to 10 million gallons/day** of ultra-pure water.

**How much is imported:** low for the built plant; moderate for the high-end polishing and metrology hardware. Water systems are designed, fabricated and commissioned locally.

**Government pull: STRONG.** "Effluent treatment plants" and "water & power supply" are named inside the Semicon 2.0 eligible-capex definition, so 30–40% is co-paid. Also inside ECMS Segment D.

**Who already does this in India:** India has a large established water-treatment engineering industry (the same firms that serve refineries, pharma and power). **Strong positive signal — this competence already exists in India and is already being bought in India**; the semiconductor-grade step-up is a purity and qualification upgrade, not a new industry.

### #9 — Enclosures and precision sheet metal for electronics

**What it is in one line:** the metal and composite housings for mobile, IT hardware and related devices, and the precision sheet-metal fabrication behind them.

**Who buys it in India:** the 300+ mobile manufacturing units and the IT hardware assemblers; named ECMS approvals already cover "enclosures for mobile and IT hardware products."

**India market now → 2030:** large but **not separately published**. Tied directly to India's **₹5.45 lakh crore (2024-25)** mobile production and **₹11.3 lakh crore** total electronics production, heading for **USD 500 bn by 2030-31**.

**How much is imported:** falling fast for finished enclosures — they are bulky and freight-sensitive — **but the input is not. PIB records that aluminium extrusion for mobile enclosures is "currently being 100% imported"**, with Hindalco approved under ECMS to localise it. **The sharper opportunity here is the extrusion and the input material, not the finished enclosure.**

**Government pull: MODERATE — and the window is closed.** ECMS Segment B-7 pays **7/6/5/4/4/3%** turnover-linked, but requires **₹500 crore** cumulative investment, and **Segment B closed 30 September 2025**.

**ECONOMICS CAVEAT — the one case where competition genuinely changes the ranking, per the client's rule 4.** Enclosures and precision sheet metal are the closest thing in this list to a true commodity: the product is differentiated mainly by price per unit against a known metal input cost, buyers are a handful of very large assemblers with strong purchasing power, and the ECMS incentive itself declines from 7% to 3% over six years — which is the government's own signal that it expects margins to compress as volume localises. **I did not find published margin data for Indian enclosure makers and cannot evidence a specific margin figure**, so I am flagging this as a structural-economics concern rather than a proven one. It is ranked below the categories above on that basis and on the closed window — **not** because Indian competitors exist.

**Who already does this in India:** a dense and capable field, including firms among the ECMS approved list. **Positive signal on demand reality; the caveat above is about price structure, not about market access.**

### #10 — Test sockets, burn-in boards, probe cards and test-handling hardware

**What it is in one line:** the consumable and semi-consumable interface hardware that sits between a chip and a tester — sockets, burn-in boards, probe cards, change kits and handler tooling, all of which wear out and are replaced on a cycle.

**Who buys it in India:** every OSAT, continuously. Test is a named part of every approved OSAT's scope, and Semicon 2.0 creates a distinct eligible sub-vertical for **"Semiconductor Test and characterization Facilities"** (Category 4c). **HCLTech has set up a semiconductor testing facility in Bengaluru**, and **Keysight** showcased semiconductor test solutions at SEMICON India 2026.

**India market now → 2030:** **NOT FOUND, and there is no HS line for it. I could not locate any India-specific market size for test sockets, probe cards or burn-in boards, and I will not invent one.** The volume logic is strong — India's approved packaging capacity is tens of millions of units per day and every unit is tested — and **globally, SEMI recorded test equipment billings up 55% year-on-year in 2025 and forecasts USD 15.3 bn in 2026, the fastest-growing equipment segment.** But the India rupee figure is unverified.

**How much is imported:** essentially all of it, from US, Japanese, Korean and Taiwanese specialists.

**Government pull: MODERATE-TO-STRONG, with the lowest technical bar in the entire scheme.** Test and characterisation facilities get **30% of capex** under Category 4(c) at a threshold of **₹100 crore capex / ₹40 crore revenue**, and Category 4(c) is the **one sub-vertical the gazette explicitly exempts from the "own or possess licensed technologies" requirement** (para 3.4.3). Every other category in Semicon 2.0 requires the applicant to own or license production-grade technology; this one does not. Test consumables also qualify as BoM under the equipment-maker PLI. **Note this is support for a test *facility*, not for manufacturing sockets — the socket-manufacturing route runs through ECMS Segment D instead.**

**Who already does this in India:** **HCLTech** (Bengaluru testing facility) on the services side; **Keysight** on the equipment side. **No Indian test-socket or probe-card manufacturer identified.** Ranked tenth on unverified market size, not on competition.

## 10. The eight numbers that matter most

1. **₹1,27,500 crore** Semicon 2.0 outlay [NOTIFIED 31 Aug 2026] — but only **₹1,000 crore** actually budgeted for ISM 2.0 in FY2026-27.
2. **10% / 8% / 6% / 4% / 2%** of domestically-sourced BoM value, 5 years from **FY2028-29**, capped at 50% of eligible capex — paid to Indian semiconductor **equipment builders** on what they buy from Indian component makers. Confirmed verbatim from the gazette.
3. **ECMS Segment D: ₹10 crore minimum investment, 25% of capex, window OPEN until 30 April 2027** — the only accessible door currently open. Segments A/B/C closed 30 Sep 2025.
4. **USD 30.32 bn** of integrated circuits imported in FY2025-26 (₹2,68,235 cr) — **3.91% of India's entire merchandise import bill**. Total electronic components ~**USD 61.1 bn**.
5. **USD 1,383.71 m** of semiconductor manufacturing equipment imported FY2025-26 (+29.4%), of which **parts and accessories USD 90.43 m (+135%, China 52%)** — the live, recurring, displaceable line.

7. **Cleanroom 15–20% and utilities 10–15% of fab capex** → **₹41,000–57,000 crore** of India-fitted scope inside the approved ₹1.64 lakh crore ISM pipeline.
