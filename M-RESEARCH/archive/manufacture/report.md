# Choosing a Manufacturing Business in India

## 1. The investment decision

**For a first manufacturing venture around Bengaluru with ₹1–30 crore available, I would choose a specialised industrial electrical-components business. My initial product would be custom, insulated formed-conductor kits for UPS systems, motor drives and power-conversion equipment.** These are shaped copper or aluminium conductors, insulation and mounting hardware manufactured to an equipment maker's approved drawings. A busbar is a rigid conductor that carries electrical current inside such equipment.

I would begin with one closely related product family, lease industrial space, outsource plating and selected specialist processes, and build competence in repeatable production, inspection and delivery. I would fund customer development and prototypes first, then establish a focused production operation with an indicative ₹5–8 crore envelope if orders and economics justify it. Having ₹30 crore available would allow patient expansion and cash reserves; it would not persuade me to install ₹30 crore of machinery immediately.

**Industrial filtration products are a close second and should be tested alongside electrical components before committing to the plant.** Filter housings and selected replacement elements could be the better business if paid trials reveal recurring demand, demonstrable operating savings and stronger margins. Precision engineering for aerospace and industrial equipment ranks next, becoming more attractive with an experienced technical partner and an existing customer programme.

This judgment balances durable profits, growth and eventual exports. It assumes no existing proprietary technology, approved supplier status or committed customer contracts. Those advantages can change the ranking substantially. It also assumes the budget must fund equipment, establishment costs, development losses and working capital, rather than equipment alone.

The evidence for electrical demand is tangible. ABB India reported Q2 2026 orders of ₹4,363 crore, up 50% year on year, and an order backlog of ₹11,898 crore. Its March 2026 Karnataka expansion establishes a relevant local manufacturing ecosystem. These company disclosures support the demand thesis; they do not establish an order opportunity for a new supplier.[^1][^2]

The most important qualification is commercial: **this is the business I would investigate first, not a recommendation to purchase equipment before customers validate the product.** Copper-heavy sales can generate substantial turnover with disappointing profits and large financing needs. The analysis below makes that risk explicit.

### Scope and confidence

The evidence cutoff is 11 September 2026. Historical observations, official estimates, company plans and analytical judgments are distinguished throughout. The comparison covers the major investable manufacturing families relevant to this location and budget; it cannot establish demand for every possible product. Confidence is high that capital-intensive commodity cells, generic solar modules and undifferentiated bulk APIs are poor default choices for this entrant profile. Confidence is moderate in the electrical-over-filtration ranking until actual quotations and paid pilots are available.

The decision gives greatest weight to attainable customers and cash generation, followed by repeat demand, defensibility, technical and regulatory feasibility, Bengaluru fit and export options. National market growth is useful evidence, but a large market does not tell us which customers will approve a new factory or what price they will pay.

## 2. Indian demand and current international conditions

India offers several overlapping sources of manufacturing demand: replacement of installed equipment, new industrial investment, electrification, healthcare, urban infrastructure and export sourcing. Their timing differs. A supplier serving several of these can avoid making its entire business dependent on one vehicle platform, a single data-centre project or one government tender.

| Evidence available at the cutoff | What it means for the factory decision |
|---|---|
| MoSPI estimates real GDP growth of 7.8% for April–June 2026, using the new 2022–23 base series.[^13] | Domestic activity supports prospecting. Do not translate GDP growth into a startup sales forecast or mix old and new statistical series uncritically. |
| July CPI inflation was 4.45%; RBI held the repo rate at 5.25% on 5 August 2026.[^14][^15] | Use actual bank quotations. An SME's borrowing cost, fees and collateral requirements are different from the policy rate. |
| FY2026–27 public capital expenditure is budgeted at ₹12.2 lakh crore.[^17] | Distinguish budgeted expenditure from executed projects. Supplying established OEMs can be more accessible than bidding directly for public contracts. |
| Reuters reported the rupee closing at ₹95.44/USD on 10 September amid oil and Middle East concerns.[^16] | Imported machines, connectors, resins and chemicals need currency protection in pricing and cash planning. This is a dated observation, not a forecast. |
| ECMS had 106 approved projects and 38 operational plants in the government's 17 August update.[^38] | Electronics localisation creates both potential customers and more competition. Approvals and projected investment are not realised sales. |

### China: opportunity and continuing dependency

India can win work when a buyer values another qualified geography, shorter delivery, customisation or local service. It cannot assume that Chinese manufacturing is becoming uncompetitive in every product. A dense supplier base, equipment capability and scale can outweigh India's labour-cost advantages. Import substitution is strongest when there is an identifiable procurement problem and the Indian production route has a lasting cost or service advantage.

The dependency often moves upstream: an Indian EV battery pack may still use imported cells; a filter may depend on imported media; electrical equipment may use imported connectors, semiconductors or specialised insulation. IEA's 2026 critical-minerals assessment continues to identify supply concentration and policy restrictions as material risks.[^22] The business must map dependency at the bill-of-material level rather than label all local assembly self-reliant.

### Oil, freight, currency and geopolitics

Higher oil and transport costs can affect plastics, chemicals, packaging and practically every export shipment. Shipping disruption can delay a low-cost component that stops the entire assembly line. A weaker rupee helps only the net foreign-currency revenue exposure: imported inputs, overseas freight, foreign-currency debt and warranty obligations can offset the benefit.

The practical response is to qualify alternative sources, negotiate short quotation validity or adjustment clauses, secure critical materials against actual orders and stress delivery delays. Use bank-supported hedging for committed currency exposure where appropriate. Speculating on commodity inventory or the rupee should not become a hidden second business.

A first factory should survive a six-month customer delay, a 30% sales shortfall, higher imported-input costs and slower collections. These are proposed stress tests, not economic predictions. A model that works only when every favourable macro assumption arrives simultaneously is too fragile.

## 4. EVs: where a first manufacturer can participate

EV adoption creates opportunities in conductors, harnesses, thermal management, power electronics, mechanical structures, testing and charging infrastructure. It also changes the demand for conventional engine and transmission parts. The sensible question is which component capability remains valuable across vehicle platforms and other industrial uses.

IEA reports Indian electric two- and three-wheeler sales growing more than 30% year on year in Q1 2026, while global electric-car sales declined in the same quarter. The different outcomes underline why geography and vehicle segment matter.[^5] India's PM E-DRIVE portal identifies an August 2026 amendment extending electric two-wheeler support to March 2028; support remains category-specific and subject to scheme conditions.[^7]

**I would prefer exposure through diversified component customers.** The first product line should be viable selling to industrial power-equipment makers even if a particular EV company loses market share. Automotive programmes can be pursued later, once their qualification costs, volumes and payment terms are known.

| EV-related route | What must be demonstrated before entry |
|---|---|
| Conductor and interconnect assemblies | Approved drawings; thermal and electrical performance; repeatable joints, insulation and traceability; clear metal pricing. |
| Harnesses | Approved connectors and tooling; crimp validation; continuity tests; production traceability and customer-specific quality requirements. |
| Battery enclosure or cooling components | Sealing, mechanical performance, corrosion and fluid compatibility; clear responsibility for system validation. |
| Battery packs | Reliable cells, safe integration, battery-management capability, degradation data, service network and funded warranty obligations. |
| Charging equipment | Differentiated use case, power-electronics capability, certification path, installation/service economics and customer acquisition. |

### Why I would not start with cell manufacturing

IEA's 2026 battery analysis reports that China produced more than 80% of battery cells in 2025 and global nameplate capacity exceeded 4 TWh.[^6] Cell manufacturing demands process knowledge, expensive equipment, high yields, materials control and substantial scale. A small apparent capacity gap in India is not proof that a ₹30 crore facility can compete against this ecosystem.

Pack assembly is a separate business. Its entry cost can be lower, but field failures, thermal events, cell warranties, storage conditions and customer claims can overwhelm a thin assembly margin. A pack business needs a contracted application and a clear source of value beyond purchasing cells and enclosing them.

### Technology and customer risk

Battery chemistries, pack architectures, motor designs and charging requirements will evolve. Tooling dedicated to one geometry can become obsolete before it earns its cost back. Customer concentration is equally important: winning one large programme can create impressive sales and dangerous dependence at the same time.

The commercial contract should address tooling ownership, engineering changes, minimum order quantities, cancellation, raw-material adjustment and warranty allocation. A nominal volume nomination is different from firm call-off orders. Motherson's established design and manufacturing capabilities illustrate the competitive strength already present in automotive harnesses.[^45]

My preferred initial capability—precise, inspected formed conductors—can participate in EV-related power systems while also serving UPS, drives and industrial equipment. Nevertheless, automotive approval must be earned separately. A successful industrial part is not automatically an approved automotive part.

## 5. AI and data centres: demand, access and competition

AI creates physical demand for power distribution, backup power, cooling, storage, racks and construction. That does not make chips or complete data centres the best manufacturing entry point. The opportunity for this budget is more plausibly in the equipment supply chain, where an existing OEM owns the overall system design and customer relationship.

IEA's April 2026 central projection puts global data-centre electricity consumption at approximately 950 TWh in 2030, compared with 485 TWh in 2025. It also highlights financing sensitivity and equipment bottlenecks.[^3] This is a global forecast, not an Indian market-size estimate or committed procurement. Google's April 2026 groundbreaking for its Visakhapatnam AI hub is a concrete Indian development, but its broad infrastructure commitment should not be treated as a component budget accessible to a Bengaluru SME.[^46]

### The part of the supply chain I would target

Start with the companies manufacturing UPS units, motor drives, power converters and electrical assemblies. Sell them a defined conductor kit with documented inspection and repeatable delivery. Their end customers may include data centres, hospitals, factories, commercial buildings or renewable-energy projects. This approach diversifies demand while keeping the initial manufacturing process coherent.

The proposed process is conventional formed-conductor manufacture and assembly. It does not initially include semiconductor manufacture, advanced laminated high-voltage busbars, complete switchboards, chip cold plates and complete coolant-distribution units. Each of those requires additional engineering, testing, capital or field responsibility.

### Why cooling is attractive but not my first default

Schneider Electric launched a Motivair liquid-cooling factory in Bengaluru in February 2026.[^4] That is evidence of local demand and capability, but it also means substantial competition already exists. A newcomer can investigate approved manifolds, frames or specified pipe assemblies for an established cooling OEM. Competing immediately in complete systems adds controls, leak performance, fluid management, commissioning and service requirements.

NVIDIA's published 800 VDC power architecture also shows that AI infrastructure design is changing.[^50] The implication is to avoid betting the entire factory on one current rack or power configuration. Conventional conductors may remain useful, but each new architecture requires its own drawings and qualification; demand does not transfer automatically.

### The customer-access problem

Large OEMs usually need more than a sales pitch about local manufacturing. They need evidence that another supplier reduces delivery risk, solves an engineering problem, offers economical small batches or supports a new programme. Existing procurement relationships, approved materials and audited processes are barriers to entry.

ABB's Karnataka investments establish a relevant local ecosystem.[^2] Mersen's busbar offering and Bommasandra presence demonstrate that sophisticated local competition exists; Rittal also has a Doddaballapura location.[^28][^29][^44] The defensible opening must be narrower than “manufacture busbars”: it might be a troublesome part family, shorter prototype cycles or repeat quality at low volumes. None of those openings has yet been confirmed by a buyer.

In the factory itself, AI can assist quotation preparation, drawing/document retrieval, production scheduling, inspection triage and maintenance analysis. The priority is accurate records and disciplined processes. Human engineering approval, calibrated measurements and accountable quality release remain essential; purchasing an AI system will not compensate for an unstable process.

## 6. Pharma APIs: strategically important, commercially demanding

The API opportunity is real, but the investment unit is a specific molecule, synthesis route, scale and customer. “India imports APIs” is too broad to support a plant decision. A molecule may have concentrated imports and still be unattractive because its price, process yield, effluent cost or buyer approval requirements make domestic production uneconomic.

Official FY2024–25 data show ₹36,124 crore of imports in the reported API/KSM/drug-intermediate basket, with ₹27,032 crore from China. The calculated Chinese share is 74.8% by import value. This is not the percentage of all ingredients consumed by India's pharmaceutical industry.[^8]

A more cautionary indicator is commercialisation. In August 2026 the government reported ₹5,070.45 crore invested under the bulk-drug PLI scheme through March, and capacity established for 28 API/KSM/intermediate products. Ten had not reached commercial production.[^9] This does not prove those projects are unprofitable, but it shows that installed capacity and subsidy approval do not automatically create sales.

### What an API project must include

The process cost must cover feedstocks, achieved yield, solvent recovery, energy, water, impurities, batch rejection, cleaning and waste disposal. The plant budget must also include analytical instruments, quality staff, validated utilities, suitable containment, fire protection, environmental systems, inventory and the time before customers approve supply. A reactor quotation is not a complete factory quotation.

FDA's Q7 guidance describes extensive quality, documentation, qualification and change-control expectations for API manufacturing.[^10] India's smaller-manufacturer transition extension for revised Schedule M ended in December 2025 under the cited notification; it is not a planning exemption for a new 2026 factory.[^11] Environmental permissions depend on the actual products and process, with pharmaceutical residue and waste management requiring specific attention.[^12]

At ₹15–30 crore, a tightly focused intermediate or specialised API project may be feasible under favourable conditions. Integrated fermentation, broad multi-product capability or highly demanding sterile/high-potency operations are different propositions. Bengaluru's scientific talent helps, but water, effluent, permitted land use and feedstock logistics must be assessed at the chosen site.

### The conditions under which I would choose APIs

- An experienced process-chemistry and quality leader is committed to the business.
- A particular route has demonstrated yield, impurity control and repeatability in pilot batches.
- At least two credible customers have defined evaluation, approval and commercial requirements.
- The economics survive lower selling prices and higher input costs without relying on temporary protection.
- The site, utilities, environmental solution and operating runway are fully costed.

Government trade measures on selected antibiotic inputs demonstrate that policy can respond to price pressure, but product-specific protection is not permanent protection for the entire API sector.[^48] US pharmaceutical policy also distinguishes product categories and has changed during 2026; the April action on patented products must not be generalised to all generic APIs.[^41]

My preferred API route would begin with process development and paid customer evaluation using an appropriately licensed contract manufacturer. Only after confirming quality, economics and customer acceptance would I own the production assets. Outsourcing reduces initial asset exposure; it does not remove quality accountability or dependence on the contractor.

For an entrant without this technical and customer advantage, I would sell well-documented equipment or components into pharma first. That captures healthcare-related demand with a more manageable initial operating scope.

## 7. The strongest alternatives

### Industrial filtration and hygienic fluid handling

This is the closest challenger to electrical assemblies. A narrow product family—such as specified stainless filter housings and replacement elements for an identified industrial application—can generate equipment sales followed by recurring replacement demand. The strongest opportunity is measurable performance: longer usable filter life, less downtime, reliable availability or lower total operating cost.

Bengaluru already has sophisticated equipment manufacturing. Sartorius describes local production of stainless components, bioreactor equipment and filtration products; its 2026 water-reuse account provides a practical example of industrial water investment.[^30][^31] These sources establish an ecosystem and demanding competition, rather than an unoccupied market. Praj's product catalogue similarly illustrates the breadth of established sanitary-system capability.[^32]

I would start in one non-sterile industrial use case, with field trials, pressure/leak testing, material traceability and service capability. Sterile drug-contact filtration, membrane chemistry and single-use bioprocess products should be treated as separate, harder businesses. A supplier using bought-in media must also understand where its own value and margin actually reside.

**Filtration wins the comparison if customers repeatedly buy replacements at attractive contribution, trials show operating savings, and an applications/service leader can support them.** Electrical wins if OEM qualification paths are clearer and metal/credit terms protect cash. Both hypotheses deserve paid validation before a major asset decision.

### Precision engineering and aerospace

Bengaluru's engineering ecosystem makes precision machining compelling, especially for a founder with process expertise and customer access. Start with specific parts and credible machine utilisation, potentially serving industrial equipment alongside aerospace. Special-process outsourcing, metrology and traceability can be as important as the CNC machine itself.

Azad Engineering's FY2025–26 annual report describes 30–48 month qualification cycles for its class of critical components and the working-capital burden of development and ramp-up.[^36] That is evidence about demanding components, not a universal timeline for all machining. It demonstrates why an established supplier's attractive economics cannot be copied simply by buying similar machines. India's defence-export growth is encouraging, but aggregate exports do not establish an accessible small-factory programme.[^37]

At the upper end of the budget, acquiring or partnering with a qualified small engineering business may be better than starting from zero. Evaluate whether customers will remain, which approvals survive ownership/site/process changes, machine condition, true receivables, environmental history and the retention of key people. This is an option to investigate, not an identified acquisition opportunity.

### Medical consumables, food and packaging

Medical and laboratory consumables offer repeat use, but product classification, tooling, clean production, testing and distribution must be understood. Non-sterile does not automatically mean unregulated. CDSCO's FAQ clarifies that an ISO 13485 certificate is not itself a universal manufacturing-licence prerequisite; applicable quality obligations and customer/export certification requirements remain separate questions.[^34]

Food processing can be attractive with a reliable supply advantage and contracted B2B buyers. The government's food-processing update documents increased capacity and exports, while also showing that many firms are already investing.[^35] I would favour a specific ingredient or contract-processing proposition over launching a broad consumer brand with simultaneous manufacturing and marketing risk.

Secondary pharma packaging, labels and protective packaging can work with anchor buyers and traceability requirements. Their recurring demand is attractive, but a generic local converter may have little pricing power. These alternatives move above the leaders only when the founder brings a concrete channel or sourcing advantage.

## 8. The electrical business I would actually build

The opening product family would be **build-to-print insulated formed-conductor kits for industrial UPS, drives and power converters**. Initial variants could include straight, offset and riser busbar sets; specified battery/rectifier links; and low-volume replacement or engineering-change kits. These share a production method and inspection discipline. The customer's drawing and qualification plan determine the permitted application.

### Manufacturing scope

Bring selected cutting, punching/drilling, bending, deburring, insulation fitting, hardware assembly and final inspection under control. Use qualified subcontractors for plating, specialist cutting and tests that do not yet justify ownership. Purchase suitable conductor stock rather than beginning with metal refining or extrusion. Record incoming material, process steps, outsourced batches and final inspection against each order.

Initial quality capability should address dimensions, burrs and edge condition, plating and contact surfaces, insulation integrity, torque where applicable and traceability. Electrical/thermal tests must match the agreed product and application. Specialist laboratories can be more economical than building a comprehensive test facility before there is sufficient work.

Complete LV panels involve system-level responsibilities beyond supplying a component. IEC 61439-1 and the applicable product part address assembly construction and verification; using certified breakers does not alone establish compliance of the completed panel.[^26] BIS requirements and implementation dates must be checked for the exact item, including whether it is an assembly or component.[^27]

### How the factory would earn its margin

The intended value is reliable conversion and assembly: economical small batches, fast drawing revisions, traceable quality and consistent delivery. Buyers must recognise that value in price or in terms that improve capital efficiency. If procurement treats the output solely as kilograms of copper plus the cheapest conversion charge, the investment case weakens considerably.

Track contribution by job, per production hour and per kilogram of conductor—not just sales turnover. Reconcile bought metal, finished-part weight, process scrap, rejected pieces and scrap sale proceeds. Document ownership of customer-funded material and tooling. A rising copper price can inflate revenue while leaving conversion earnings unchanged.

Quote material and conversion elements separately where customers accept this. Agree metal-price reference, adjustment timing, scrap assumptions, tooling amortisation, minimum batches and engineering-change fees. Customer-owned material can reduce cash exposure, but contractual ownership, storage and loss responsibilities must be clear. This is a negotiation objective, not an assumed benefit.

### Expansion sequence

The first expansion should add capacity or automation to a proven bottleneck. Matching mechanical mounting kits may be sensible if repeat customers request them. Advanced laminated busbars, automotive interconnects or cooling components should follow only after their engineering and qualification costs are justified. Mersen's product range illustrates that advanced busbars involve substantial materials and design knowledge.[^28]

The organisational priority is an experienced production/quality leader and a salesperson who can work with engineering and procurement teams. A founder should not outsource technical accountability to the machine vendor. Begin with a limited number of parts and excellent records; a large catalogue of unproven capability creates complexity before revenue.

The ability to manufacture many shapes is not, by itself, a moat. The defensible asset is an approved, reliably executed part family and the customer's confidence that switching suppliers would add risk or effort.

## 9. Bengaluru location, people and operating requirements

For a first unit, I would compare a leased industrial building in the Peenya–Nelamangala corridor against suitable buildings around Bommasandra/Jigani and, where relevant, Doddaballapura/Hoskote or Harohalli. This is a search shortlist, not a claim that any particular building has suitable utilities, permission or an attractive current rent. The final choice should follow the first likely customers and the required workforce.

| Location decision | Evidence to obtain before signing |
|---|---|
| Customer access | Actual truck travel time, frequency of deliveries, prototype visits and response expectations. |
| Workforce | Availability and retention cost of operators, quality staff, supervisors and engineering leadership; commute and shift feasibility. |
| Building | Permitted industrial use, access, loading, floor capacity, ventilation, layout, expansion rights and landlord consent. |
| Power | Sanctioned load, connection/upgrade schedule, reliability, demand charges, energy charges and all applicable adjustments. |
| Water and waste | Approved source, process consumption, storage, discharge/waste route and contractor capability. |
| Total cost | Rent and deposits, fit-out, transport, power, maintenance, insurance, recruitment and management time. |

Dry forming and assembly may reduce initial water and effluent complexity compared with chemistry or wet surface treatment, but the classification depends on the actual process. CPCB's revised categorisation distinguishes processes and pollution characteristics; site-specific applicability and Karnataka implementation must be checked.[^43] Outsourcing plating reduces onsite complexity while creating subcontractor-quality and logistics obligations.

### Incentives: useful, but limited by eligibility

Karnataka's April 2026 operative guidelines use their own plant/machinery bands for incentive calculations: micro below ₹1 crore, small ₹1–10 crore and medium ₹10–50 crore. These differ from national MSME classification. Bengaluru Urban and Rural are Zone 3, with relevant Zone 3 financial incentives limited to Rural. General-category small enterprises have capital subsidy of 10% of eligible fixed assets, capped at ₹50 lakh, in Zone 3 Rural; Zone 2 offers 20% capped at ₹1.5 crore and Zone 1 offers 25% capped at ₹2 crore. Conditions, instalments and funding availability apply. Harohalli/Ramanagara are Zone 2. Confirm the precise site, category and eligible expenditure in writing.[^23]

National MSME thresholds effective April 2025 are ₹2.5/10 crore investment/turnover for micro, ₹25/100 crore for small and ₹125/500 crore for medium.[^24] Total project funding is different from plant/equipment investment. The applicable classification should be calculated rather than inferred from the amount of money available.

CGTMSE can support eligible lending up to the scheme's ₹10 crore credit ceiling, subject to lender appraisal and terms. It is a guarantee mechanism, not a grant or automatic financing approval.[^25] The cash model should assume zero unapproved subsidy receipts. Do not buy an unsuitable site to obtain a relatively small incentive.

### Operating compliance and management

Before establishing the plant, map industrial/building permissions, pollution category and consents where applicable, fire and electrical requirements, worker safety, employment registrations, waste obligations and product approvals. Requirements vary with process, equipment, workforce and building. NSWS helps identify approvals; the relevant departments decide them.[^42]

Budget payroll, training, protective equipment, calibration, maintenance, insurance and founder remuneration as real costs. Product liability, business interruption and customer-contract exposure deserve review. Build a simple production and quality system from the first order: controlled drawings, approved suppliers, calibration, inspection, non-conformance, corrective action and lot traceability.

## 10. Capital allocation and financial feasibility

All numbers in this section are **illustrative planning assumptions**, not observed startup margins, machine quotations or revenue forecasts. Prices and product mix must be established through actual requests for quotation. All operating figures exclude GST; project funding must separately cover recoverable-tax timing and any non-recoverable taxes. Land purchase is excluded.

### Illustrative ₹8 crore initial funding envelope

| Use of funds | ₹ crore | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Selected machinery and installation | 1.90 | Forming/machining capacity matched to initial parts, rather than a complete multi-process factory. |
| Tooling, fixtures and quality/test equipment | 0.70 | Repeatability, inspection and basic testing; specialist qualification tests outsourced. |
| Lease deposit, fit-out and utilities | 0.60 | Suitable leased space and required electrical/production setup. |
| Product development and qualification | 0.45 | Engineering, prototypes, samples and external testing. |

| **Total** | **8.00** | Funding requirement includes operating liquidity, not just fixed assets. |

This envelope is a feasibility placeholder. A quotation exercise may show that fewer machines and a larger cash reserve are better. If the maximum available funding is ₹1–3 crore, the appropriate scope is a smaller engineering/assembly or harness operation with extensive subcontracting. The ₹8 crore plan should not be compressed by eliminating its working capital.

At ₹10–20 crore, retain funds for expansion after repeat orders. At ₹20–30 crore, compare scaling the validated line with a carefully diligenced acquisition or partnership. The larger budget does not remove the need for technical capability or customer approval.

### Economics under different commercial outcomes

Contribution means sales less materials, outsourced processes, variable labour/energy, freight, expected scrap/rework and warranty allowance. Fixed operating expense includes salaried staff, rent, maintenance, selling, compliance and founder remuneration. Avoid counting the same labour or maintenance cost twice.

| Illustrative outcome | Annual sales, ₹ cr | Contribution | Fixed cash costs, ₹ cr | EBITDA, ₹ cr |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Weak sales and pricing | 8.0 | 24% | 2.20 | **−0.28** |
| Copper-conversion pricing | 13.5 | 20% | 2.20 | **0.50** |
| Better engineered-product pricing | 13.5 | 28% | 2.20 | **1.58** |
| Strong volume and mix | 18.0 | 30% | 2.60 | **2.80** |

These outcomes are not assigned probabilities. In particular, **28–30% contribution must be proven, not assumed**. The ₹18 crore sales case is not a claimed machine capacity: the production plan must derive feasible output from actual parts, cycle times, shifts, yield and selling prices.

At 28% contribution and ₹2.2 crore fixed cash cost, EBITDA break-even is approximately ₹7.86 crore sales. With assumed annual depreciation of ₹0.35 crore and finance costs of ₹0.40 crore, pre-tax accounting break-even rises to approximately ₹10.54 crore. Finance cost is a separate placeholder pending actual debt amounts, rates and fees. Debt principal, replacement capital expenditure and working-capital growth are additional cash demands.

## 11. Working capital, returns and downside protection

The better-pricing example produces ₹1.58 crore EBITDA, but after illustrative depreciation and finance costs, pre-tax profit is only ₹0.83 crore. Tax and reinvestment reduce cash available further. On the full ₹8 crore funding envelope, illustrative EBIT of ₹1.23 crore represents a 15.4% pre-tax operating return. This conservative denominator includes reserves and setup spending; it is not a forecast ROCE or investor IRR.

That result is not automatically compelling for the operating risk. I would set an investment hurdle around 20% steady-state pre-tax operating return on capital as an internal target, adjusting it to actual financing and risk. With the same ₹8 crore capital, ₹2.2 crore fixed costs, ₹0.35 crore depreciation and 28% contribution, approximately ₹14.8 crore sales would be needed to reach that target. This holds capital constant: extra working capital must fit the reserve or the return must be recalculated. If customers do not support the necessary price, throughput and collection terms, reduce capital intensity or reject the project.

### Working-capital illustration

Assume annual sales of ₹13.5 crore and annual material purchases of ₹8.1 crore. The simplified calculation below includes receivables plus material inventory less material supplier payables. It excludes GST timing, work-in-process conversion costs, other accruals and cash balances; it is a starting estimate, not a complete bank assessment.

| Cash-cycle assumption | Calculation | Funding tied up, ₹ cr |
|---|---|---:|
| Receivables: 60 days | 13.5 × 60 / 365 | 2.22 |
| Material inventory: 45 days | 8.1 × 45 / 365 | 1.00 |
| Supplier credit: 30 days | 8.1 × 30 / 365 | −0.67 |
| **Simplified working capital** | Receivables + inventory − payables | **2.55** |
| Slower cycle: 90 receivable / 60 inventory / 30 payable days | 13.5 × 90 / 365 + 8.1 × 30 / 365 | **3.99** |

The slower cycle needs roughly ₹1.44 crore more before other cash requirements. It exceeds the ₹2.6 crore working-capital provision. A growing business can therefore exhaust liquidity even when the profit and loss account appears healthy. Statutory payment protections and invoice-financing platforms do not eliminate buyer acceptance delays or disputes; Crisil's August 2026 analysis emphasises implementation challenges.[^47]

### Stress tests that matter most

| Shock | Illustrative effect or required test |
|---|---|
| Copper rises 15% without repricing, from 60% of sales | Removes approximately 9 percentage points of contribution: ₹1.215 crore at ₹13.5 crore sales. |
| Collections slow by 30 days | Adds approximately ₹1.11 crore receivables at ₹13.5 crore annual sales. |
| Sales begin six months late | Additional salaries, rent, debt costs and qualification spending must be financed; exact burn comes from the launch budget. |
| Largest customer cuts call-offs | Test order-specific stock obsolescence, unused tooling and the time needed to replace contribution. |
| Rejections or field claims increase | Recalculate lost material, replacement freight, rework, credit notes and potential liability. |

The preferred protections are commercial and operational: smaller committed batches, metal adjustment, deposits for tooling, realistic credit limits, documented acceptance and agreed engineering-change treatment. Do not assume all customers will accept these terms. Their willingness is part of the decision about whether to serve them.

No credible payback period or IRR can be promised without priced parts, realistic utilisation, financing and a cash-flow schedule. The purpose of these calculations is to show what must be true for the business to deserve investment.

## 12. Customer acquisition and export strategy

The first customers should be equipment makers with a specific reason to qualify another supplier. Relevant prospects include UPS, drive, power-converter and panel OEMs and industrial-machine builders. ABB's Bengaluru operations and Schneider's local manufacturing illustrate the ecosystem; they are not confirmed buyers, open tenders or guaranteed routes into procurement.[^2][^4] Include medium-sized firms so that the entire sales effort does not depend on a few multinationals.

For the filtration alternative, investigate maintenance, production and procurement teams at industrial plants and the OEMs already serving them. Anthem's facilities and vendor-registration channel provide one identifiable Bengaluru-area prospecting route.[^33] Critical pharma-contact applications may require a longer path than noncritical industrial services. A vendor-registration submission is not supplier approval.

### Build an attainable market from customer evidence

A national market-size percentage is an unreliable sales plan. For each prospect, establish relevant annual part spend, actual purchase history where shared, approval probability, expected share of purchases and timing. An illustrative pool of 25 reachable buyers spending ₹1 crore each on relevant parts would represent ₹25 crore before access and competition. At a hypothetical 20% share, it produces ₹5 crore sales—not enough for the larger example. These figures are a demonstration of the method, not market findings.

Ask why an additional supplier is needed, which drawings are available, what currently fails, what landed price is paid, how samples are approved, how long approvals take and what happens when engineering changes. Obtain the actual quality manual and purchase terms. Request paid samples or tooling rather than treating verbal enthusiasm as demand.

### Export sequence

Initially, supplying Indian OEMs with international operations can build experience without immediately carrying every overseas commercial responsibility. Direct exports should follow a named customer's specifications and landed-cost model. Compact engineered assemblies may tolerate freight better than bulky low-value metalwork, but tariff classification, packaging, insurance, quality documentation, collections and warranty must all be priced.

US policy changed materially in 2026. USTR's July action provides an additional 10% Section 301 duty for covered Indian goods, with exceptions; it is not a uniform all-in rate. Other product duties and regimes may apply. Obtain written classification and origin analysis and recalculate landed duties before quoting and shipping.[^18]

The UK government's September notice confirms its India FTA entered into force on 15 July 2026. Preferences depend on eligible products and origin documentation.[^19] The European Commission lists EU–India negotiations as concluded, which is different from an agreement in force; immediate preferential access should not be assumed.[^20]

EU CBAM entered its definitive regime in January 2026 for specified goods. Coverage follows customs scope and embedded emissions, not the mere presence of steel or aluminium in every finished product.[^21] Material and energy records are still useful because OEMs may request them even where the component is outside current CBAM scope.

I would diversify export destinations only after the domestic operation proves quality and cash discipline. Exporting is a route to growth and customer diversification; it does not automatically increase net margins or remove customer concentration.

## 13. Other factors that can change the choice

### Solar, storage and energy equipment

Renewable investment supports electrical equipment, but each segment has different economics. MNRE reported about 144 GW of ALMM-listed module capacity by the end of 2025.[^39] This is listed capacity, not utilisation or total output. A new module line must compete on cost, technology, buyer acceptance and finance, while navigating domestic sourcing rules and export trade measures. MNRE announced a limited ALMM relief window for selected projects in July 2026, illustrating the need to check exact applicability.[^49]

US Commerce's February 2026 solar countervailing-duty determination was preliminary; it should not be used as a verified final all-in rate for a September investment decision.[^40] The unresolved exact current duty stack is a reason to require specialist product-level verification before underwriting that route. It is not necessary to assume a particular final rate to recognise that export dependence adds risk.

Grid and industrial equipment components offer a broader customer base, while battery storage integration needs controls, cell sourcing, installation and warranty capability. Selling components to established system makers is the more manageable opening for a generalist.

### Labour, automation and factory execution

Bengaluru's engineering ecosystem is an advantage only if the business can attract and retain the right people at an affordable cost. Supervision, training and employee transport may materially affect productivity. Automation should address a measured bottleneck or recurring quality problem, rather than be installed as a symbol of sophistication.

Track first-pass yield, on-time delivery, overtime, scrap, machine downtime, customer complaints and contribution per constrained production hour. These measures help distinguish genuine operational improvement from higher turnover caused by commodity prices. Build preventive maintenance and spare-parts planning into the launch budget.

### Environmental, legal and reputational exposure

The process determines environmental obligations. Chemistry, plating, coatings, foundry work and recycling can create liabilities that simple assembly does not. A low-cost contractor is not a good supplier if its permits, waste handling or process control are unreliable. Product defects can also create losses disproportionate to the selling price of a small component.

Protect customer drawings and commercially sensitive production data, including when using external software or AI tools. Clarify intellectual-property ownership, confidentiality, tooling ownership, recall obligations, liability limits and termination rights. These terms can determine whether a profitable-looking order is worth accepting.

### Competition, cyclicality and business-model choice

Many supposedly attractive sectors contain weak businesses. Generic fabrication, commodity chemicals, undifferentiated packaging and contract assembly can all suffer price pressure. Conversely, a small specialist in an ordinary sector can earn good returns through reliable service, a recurring installed base or difficult qualification.

For a founder whose strongest asset is selling rather than production, a subcontracted manufacturing model may be a sensible first stage. It is not equivalent to owning a factory, but it reveals demand and process economics before fixing the asset base. For a founder with operational expertise but weak sales access, partnering with a commercial operator or acquiring a customer-backed business may be better.

The choice changes if there is already a strong founder advantage. A chemist with a validated route and customers can make APIs the winner. An aerospace production leader with an approved programme can make precision engineering the winner. An industrial-water salesperson with recurring replacement customers can make filtration the winner. Capital should follow that demonstrated advantage.

## 14. A practical 90-day decision programme

The first 90 days should decide which plant deserves to exist. They are not a promise that production, licences or customer approvals can all be completed in three months. An initial diligence and prototype budget of approximately ₹25–50 lakh is a planning allowance within the total capital, released in stages; actual specialist tests may change it.

| Period | Work and tangible evidence | Decision gate |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–15 | Recruit an experienced technical adviser/operator; identify 30–40 relevant prospects; define one electrical part family and one filtration application. | There are reachable buyers and a credible technical owner for each shortlisted route. |
| Days 16–35 | Conduct 20–30 substantive buyer discussions across the two routes; obtain drawings/specifications, target prices, volumes, approvals and payment terms. | Several real RFQs and a clear reason customers need another supplier. |
| Days 36–60 | Produce samples through qualified subcontractors; obtain independent tests; cost actual material, yield, processing and delivery. | Paid trials or development orders, not only letters of interest. |
| Days 61–75 | Compare machinery/lease quotations, staffing, compliance requirements, bank terms and realistic cash cycles. | The downside can be funded and the operating return clears the chosen hurdle. |
| Days 76–90 | Choose the stronger product family; agree qualification and repeat-order plans; release only essential capex. | At least two credible customer paths, proven sample quality and commercially workable terms. |

The proposed counts are management gates, not industry averages. Longer qualification can be acceptable if the team, customer and cash runway justify it. A paid prototype is still not a production contract; the funding plan must distinguish development revenue from repeat commercial shipments.

### Conditions that stop or materially resize the project

- Buyers want commodity pricing that fails the return hurdle even at realistic utilisation.
- Copper or other material exposure cannot be repriced or financed economically.
- One customer determines the viability of most machinery or inventory without adequate contractual protection.
- The production/quality leader cannot be recruited or the required process is outside the team's competence.
- Qualification, utility or environmental requirements exceed the available time and funding.
- The model requires unapproved subsidies, implausibly fast collections or full utilisation from launch.

### What I would do with different amounts of capital

With ₹1–3 crore, I would favour a small industrial harness/assembly operation or a subcontracted conductor-kit business, keeping cash available for development and collections. With ₹5–10 crore, I would build the focused electrical operation only after validation. With ₹10–20 crore, I would fund the same entry carefully and retain money for capacity or one proven adjacent capability. With ₹20–30 crore, I would also evaluate an existing qualified supplier or a partner-led precision business before choosing greenfield assets.

### Final recommendation

**My first choice remains a Bengaluru-based manufacturer of custom industrial conductor assemblies, initially supplying UPS, drive and power-conversion OEMs.** Its attraction is the combination of a coherent manufacturing process, local engineering capability and several potential customer industries. EVs and AI can add demand without being the only reasons the business exists.

I would run a parallel, smaller validation of industrial filtration replacement products. If its paid trials demonstrate stronger recurring margins and customer access, I would choose it instead. I would not select commodity API production, battery cells or solar modules merely because India needs more domestic supply.

The next investment should purchase evidence: priced customer requirements, successful samples, a capable team and a complete cash model. The factory should follow that evidence.
