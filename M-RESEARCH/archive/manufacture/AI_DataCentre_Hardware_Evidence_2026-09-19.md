# AI and data-centre hardware: Karnataka opportunity evidence

Research date: 19 September 2026. Independent growth-first screen for a new Karnataka manufacturing business. Assume an experienced technical team, access to useful introductions and financing for a focused launch; do not assume unlimited capital, access to proprietary NVIDIA/AMD designs or an available foreign OEM partnership. The earlier ₹1–30 crore envelope is a working reference, not confirmed funding. No outreach was conducted and earlier reports were preserved.

## Recommendation

**The strongest accessible AI-infrastructure manufacturing opportunity is qualified liquid-cooling distribution hardware, followed by engineered rack-power interconnects and specialised power magnetics.** These are routes into a growing infrastructure supply chain without first owning a GPU platform. The recommended first cooling product is a defined manifold, tested hose/tube assembly or fluid module supplied into an OEM-approved loop. A full CDU and a chip-level cold plate are different products with different approval burdens.

Growth-first ranking does lift cooling above its position in the earlier EV-only screen. The reason is new, specific evidence: Schneider has launched a liquid-cooling production plant in Bengaluru, while current GPU architectures require substantially more sophisticated power/cooling infrastructure. It does not establish that Schneider will onboard this startup or that every installed Indian AI rack contains locally purchasable parts.

**Ranking is a commercial judgment combining growth exposure and achievable entry, not a claim that the largest global market is the best startup.** The first six products below merit investigation. The last three are strategic alternatives with materially harder access or a different business model. Following the user's clarification, established competitors are treated as evidence of demand and capability benchmarks, not grounds to reject a category. Strong buyer access can particularly improve the server-integration case.

## Why the demand direction is credible—and where the inference stops

NVIDIA's 11 August 2026 update describes an MGX-compatible 800 VDC power rack intended for the second half of 2026, a hybrid path for existing AC facilities, and a row-power-centre stage expected in 2027. It references March 2026 joint work with Google/Microsoft and a July 2026 OCP specification. This is a current architecture/availability roadmap, not evidence that all Indian data centres have adopted 800 V or that an Indian supplier has a purchase order. [NVIDIA current roadmap](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/800-vdc-power-architecture-ai-factory/)

Existing GB200/GB300 documentation already defines rack-scale systems; the DGX SuperPOD reference architecture combines compute, networking, management and storage and includes both liquid and air cooling. Therefore, a full-rack sale can bring much of its own internal hardware. Counting GPUs installed in India and multiplying by a hypothetical startup cold-plate price is not a valid addressable-market estimate. [NVIDIA rack-system guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx/dgxgb200-user-guide/), [DGX SuperPOD reference architecture](https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx-superpod/reference-architecture-scalable-infrastructure-gb200/latest/dgx-superpod-architecture.html)

AMD's 2026 Helios disclosures describe an Open Rack Wide platform; current filings also report expanded collaboration with Microsoft. This supplies a second architecture/ecosystem growth signal. Open specifications lower interface opacity but do not remove OEM qualification, implementation IP or commercial supplier selection. [AMD Helios technical announcement](https://www.amd.com/zh-cn/newsroom/press-releases/aai-2026-helios-update.html), [AMD August 2026 filing](https://ir.amd.com/financial-information/sec-filings/content/0000002488-26-000121/0000002488-26-000121.pdf)

The commercially relevant inference is that more approved cooling and power hardware will be required. The missing fact is **which bill-of-material items the actual Indian OEM/integrator will outsource to a new Indian supplier**. Obtain that answer before treating global GPU growth as startup revenue.

## Cooling: strongest growth-first candidate, with an exact entry boundary

Schneider Electric announced the launch of its Motivair liquid-cooling factory in Bengaluru on 18 February 2026: its first such production site in India and third globally. This is stronger evidence than an earlier planned-investment statement. It establishes a real local manufacturing ecosystem and an incumbent capable of supplying complete systems. It does not disclose the startup-accessible Indian BOM or a supplier invitation. [Schneider factory launch](https://www.se.com/in/en/about-us/newsroom/news/press-releases/Schneider-Electric-launches-Motivair-liquid-cooling-solutions-factory-to-accelerate-India%E2%80%99s-highdensity-AI-infrastructure-699493fd07a7fbd2c30cbafa/)

Delta separately states that its GoCool 3 MW liquid-to-liquid CDU began shipping in March 2026. Its May 2026 product disclosure describes rack and row cooling, including a 140 kW in-rack CDU for GB300 NVL72. These show that competition is at system level with controls and platform integration, not just metal fabrication. [Delta shipping announcement](https://www.deltapowersolutions.com/en/mcis/news-2026-gocool-3mw-cdu-now-shipping-powering-the-next-generation-of-ai-data-centers.php), [Delta 2026 architecture](https://www.delta-americas.com/en-us/news/delta-800-vdc-ai-factories-power-cooling-microgrid-solutions)

The opportunity has three separate commercial layers:

1. **Facility-side infrastructure:** prefabricated distribution sections, pump/filtration modules and approved connections into the CDU. More locally installable and serviceable, but lower technical differentiation can attract price competition.
2. **Technology cooling loop:** qualified manifolds, tubing/hoses, approved quick disconnects, cleanliness and leak-tested assemblies between CDU and compute equipment. Better differentiated, but the OEM controls materials, interfaces and approval.
3. **Direct-to-chip cold plates:** thermal performance, channel geometry, flatness, interface loading and package-specific design. These may arrive already integrated with a server or rack; they are not automatically bought by the Indian data-centre owner.

**Recommended launch:** one OEM-specified product in layer 1 or 2, with design-for-manufacture support and a documented testing/cleanliness process. Buy approved quick disconnects initially rather than claiming a complete locally developed valve technology. Move to cold plates only with the customer's package/design rights and a funded validation programme.

OCP's current cooling work explicitly spans cold plates, tubing, manifolds, quick disconnects and CDUs. Its workstreams cover fluid chemistry/material compatibility, connector interfaces, hoses and performance; some documents remain drafts. Passing a generic water-pressure test is insufficient, and an open OCP interface is not automatic approval by NVIDIA, AMD, Schneider or a cloud operator. [OCP cooling project](https://www.opencompute.org/community/cold-plate), [active specifications/workstreams](https://www.opencompute.org/wiki/Cooling_Environments/Cold_Plate)

Qualification should cover the buyer's required coolant/material combinations, pressure and temperature envelope, flushing/cleanliness, pressure drop, leak detection/isolation, fatigue and corrosion lifetime, and change control. These are engineering gates; no universal numerical acceptance limits are asserted here. Different customer programmes can require different limits.

## Rack power: strong second platform, avoid conflating conductor hardware with conversion

TE's current portfolio documents engineered power busbars, insulation/plating/joining options, monitoring and liquid-cooled busbars; its OCP products cover rack-level power distribution. This demonstrates existing technical competition and the distinction between a busbar assembly and a bare copper strip. [TE power busbar portfolio](https://www.te.com/en/products/connectors/power-connectors/intersection/power-busbar-solution.html)

The startup entry is an OEM-approved conductor/insulation/mechanical assembly, with verified contact resistance, thermal rise, creepage/clearance, dielectric performance and fault requirements for its exact application. Start from the current customer's voltage/current specification. Do not buy machinery solely on an assumption that 800 V has replaced every 48/54 V architecture.

A complete power shelf additionally requires control firmware, high-frequency power conversion, semiconductor sourcing, protection coordination, EMI/EMC validation, system communication and redundancy. Delta already offers integrated power/cooling architectures. An OEM may buy the shelf as a complete qualified system; the Indian data-centre operator may never select its internal transformers or busbars individually. [Delta AI portfolio](https://www.deltaww.com/en-US/landing/Delta-for-AI)

**Power magnetics can be an independent component business** because a defined transformer/choke can serve multiple power-conversion programmes. But the customer must nominate its design or approve the supplier's design. Core loss/saturation, winding AC loss, thermal performance and insulation require specialised design and test. Delta's 2025 annual report describes advanced inductor structures and proprietary materials for AI power delivery: this is evidence that leading-edge point-of-load inductors are not interchangeable with ordinary wound ferrite parts. [Delta annual report](https://filecenter.deltaww.com/IR/download/annual_report/2025annual.pdf)

## Cabling and optics: separate accessible assembly from leading-edge silicon/optics

TE markets 224G-capable interconnects and OSFP products supporting 200G per lane/1.6T ports. Its offerings include passive copper, active copper, optical and near-chip connections. The buyer is procuring a signal-integrity-qualified channel, not only a mechanically correct cable. [TE technical portfolio](https://www.te.com/en/industries/data-centers-ai/technologies/224g-gigabit-ethernet-solution.html)

A new entrant could manufacture a narrower passive assembly using licensed/approved connector systems and appropriate test equipment. For electrical high-speed cable, insertion/return loss, crosstalk, connector termination quality and channel verification matter. For passive optical assemblies, connector geometry, end-face cleanliness, insertion/return loss and traceability matter. Ordinary patch-cord assembly is easier but faces stronger commodity competition and captures less of the AI-specific value.

**Do not bundle 800G optical transceivers into the same investment proposal.** A transceiver can require lasers, photonic components, DSP/driver/TIA devices, precision assembly/alignment, firmware, thermal design and module qualification. No accessible technology partnership or competitive all-in factory cost was established for this user. The available evidence justifies a partner-led investigation, not a promise that a simple cable factory can become a 1.6T optical-module supplier.

## Servers: local manufacture is real, but imported compute still dominates the critical inputs

Giga Computing reports that Syrma SGS has commenced server motherboard PCBA at Chennai, naming MS73-HB0 and MZ33-AR1, with subsequent system integration/box build on its roadmap. Its announcement page is dated 28 January 2026, while the body contains a conflicting January 2025 dateline; the earlier partnership announcement is dated December 2025. Use it as evidence of the disclosed manufacturing milestone and location, not an unqualified precise chronology. This is **Giga Computing/Gigabyte, not a verified Syrma–MiTAC partnership**. [Giga/Syrma milestone](https://www.gigacomputing.com/en/news-detail/syrma-sgs-technology-limited-and-giga-computing-mark-first-build-milestone-for-server-manufacturing-in-india/), [original partnership announcement](https://www.gigabyte.com/la/press/news/2334)

Netweb describes server/HPC design, integration and testing under its own platforms, and its company material identifies manufacturing in Faridabad. A Bengaluru sales office must not be presented as a Bengaluru GPU-server factory. [Netweb product/business description](https://www.netwebindia.com/), [manufacturing location](https://www.netwebindia.com/career.php)

For a new entrant, an application-specific inference appliance is more credible than claiming a full domestic GPU server ecosystem: purchase supported compute modules, solve an actual enterprise/industrial workload and own the integration, thermal design, deployment and service. This is a valid value-added systems business, but it does not replace the GPU, HBM, CPU or most leading networking silicon. Inventory, OEM support rights, firmware security, warranty and customer credit terms can consume more cash than final assembly equipment.

Server-board design/PCBA and complete high-end rack manufacturing require a more substantial programme with design access and validation than assembling purchased server boards. No right to NVIDIA reference designs or allocation of scarce compute components follows from political or industry introductions.

## GPU cloud: strong demand theme, different investment decision

An owned GPU cloud earns revenue from billable utilisation, not from local manufacturing. It competes with established providers: E2E currently publishes B200/H200/H100 offerings and per-GPU-hour tariffs, and separately announced revised pricing effective July 2026. A new provider cannot infer its attainable price from a historic rate or assume 100% paid utilisation. [E2E live pricing](https://www.e2enetworks.com/pricing), [2026 tariff update](https://docs.e2enetworks.com/docs/myaccount/billing/pricing-update-july-2026/)

The core underwriting calculation is: owned GPU count × available hours × paid utilisation × realised net tariff. Deduct energy/cooling/colo, networking/storage, software, support, hardware finance, downtime and economic depreciation. At an unchanged price and GPU count, moving from 50% to 80% paid utilisation raises revenue by 60%; it does not reduce the original hardware purchase price. This is arithmetic, not an assumed actual utilisation forecast.

I would not rank speculative GPU-cloud ownership above the component opportunities for this brief. A committed customer, capacity reservation or differentiated managed workload could change that decision. A small deployment may fit the working envelope, but a small deployment is not automatically competitive with an established national cloud. Current hardware quotations and utilisation commitments were not obtained.

## Capital and first commercial milestones

No independent equipment quotations were obtained. Narrow operations in ranks 1–3 and 5–6 can be investigated within the historical ₹1–30 crore envelope using leased premises, selective outsourcing and a staged customer programme; this is a scope judgment, not a verified all-in cost. Rank 4 can also begin within the envelope at limited system volume, but GPU/server inventory and customer credit materially affect the cash requirement. The lowest end of that envelope will generally fund engineering/prototypes rather than a complete automated factory with all validation capabilities and extended operating runway.

Complete CDU, optical-module and high-power conversion programmes require separate partner/IP, validation, warranty and working-capital budgets. A JV can provide technology access but is not automatically available. Government schemes and the user's network may help introductions and institution access; they do not substitute for customer engineering approval or commercial underwriting.

For the first three candidates, request a **paid prototype/qualification programme** against an existing or new OEM product. Obtain the actual BOM boundary, drawings/interfaces, expected volumes, incumbent origin, target price, qualification responsibilities and warranty allocation. For racks bought complete, identify which procurement sits with the foreign OEM and which sits with the Indian integrator/facility contractor.

**Best initial platform:** an AI-infrastructure fluid/power component supplier, beginning with one qualified product and customer. Avoid describing one factory as simultaneously competent in cold plates, UHP semiconductor gas, 800 V power conversion and optical transceivers: the processes, contamination controls, electronics and qualification regimes differ substantially.
