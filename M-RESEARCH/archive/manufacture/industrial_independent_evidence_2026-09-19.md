# Independent industrial manufacturing opportunity evidence ledger

Research cut-off: 19 September 2026. Scope: independent research beyond the supplied EV/semiconductor deck; no attachment reanalysis and no outreach. This is an input to a combined ranking, not a claim that all ten screened industrial categories belong in the final ten.

Working scenario: Bengaluru access, an experienced technical team and useful introductions through H. D. Kumaraswamy, with an initial total funding envelope of approximately INR 1–30 crore. Access, funding and expertise are scenario assumptions, not verified founder assets or promised procurement. No subsidies, government orders, land concessions or exclusive licences are assumed.

## Recommendation

**The strongest practical new candidate is precision injection-mould tooling for demanding electrical components: connector housings, relay bobbins and similar small technical parts.** Build a specialist tool room around a few repeatable product families, first securing tool drawings and trial requirements from two buyers. MHI reports substantial broad tooling imports, and CEA separately identifies high-precision injection-mould tools as an import-localisation need. These two sources establish a credible direction; they do not establish the accessible revenue for a new factory. [S1, S2]

**Precision-ground ball screws are the strongest second candidate for technological import substitution, but the factory investment case is less certain.** CMTI identifies high import dependence and relevant domestic technology. Its latest newsletter also explicitly describes high capital requirements and entrenched Taiwanese/Chinese competition. It transferred high-speed ball-screw technology to Jyoti CNC in February 2026. An independently available licence, acceptable unit economics and a complete competitive factory within INR 30 crore have not been established. Prefer a limited product-range partnership or qualified component programme, subject to an engineered investment study. [S3–S6]

**Third: narrowly specified relay/solenoid winding, assembly and electrical-test machinery.** CEA identifies relevant imported equipment, while existing domestic machine builders show that entry at a manageable scale is plausible. The opportunity is a production process that a buyer currently imports and cannot adequately source locally; generic winding machines already have Indian competition. This is distinct from making relays or operating a coil-winding job shop. [S2, S9–S12]

## Evidence and estimate rules

- Import statistics for broad industries are background, not a product's addressable market. A public purchase of a branded item does not establish foreign origin or acceptance of an alternative.
- A localisation consultation is not a purchase order or active tender. Historical lists establish a sourcing route only; current specifications and volumes must be obtained.
- The funding ranges below are **analyst screening estimates**, not supplier quotations or bankable project costs. They assume a rented industrial building, selected machinery, lean technical staffing, initial development and working capital; exclude land purchase, financing costs and subsequent scale-up. The upper bound is not a guarantee the project can be delivered within it.
- Timelines and qualification work are engineering/commercial judgments, not promised buyer schedules. Standards and procurement conditions must be checked against the selected product.
- Company websites establish that a supplier markets a capability; they do not independently validate its quality, production volume or market share.

## What the connection can concretely improve

MHI's documented capital-goods work and its CMTI relationship offer a relevant route to technical discussions, technology due diligence, shared testing and introductions to established machine-tool companies. For this industrial screen, that is a more direct fit than assuming every EV, semiconductor or defence factory falls under the ministry. [S1, S3–S5]

A useful meeting request would be: identify three currently imported part or tool families; arrange discussions with the actual engineering and procurement teams; obtain specifications, recent order quantities, landed costs and failure concerns; then seek a paid development or supplier-qualification programme. A technology-transfer meeting should settle what IP is licensable, whether any exclusivity applies, expected process yields, required machines and responsibility for unsuccessful qualification. These are proposed uses of access, not confirmed entitlements.

BEML and MDL are defence-sector routes; they should not be represented as MHI subsidiaries. Railways, MeitY, semiconductor OEMs and private tool buyers have their own technical and commercial decisions. Existing tender rules and supplier qualification still apply. A ministerial introduction can accelerate the right conversation; it is not evidence of contracted revenue.

## Specific findings that change the shortlist

1. **The latest ball-screw information strengthens the import case but weakens a simple startup-factory pitch.** CMTI's 28 February 2026 newsletter records the Jyoti transfer and explicitly describes high investment and strong overseas competition. Its INR 3,178 crore estimate combines ball screws and LM guides and applies a machine-cost assumption; it is not measured ball-screw imports or an independently confirmed addressable market. [S4]
2. **CMTI catalogues are not a catalogue of unclaimed exclusive businesses.** Air-bearing spindle technology had already been licensed historically; optical glass-scale technology was transferred to Tech Source Solutions. The TTT4 described in a 2023 profile is manually operated, not evidence of a ready-to-license competitive five-axis CNC rotary table. Existing suppliers also include UCAM and Setco. [S28, S30–S32]
3. **Product selection should precede factory selection.** A CNC/EDM plant can serve tooling, hydraulic parts and selected semiconductor equipment parts, but simultaneously targeting all three creates incompatible cleanliness, process and qualification demands. Select the first paid part family; add related capacity after process capability is demonstrated.
4. **Do not claim “zero local competition.”** MTAR's website calls itself the sole Indian ball-screw manufacturer, while other Indian firms also advertise manufacture. Treat exclusivity as an unverified marketing claim; use MTAR's actual product capability as evidence of competition. [S6, S13]
5. **The evidence does not support moving generic chemicals or medical consumables above these finalists.** Those sectors were not deeply investigated in this bounded screen, so this is not a finding that they lack opportunities.

## Exclusions and access limitations

- An indexed IMTMA FY2024–25 annual-report URL redirected to an unrelated gambling domain when opened. It was excluded; use the official MHI parliamentary figures above, and do not combine unreconciled production estimates from different publications.
- No item-level DGCIS extract, customer landed-cost sheet, bankable machinery quotation, executed technical licence or purchase commitment was obtained. These remain necessary for choosing a factory investment.
- Several CMTI pages/PDFs failed in the web reader. The February 2026 newsletter was verified by direct PDF retrieval and extraction; some other official PDF findings came from indexed passages. Source-specific scope is stated above.
- No contact information was used to message vendors or government officials. No builder scripts or supplied research files were executed or modified.
