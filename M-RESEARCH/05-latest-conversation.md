## User: how to use the network

> what exactly should we find out throught the netowork the best thing to find out so that we can do this the best way possible

## Assistant

**The most valuable thing your network can find is a customer who says: “We need this specific product, and we are willing to test an Indian-made version and buy it if it passes.”**

That gives us a clear product to build, a buyer and a route to our first order.

The main question to ask is:

> **“Which products do you regularly import that you would like someone to manufacture in India—and what would we need to do to become your supplier?”**

Then get these answers:

| What to find out | Exact question to ask |
|---|---|
| **1. The exact product** | “Can you share the product name, specifications or drawings that we would need to manufacture it?” |
| **2. The actual buying quantity** | “How many do you buy each month? How much more do you expect to need over the next two years?” |
| **3. Why they want another supplier** | “Is your problem price, delivery delays, quality, limited supply or dependence on imports?” |
| **4. The price we must achieve** | “At what price would you seriously consider buying from us? What would the payment terms be?” |
| **5. How we get approved** | “What tests must our product pass, who approves it, how long does approval take and who pays for testing?” |
| **6. Whether they will support a trial** | “Will your engineers help us develop the first samples? Would you place a paid trial order?” |
| **7. What happens after approval** | “If we pass your tests and meet the agreed price, what initial quantity could you order?” |
| **8. How long the product will remain useful** | “Is this product likely to remain in use for several years, or will your next machine or chip design replace it?” |

**Use the network to reach the people who can answer these questions together:**

- **Purchasing head:** knows quantities, prices and supplier problems.
- **Engineering or quality head:** knows specifications and controls product approval.
- **Factory operations head:** knows what causes delays and what the factory repeatedly needs.

Supplier approval is a real process. For example, Micron publishes registration and qualification requirements for prospective suppliers. A meeting should help establish that approval path. [Micron’s supplier information](https://www.micron.com/about/company/suppliers)

Your connection to H. D. Kumaraswamy could also help arrange two other useful sets of introductions:

| Introduction | What we should obtain |
|---|---|
| **An experienced technology partner** | Proof they already manufacture the product successfully; what technology they will transfer; training, machines, costs and responsibility for making good products consistently. |
| **India Semiconductor Mission and Karnataka’s industry team** | Written clarity on which support applies to our exact project, eligible expenses, application timing, land and infrastructure availability, and when assistance would actually be paid. |

**My suggested target for the first round of meetings:** collect **three specific product opportunities from several potential customers**, then compare their quantities, achievable prices, approval difficulty and technology requirements.

The strongest early result would be **a paid sample order with agreed tests**. The stronger result after that is **a successful trial followed by a repeat production order**.

A letter saying someone is interested is useful, but it does not guarantee purchases. **Build the factory plan around evidence of what customers will buy, at what price, and after which tests.**

## User: what the research already answered

> when you did your research what did you find out as the answer to these wqwuestions tell it to me in simple words

## Assistant

**I found real demand and specific products worth investigating. Some answers—especially prices, quantities and willingness to buy from us—still need to come from customers.** My research used public company announcements, financial reports and supplier requirements; I have not contacted buyers.

Here is what I found against those questions:

| Our question | What the research actually answered |
|---|---|
| **1. What exact products could we make?** | Three clear candidates are **chip-testing sockets and boards**, **leadframes**—small metal frames inside certain chips—and **advanced package substrates**—the complex base beneath a processor. We have identified product categories; we still need a buyer’s exact design and specifications. |
| **2. How much would customers buy?** | **I did not find reliable monthly purchasing quantities for these products from individual Indian buyers.** Factory announcements show planned production, but that cannot directly tell us how many sockets, boards or machines they will buy. |
| **3. Why would they need another supplier?** | There is evidence of **insufficient supply in advanced substrates**. IBIDEN reported shortages and rising prices. For leadframes, Tata and Enomoto are exploring Indian manufacturing. These are real reasons to investigate, although each buyer’s reason for choosing us remains unknown. [IBIDEN](https://www.ibiden.com/ir/items/en_QA_FY2026Q1.pdf), [Tata–Enomoto announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2312234&lang=2&reg=48) |
| **4. What price would they pay us?** | **Not established.** I did not obtain customer quotations or negotiated purchasing prices. We therefore cannot yet calculate a dependable profit margin for our proposed product. |
| **5. How would we become an approved supplier?** | There is a formal process involving registration, qualification and meeting the customer’s requirements. Micron publishes this process. **The exact tests, cost and time for our chosen product are still unknown.** [Micron](https://www.micron.com/about/company/suppliers) |
| **6. Would they help develop samples or pay for a trial?** | **No commitment has been obtained.** Existing industry partnerships show collaboration happens, but they do not establish that someone will partner with our company. |
| **7. Would we receive orders after passing their tests?** | **No order has been confirmed for us.** I did find actual business in this market: Smiths won an exclusive testing-socket contract for an AI/GPU chip programme. That proves customers buy these products, while also showing that some programmes already have selected suppliers. [Smiths](https://www.smiths.com/news-and-insights/news/2025/smiths-interconnect-announces-significant-contract-win) |
| **8. How long would our product remain useful?** | The need to test and package chips continues, but individual designs change. A socket made for one chip may need redesigning for another. **We have not established the expected life of a particular product we would manufacture.** |

Two further answers remain open:

- **Technology partner:** I found examples of partnerships, but no company has offered us its technology, training or commercial terms.
- **Government support:** Relevant schemes exist, but our eligibility and assistance amount depend on the exact project. No funding has been approved for us.

**The strongest discovery was a shortage in advanced substrates. The testing-products research established a real market, but did not prove an India-wide shortage of testing sockets or boards.**

The most valuable missing information is now very specific: **one buyer’s product specifications, annual quantity, acceptable price and willingness to approve our samples.** Those four answers would let us judge whether a particular factory makes business sense.
