Where we win, where we lose, and what is worth copying

An honest scoring of XELOR against Workroom and Seewise — what we genuinely do better, what they genuinely do better, and the specific things they have worked out that we should simply adopt rather than reinvent.

17 August 2026 Written to be uncomfortable where it should be

1The uncomfortable summary

Our software is probably the best built of the three. Almost nobody can tell, because everything we are good at is invisible to a factory owner, and everything they are good at is the part a factory owner actually looks at.

What we are genuinely better at

  • How much of the factory we cover
  • How carefully it is built and checked
  • Being honest about what does not work
  • Two ways to buy it, not one

Where we are clearly behind

  • Not one real customer
  • Not one person willing to vouch for us
  • No easy first step for a buyer to say yes to
  • Nothing that brings strangers to us
  • No money in the bank

What we have that they do not

  • A way in through CoSol on day one
  • Peenya on our doorstep — 13,000 factories
  • A published list of our own gaps
  • Software that decides, not just watches

The single sentence that matters. Workroom has six people, five web pages and no proof on those pages — and they still won Apollo Tyres, because they had four customer results and we have none. The gap between us and them is not engineering. It is one live factory.

3Against Workroom — the head-to-head

This is the fight. Same buyer, same problem, same country. Here is where each of us actually stands.

Where we beat them

  • We cover more of the factory. They do planning, execution, traceability and quality data. We add money, people, maintenance, customer service and purchasing. A factory replacing Tally and Excel needs all of it.
  • We have two products, they have one. XELOR Core for plants with nothing, XELOR Layer for plants that already bought something. Workroom must always displace whatever is there.
  • We are honest about our gaps. They publish nothing about limits. When a buyer finds a gap themselves, trust breaks. When you told them first, it does not.
  • We can run without the internet. A real objection in Indian plants; nothing on their site answers it.

Where they beat us

  • They have customers and we do not. Apollo Tyres, Hindustan Pencils, ALSTEF, Plumage. Everything else on this page is a footnote next to that.
  • They have ₹6.2 crore. Runway to hire salespeople while we are still studying.
  • Their founders carry Forbes 30 Under 30. A credential that opens meetings.
  • Their one-liner is better than ours. “Thinks like your best planner, executes like your best supervisor” needs no explanation. Ours still needs a paragraph.
  • They are already live in plants. Every month running is learning we do not have.

The one place they are genuinely exposed

Their solution pages have no proof on them at all. Their four customer results sit only on the home page. Click “Workforce Planning” and you get a feature list and a screenshot — no numbers, no customer, no story.

Meanwhile they are six people supporting Apollo Tyres and an international automation group. Support and delivery are certainly stretched. If we can offer the same coverage with better attention on a first pilot, that is a real, honest wedge — not a marketing claim.

4Against Seewise — not a fight, a lesson

They are not really our competitor. They are the best teacher available, and they are teaching for free on their own website.

Why they are not really a rival. Seewise can see. It cannot decide. A camera watching a furnace cannot take an order, check whether the material is in stock, raise a purchase order, or tell you whether you can promise a date. They are a very good sensor on the wall. We are the system that runs the business. A serious factory ends up wanting both.

And the direction of travel favours us: adding cameras to a system of record is far easier than adding a system of record to cameras.

Six things they do that we should simply copy

1. Name the job, not the software

They have pages called furnace monitoring, weighbridge automation, truck loading. A foundry manager sees their own job on the screen.

We have “SPAR”, “KILN” and “AXLE”. Nobody outside our team knows what those mean.

2. Offer a two-week trial

14 days, free analysis, uses equipment they already own, support throughout. It removes every reason to say no and needs no boss’s signature.

We currently ask people to look at a demo. That asks for belief, not a decision.

3. Publish how the rollout goes

Four steps with real numbers, including admitting it starts at 60% accuracy before reaching 98%. The admission is what makes the rest believable.

We are already good at this instinct. We just apply it to the product, not to delivery.

4. Use one case-study shape, every time

Challenge → what we switched on → how it works → the numbers → what it meant for the business. Six studies, identical shape.

Anyone on the team can fill in a form. Nobody has to write from scratch.

5. Hide the customer, keep the detail

“A metal foundry”, “an automotive components maker”. Logos shown elsewhere. This lets them publish detail a named customer would never sign off.

Our first pilot customer will be nervous. This is how we still get to publish.

6. Put the comparison table on your own site

They compare themselves against old vision systems and generic AI tools, in public. It looks confident and saves the buyer the work.

Our deck already does this on one slide. It should be a page, not a slide.

And two commercial moves worth thinking hard about

A consulting arm alongside the product

They run consulting.seewise.ai selling engineering services. It is very likely how they funded 42 people with no investors.

For us: CoSol already sells services into these factories. Paid work inside a plant is how you learn the problem and earn the right to sell the product — exactly the path Workroom’s founder took.

Recruit partners instead of customers

They are hiring a channel partner manager — someone whose job is to get other companies selling for them.

For us: we are already inside a Cisco Gold Partner. They are hiring for a relationship we were born with, and we are not using it.

Where our material is actually better

  • Our numbers are shown with the arithmetic. Our cost slide shows how ₹1.7 crore is reached, line by line. Neither competitor shows their working. Ours is more believable to anyone who checks.
  • We say what we cannot do. Neither of them publishes a limits page. In a market where every company claims AI, this is a genuine and unusual position.
  • We priced our claims low on purpose and said so. We model 10% less downtime where the industry quotes 30–50%. That is a trust-builder no competitor offers.

So the honest conclusion is narrower than it first looks. Our content is good — arguably the most trustworthy of the three. What we lack is reach (nowhere for a stranger to land), proof (no customer), and an easy first step (nothing to say yes to). Those three are fixable in months, and none of them requires more engineering.
