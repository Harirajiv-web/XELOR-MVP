The problem

Eight leaks. Every factory
has all eight.

None of them is dramatic. None appears in the accounts. Together they decide how many orders a factory can take.

  • 01Tools that do not talk Tally for billing. A register for stock. WhatsApp for suppliers. A diary for the floor. Every number typed 3–4×
  • 02No idea what is happening now You find out about today on Monday — after the shift that caused it went home. Decisions a week late
  • 03Machines stop with no warning Two in three plants, every month, about four hours a time. The fix starts after it stops. ~11% of revenue
  • 04Stock counted by hand Too much of what you do not need. None of what you do. Cash frozen · lines still stop
  • 05Every order made by hand POs, sales orders, job cards, invoices — typed, re-typed, chased, corrected. Hours a day, per person
  • 06You cannot find your own data The answer exists. Finding it costs more than the decision is worth. 15 min per document
  • 07No proper flow of work No two orders take the same route. What happens next depends on who came in today. Promises made on hope
  • 08This is most of Indian manufacturing 7.83 crore registered MSMEs, making 35.4% of India’s manufacturing output. The market, not an edge case

The nine

Nine areas of responsibility.
Kept apart on purpose.

Not nine separate AIs — one system doing nine jobs, kept apart so the one counting your stock can be shown never to have opened your payroll.

01

The supervisor

ONYX

Frames the mission and consults only the specialists whose answer could change it. Deliberately the least powerful of the nine — one read tool, no write capability.

02

Governance

HEXA

Who is allowed to do what, whose approval is needed, and the record that cannot be edited afterwards. The same rules bind every one of the nine — including the supervisor.

03

Sales & customers

MICA

What was promised, to whom, at what price, by when — and which delivery, service or warranty commitment is about to slip.

04

Stores & buying

SPAR

What is on the shelf, what is on order, where a shortage threatens a promise. Prepares the buying; never places it alone.

05

Design & planning

AXLE

Connects what the product is made of with what demand requires: what to make, what to buy, by when — and which dates cannot be met.

06

Shop floor & quality

KILN

What the plant is making, whether it passes, whether the machines will still be there tomorrow. Holds the batch until quality is settled.

07

Money & people

RASP

The accounting truth, the cash position, what is already committed — and the payroll consequence behind an operating decision.

08

Service & operations

RELAY

The running of the plant after the sale — service and managed-operations work, rather than the making of the goods.

09

The watchman

ACHILES

Watches whether XELOR itself is healthy. The only one that cannot act at all, by construction — giving it the ability would take a code change and a review.

The honest boundary

What works today.
And what does not.

Every line on the left is checked automatically every time we change the software — if one stops being true, the build fails. Every line on the right is designed and not yet built.

Working now · checked on every build

  • Nine specialists, each fenced into its own job — the one that reads stock physically cannot open payroll
  • Twenty-four working areas of a factory — taking the order, buying, making, checking, paying people
  • Only one action in the whole system can change anything — and it needs a person’s approval first
  • Five plans, fixed in advance — it chooses between them; it cannot invent a new one while running
  • A record of every decision that cannot be edited afterwards, and one factory’s data can never surface in another’s

Not built yet · named, not buried

  • No connection to an outside system is live yet — SAP, Tally and MES are designed and today driven by simulation
  • The language model is switched off on purpose — today’s reasoning is arithmetic on real numbers, not a chatbot
  • No quotations yet — and for an MSME the quotation is the sales process
  • Buying stops at goods-in — no supplier invoice, no matching, no payment run
  • Quality problems can be flagged but not investigated — the follow-up trail does not exist
The safety was never in the model.Which is why a smarter model changes nothing on this page.

Deployment

Three ways to run it.
Most factories should run two.

Cloud

RUNS OFF-SITE
  • Latency 200–500 ms
  • Data leaves the plant
  • Offline: stops
Fine for month-end analysis. Not for a decision the line is waiting on.

Hybrid

RECOMMENDED
  • Instant, private decisions on the floor
  • Heavy learning across plants in the cloud
  • Offline: keeps running
The 2026 consensus: ~40% faster response, 30–50% lower cloud cost than cloud-only.

Edge

RUNS ON THE FLOOR
  • Latency under 200 ms
  • Data never leaves
  • Offline: unaffected
For plants that will not send data outside the gate. A customer, not an objection.

“Where does our data go?” is the question that kills industrial software deals — having a real answer for all three is how we stay in the room. One codebase either way: deployment is a configuration, not a different edition with a different price list.

The direction of travel is set. Gartner expects more than two-thirds of enterprises to deploy edge AI by 2029, up from 10% in 2025. Building edge-capable from the start is not conservatism; it is where the market is going.
