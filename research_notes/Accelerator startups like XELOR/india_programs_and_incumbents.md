# India: accelerator-backed startups similar to XELOR, plus Indian incumbents and government rails (as of 2 Oct 2026)

Method note: the research ran on web search snippets because direct page fetches to inc42.com and atoms.accel.com were blocked by the egress proxy. Every fact below is attributed to the search-result source shown. Facts dated 2026 come from 2026-dated articles and have not been checked against primary filings. "Closeness" compares each company with XELOR's core loop: a short part found in the material check, then the RFQ written from item master/BOM, quotes from suppliers through a no-login link, ranking, PO, phone approval, then GRN/inspection feeding an earned supplier record, plus capacity sharing, a factory "passport", and the GST/e-invoice/43B(h) layer.

## Q1. Which Peak XV Surge and Accel Atoms cohort companies (2019–2026) are in manufacturing procurement, supplier networks or MSME industrial B2B?

### Takeaway
Very few. Procol (Sequoia Surge 03, 2020) is the only clear Surge procurement company found, and it sells enterprise procure-to-pay, not SME-factory software. Accel Atoms' manufacturing picks (Ripik AI in Atoms 2.0, LevelPlane, Asets in Atoms 3.0) work on plant AI, automation and CAD, not supplier networks. The latest Surge cohort (Surge 12, Sept 2026) leans toward AI, robotics, space, consumer, health, music and fintech, and 13 of its 18 companies target global markets.

### Inferences
- Neither flagship Indian accelerator has backed an "SME-factory ERP plus supplier network" company in the cohorts visible here. That is a white space for XELOR, or a sign that VCs see SME-factory SaaS as a hard market to sell into.
- Procol is the most useful comparator from these programmes. It shows an agentic RFQ/sourcing product can sell in India, but to enterprises. XELOR's difference is the SME factory as buyer, suppliers responding with no login, and earned performance records tied to GRN and inspection.

### Gaps
- Full company lists for Surge 1–12 and Atoms 1.0, 2.0, 2025 and 2026 could not be retrieved (inc42 and atoms.accel.com fetches were blocked). Some Surge companies in B2B industrial, supply-chain finance or logistics may therefore be missing.
- Whether Sourcewiz/WizCommerce or any other manufacturer-facing SaaS went through Surge was not confirmed (see Q2).

## Q2. Which Indian startups (founded or funded 2022–2026) build AI procurement/sourcing for manufacturers, WhatsApp-native supplier communication, MSME factory ERP with a supplier network, or contract-manufacturing networks?

### Takeaway
The newer Indian companies split into three groups: (a) category-specific AI procurement marketplaces (1Buy.AI for electronics, NowPurchase for metals/foundries, Metalbook for metals, Mstack for chemicals); (b) contract-manufacturing networks (Karkhana.io, BIDSO, and Fashinza, which is winding down); (c) small AI tools for quoting and procurement (Sculpt YC S22, Waybill YC S26, BharatTender). No company found combines SME-factory ERP, no-login WhatsApp supplier quoting, and earned supplier records the way XELOR does.

### Inferences
- The well-funded money in India's "manufacturing procurement" goes to category marketplaces (metals, chemicals, electronics) that earn on material margin, not to SaaS. XELOR's software-plus-network model competes with these marketplaces for the factory's buying relationship, and could also be supplied by them.
- 1Buy.AI is the closest new entrant on the AI RFQ engine. Karkhana.io and Zetwerk are the closest on networks of SME-factory capacity. Procol is the closest on agentic sourcing software.
- Fashinza's wind-down suggests managed factory networks struggle on unit economics. That supports XELOR's approach of letting factories run their own records instead of a platform taking margin (an inference, not an established finding).

### Gaps
- Announcement dates for 1Buy.AI's seed and Metalbook's Series A were not exactly confirmed.
- Funded MSME-factory ERP startups (e.g., production-planning SaaS for SME machine shops) did not appear in searches. Their absence may be a search limitation. Candidates from background knowledge that were not verified this session: Swipe (GST billing), FloBiz/myBillBook, Deskera, Marg ERP.
- No source found for Indian-founded, abroad-headquartered players such as Kavida.ai (CB Insights profile only), Elchemy, or Microfactory (US-managed, India-made; website only — [microfactorymfg.com](https://microfactorymfg.com/)).

## Q3. Who are the incumbents and which parts of XELOR's scope do they cover?

### Takeaway
Incumbents cover XELOR's scope in pieces. Zetwerk, Karkhana and Fashinza run managed factory networks. OfBusiness, Moglix, Infra.Market, Bizongo, Tata nexarc and JSW-type players are category or indirect procurement marketplaces, often with financing attached. IndiaMART/Busy, Tally, Zoho, Vyapar and myBillBook own MSME accounting, GST and e-invoicing. Procol, Kissflow and ProcMart sell enterprise P2P and RFQ tools. Several incumbents are heading to IPO in 2026–27, so they will have capital to push down-market.

## Q4. Which government and ecosystem initiatives overlap with XELOR (ONDC B2B, GeM, TReDS, MSME Sampark, Udyam, 43B(h))?

### Takeaway
2026 tightened the payment rails. The MSMED (Amendment) Act 2026 (assent 13 Aug 2026) mandates TReDS for certain MSME receivables. CPSEs must settle MSME invoices through TReDS (notified 30 Jun 2026). Budget 2026 links GeM with TReDS. Together with Section 43B(h)'s 45-day rule, this raises the value of XELOR's MSME-payment compliance features. ONDC's B2B archetype and the MSE-TEAM scheme cover discovery and reputation but are not a procurement workflow.

### Cited Findings
- **MSMED (Amendment) Act, 2026**: Presidential assent on 13 Aug 2026. It mandates routing certain MSME receivable settlements through TReDS, tightens dispute timelines and revises penalties. — [SCC Online blog, 19 Aug 2026](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/08/19/micro-small-medium-enterprises-development-amendment-act-2026/); [VARINDIA (Rajya Sabha passage)](https://www.varindia.com/news/rajya-sabha-passes-msme-bill-2026-to-speed-up-payments-through-treds)
- **CPSE TReDS mandate**: a 30 Jun 2026 notification requires CPSEs to settle MSME invoices through RBI-authorised TReDS platforms. Budget 2026 also announced GeM–TReDS integration. TReDS discounting reached ~₹3.47 lakh crore in FY26. Some of these figures come from secondary or blog sources and should be checked against PIB. — [Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/union-budget-2026/budget-2026-treds-mandate-nbfcs-cml3hv2nz0001rkupo6arc1r1); [Bidz Professional](https://bidzprofessional.com/what-is-treds-gem-portal/); [DP Jadhav](https://www.dpjadhav.com/post/govt-mandates-treds-for-cpse-msme-payments-what-manufacturers-need-to-know)
- **Section 43B(h)** (Finance Act 2023): if a buyer pays a micro or small enterprise later than the MSMED Act s.15 limit (agreed date, but never more than 45 days from acceptance; 15 days if there is no agreement — sources differ, see below), the buyer can deduct the expense only in the year of actual payment. Medium enterprises are excluded. — [Taxmann FAQs](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/faqs-understanding-section-43b-h-of-the-income-tax-act); [TaxGuru clarification](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/finance-ministry-clarifies-section-43bh-45-day-msme-payment-rule.html). Note: one search summary said "45 days where there is no written agreement". Under MSMED s.15 the no-agreement limit is 15 days, so XELOR's rules engine should check the statute text.
- **MSME Samadhaan** is the government's delayed-payment monitoring portal. — [samadhaan.msme.gov.in](https://samadhaan.msme.gov.in/)
- **ONDC B2B / MSE-TEAM**: under RAMP, the MSE TEAM initiative aims to bring 5 lakh MSEs (2.5 lakh of them women-owned) onto ONDC. MSEs joining the B2B archetype are encouraged to bring existing buyers online to "build their reputation". ONDC has reportedly onboarded 5 lakh+ sellers. — [MSME RAMP guidelines (May 2026 PDF)](https://ramp.msme.gov.in/ramp/sites/default/files/2026-05/Approved-msme-team-guidelines_0.pdf); [Costbo](https://www.costbo.com/post/top-ondc-platforms-in-india-2026)
- **Udyam**: ~7.9 crore enterprises registered by Mar 2026 (4.72 crore on the Udyam Portal, 3.21 crore on Udyam Assist). — [Wikipedia: Udyam Registration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udyam_Registration)
- **Budget 2026-27** announced a ₹10,000 Cr SME Growth Fund and ₹2,000 Cr more for the Self-Reliant India Fund. — [NIIR blog (secondary)](https://www.niir.org/blog/budget-2026-msme-opportunities/)

### Inferences
- XELOR's 43B(h) and 45-day tracking, together with Udyam-status capture for each supplier, is now a compliance necessity and fits the 2026 TReDS push. A next step would be to export invoices to TReDS platforms (RXIL, M1xchange, Invoicemart).
- ONDC B2B's reputation idea is generic, platform-level reputation. XELOR's earned supplier record, based on GRN and inspection events, is more detailed and could in principle be published to ONDC as a credential.
- GeM is a government-buyer marketplace. For a pump maker it matters only if the factory sells to government (via GeM) or to CPSEs (now TReDS-routed).

### Gaps
- MSME Sampark (the job portal) and any 2026 changes to GeM for MSE vendors were not researched in depth.
- ONDC B2B order volumes for industrial categories: no reliable figure found (the ONDC site showed placeholder metrics).
- Primary PIB or Gazette text for the 2026 Amendment Act and CPSE notification was not fetched.

## Q5. Any evidence of startups doing "earned supplier records" or capacity sharing among Indian SME factories?

### Takeaway
There is little evidence. Supplier "trust scores" in India come from financial, GST and credit data (Recordent, BharatFinMetric, SignalX) or from platform KYC badges (IndianMSME, IndiaMART-style verification), not from measured delivery and inspection results. Idle-capacity sharing exists as directories and blog-level marketplaces (TheSupplier.in, AskCNC) and inside managed networks (Zetwerk, Karkhana). No company was found where factories share live capacity peer-to-peer from their own ERP.

### Cited Findings
- **Recordent "KnowYourBusinessScore"**: a 0–100 risk score combining credit behaviour, financial compliance, credit ratings, B2B transactions, legal checks and GST compliance. It rates buyer credit risk for MSMEs. — [SMEStreet](https://smestreet.in/finance-banking/knowyourbusinessscore-to-help-msmes-assess-buyer-credit-risk-12493875)
- **BharatFinMetric**: a single "Trust Score" built from financial data, for vendor ecosystems and lenders. — [bharatfinmetric.com](https://bharatfinmetric.com/)
- **SignalX**: AI vendor verification, including Udyam/MSME status checks. — [SignalX](https://signalx.ai/vendor-msme-status-check/)
- **IndianMSME**: KYC- and category-certified MSME profiles. Verified ratings and transaction badges are described as planned. — [indianmsme.in](https://www.indianmsme.in/)
- **Idle CNC capacity**: TheSupplier.in writes about buyers using idle MSME CNC capacity and cites 50,000+ CNC shops in India. AskCNC is a machinery, spares and job-work marketplace. — [TheSupplier.in](https://thesupplier.in/how-buyers-can-leverage-idle-machine-capacity-in-india-a-smart-way-to-cut-cnc-costs/); [AskCNC](https://askcnc.in/)
- **Managed capacity networks**: Karkhana.io is raising money to "ramp up supplier base". Fashinza ran 250+ factories before winding down. — [Outlook Startup](https://startup.outlookindia.com/sector/saas/karkhana-io-raises-6-3-million-in-series-a-funding-news-10220); [Clay](https://www.clay.com/dossier/fashinza-funding)
- **ONDC B2B** encourages sellers to bring existing buyers to "build their reputation", which is a nascent idea of network reputation. — [MSME RAMP guidelines](https://ramp.msme.gov.in/ramp/sites/default/files/2026-05/Approved-msme-team-guidelines_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- XELOR's "earned record" (GRN, inspection and on-time delivery written into the supplier's profile) and the "passport" a factory shows its customers appear new in the Indian SME context. Existing trust signals are financial or KYC-based rather than operational.
- Peer-to-peer capacity sharing between factories running the same ERP has no visible Indian competitor. The nearest models are intermediaries (Zetwerk, Karkhana) that capture the margin.
- Risk: scores built from operational data can be gamed or disputed. Recordent and credit bureaus show Indian MSMEs will accept scores, but adoption depends on whether buyers trust them.

### Gaps
- No search found Indian startups that score suppliers on QC rejection or OTIF. Enterprise P2P tools such as Procol and Kissflow probably include supplier scorecards (inferred from their "Supplier Agent" and supplier-management modules), but no source confirms OTIF/QC-based scoring.
- No evidence was found of Zetwerk or OfBusiness exposing supplier performance records to third parties.
