## 1. Do factories win business through short video and social content?

### 1a. China: Douyin, Kuaishou, 1688, Alibaba.com

- **Alibaba.com livestreaming (cross-border B2B).**
  - More than **40,000 businesses** had tried Alibaba.com's cross-border livestreaming, with **5,000–6,000 broadcast sessions a day**, about two years after launch (2023 coverage).
  - Yiwu O-Choice, a silk supplier, sold **$650,000 in one one-hour session**.
  - Zhejiang Aidu Garments reported **+38% YoY Q1 sales** after adopting livestreams, crediting virtual tours of its office, R&D centre and showroom with landing orders faster.
  - Sources: [China Services Info, May 2023](https://www.chinaservicesinfo.com/s/202305/10/WS645c554a498ea274927ba200/cross-border-livestream-marketing-boosts-trade.html), [China Daily WIC, May 2023](https://subsites.chinadaily.com.cn/wic/2023-05/08/c_884138.htm).
- **Alibaba.com live features.** Buyers can watch product demos and factory tours in real time. In 2020 Alibaba.com ran online trade shows with about 1,000 B2B livestream sessions. During the March 2024 Expo, active US SME buyers rose 14%. Sources: [PR Newswire, Alibaba.com March Expo](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/alibabacom-witnesses-notable-increase-in-us-based-sme-buyers-during-its-annual-march-expo-302096058.html), [Alibaba.com livestreaming: how it works](https://seller.alibaba.com/businessblogs/alibabacom-livestreaming-how-it-works-px001ubcm). Alibaba.com targeted GMV growth of 20% to about $60B in 2024 ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3265652/alibabas-b2b-e-commerce-platform-eyes-20-growth-2024-shrugs-rising-competition), [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2024/06/06/alibaba-expects-to-grow-b2b-gmv-in-2024/)).
- **Taobao (consumer) livestream conversion** is often quoted as averaging about 32% ([WalkTheChat](https://walkthechat.com/live-streaming-triples-wechat-conversion-rates-and-4-other-industry-reports/)). It is a vendor-reported B2C figure. **Do not use it as a B2B benchmark.**
- **Douyin (secondary).** These numbers come from a sourcing-agent blog. Confirm them against ByteDance or official data before quoting externally.
  - Factory livestreaming of production lines, warehouses and QC is described as routine, and it lets buyers check a supplier is legitimate.
  - Douyin's 2025 e-commerce GMV reportedly passed Pinduoduo's.
  - Merchants doing "store broadcasts" rose 115% YoY.
  - The Guangdong apparel belt saw 38% YoY growth in transactions as OEM-only factories began selling directly.
  - Source: [Looperbuy blog](https://blog.looperbuy.com/?p=24090) (secondary).
  - A Douyin-linked industrial trade show reportedly sold about **€46M of industrial goods in three days** ([Visable magazine](https://visable.com/en_uk/magazine/basics/b2b-marketing-with-tiktok)). The specific event is **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Kuaishou** announced plans for 100+ industrial-belt bases, 10,000 trained livestreamers, 1M+ livestream sessions, and an incubation project to take 100,000 merchants to ¥1M a year in sales ([China Film Insider](https://chinafilminsider.com/kuaishou-to-build-livestreaming-e-commerce-base-in-chengdu/), [KrASIA](https://amp.kr-asia.com/short-video-platform-kuaishou-unveils-ambitious-new-e-commerce-targets-amid-livestreaming-boom)). These are targets from around 2020–21, not results.
- **1688 (domestic wholesale).**
  - Puts "source factory" (源头工厂) listings first.
  - Reported more than **100M annual active buyers** by February 2025, with business buyers about 90% of transaction volume.
  - Launched "1688 Overseas" in March 2025.
  - Livestreamers and content creators are now a buyer segment on 1688.
  - Sources: [WorldFirst](https://www.worldfirst.com/af/?p=44348) (secondary), [Shenzhen DRC](https://drc.sz.gov.cn/ywb/szxw/tpxw/content/post_9892588.html).
- **Factory towns built on livestreaming.** In Baigou, Hebei (about 4,000 luggage makers), factories livestream from the floor, with workers acting as hosts and showing inspection, production and dispatch ([LeatherBiz](https://leatherbiz.com/News/156350)). China's livestream e-commerce revenue was about ¥5.8T in 2025 (same source).

### 1b. The "factory reels" trend on TikTok and Instagram

- **Hashtag scale.** #factory had more than **4.5B views** on TikTok, and related videos ran into the billions. Viewers call looping production clips "soothing" and "mesmerising". Sources: [Thomasnet Insights, "5 billion views and counting"](https://www.thomasnet.com/insights/5-billion-views-and-counting-why-are-factory-tiktoks-trending-thoughts-after-dark/), [nss magazine](https://test5.nssmag.com/en/fashion/36724/tiktok-tendance-usines-mode-manufacture-popularite).
- **Rest of World (2024): Chinese factories using TikTok and Instagram for B2B.**
  - Factories promote electronics components, lighting and industrial chemicals.
  - Buyers from the Americas, Europe and Africa have **visited factories after seeing the videos**.
  - A light-box salesman's comedic Instagram page reached about **450k followers**.
  - The key lesson is a tension: content that is too commercial gets no reach, and content that is too funny gets views but puts buyers off.
  - Source: [Rest of World, 2024](https://restofworld.org/2024/chinese-factory-tiktok-b2b-sales/).
- **Much factory content is made for factories, not by them.** Inquiries to agencies that run factory TikTok accounts reportedly **tripled** in a year, and many accounts now use English-speaking staff on camera. Sources: [Rest of World, 2021, "The Chinese content farms behind factory TikTok"](https://restofworld.org/2021/the-chinese-content-farms-behind-factory-tiktok/), [People Daily Digital](https://peopledaily.digital/business/tiktok-is-new-manufacturers-trade-weapon).
- **"Factory-direct" virality in 2025.** MIT Technology Review covered Chinese manufacturers going viral on TikTok in April 2025, during the US tariff fight ([MIT TR](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/04/28/1115876/why-chinese-manufacturers-are-going-viral-on-tiktok/), [Substack commentary](https://brendonbeebe.substack.com/p/tiktoks-factory-direct-trend-how)). **[UNVERIFIED, from memory, not fetched]:** much of that wave made dubious "we make luxury brand X" claims. It is a cautionary example of fake or misleading factory content.

## 2. B2B platforms that added video or social features

- **IndiaMART.**
  - Launched free **video profiles** in June 2010, with one- to two-minute clips of premises, infrastructure and manufacturing processes. The stated aim was to bridge the **trust gap** for remote buyers ([IndiaMART corporate](https://corporate.indiamart.com/2010/06/07/indiamart-launches-videop-rofiles-for-suppliers)).
  - Today suppliers can connect Instagram or YouTube to show videos in their catalog. Paid packages include 30-second mobile videos ([IndiaMART Videos](https://flips.indiamart.com), [Refrens package analysis](https://www.refrens.com/grow/detailed-overview-and-analysis-of-all-indiamart-packages/)).
  - The lesson: video sits **inside the catalog as proof**. It is not a separate social destination.
- **Alibaba.com.** Has livestreams, factory tours and a Verified Supplier programme ([Alibaba Verified Supplier](https://seller.alibaba.com/verified-supplier)). Results are in section 1a.
- **Made-in-China.com.**
  - Live factory tours and out-of-box reviews, semi-monthly live events, and a 360°/VR "SMART EXPO".
  - About 6M suppliers.
  - Sources: [TechNode Global](https://technode.global/?p=29866), [Business Wire 2021](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210812005405/en/Made-in-China.com-Source-Chinese-Products-in-a-Smart-Way).
- **Thomasnet (Xometry).**
  - Offers "Thomas Verified + Video", with product, project, capability, **factory-tour** and company-overview videos. Video production is bundled free with video-ad packages.
  - The Thomas Verified badge confirms North American operation, accurate data, a validated product and service list, and that the supplier is open to quoting.
  - Thomasnet reaches about 1.4M industrial buyers.
  - Sources: [Xometry IR](https://investors.xometry.com/news-releases/news-release-details/xometrys-thomas-expands-self-serve-listing-categories-its), [mfgrobots on Thomas Factory Tours](https://www.mfgrobots.com/Article/mfg/it/13612.html).
  - A "**32% uptick in engagement for suppliers with video tours**" also appeared in the search results but could not be traced to a primary source. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **Meesho.**
  - Runs "Video Finds" (shoppable short videos), "Live Shop", and a creator affiliate programme with 21,000+ creators.
  - Content commerce reportedly drove a **3x increase in order volume in 2024** and reached 14.5M users in year one ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/samosa-snippets/meesho-reels-strategy-engaging-audience-with-infotainment-and-interactive-content-1378835), [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/snippets/meesho-launches-creator-marketplace-to-pump-influencer-driven-sales-8736217)).
  - Meesho began as WhatsApp reselling ([TechCrunch 2016](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/17/meesho-wants-to-make-selling-through-whatsapp-more-efficient-and-less-painful)). That is the pattern of riding an existing social graph rather than building one.
- **Udaan.** No evidence of a video or social feed was found. Its value is in chat and negotiation inside a transaction app ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/companies/udaan)).
- **Faire.** No evidence of a video feed was found. It wins on net-60 terms, free returns and curation ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/faire)). The lesson: in wholesale, **financial terms and curation beat content**.
- **LinkedIn.** Has 160M+ Indian members (section 1c). It is the default B2B "social" layer for mid-size and larger Indian manufacturers, and Xelogram would compete with it for the buyer-side audience.

**What worked.** Every successful B2B video feature was attached to a **listing, RFQ or order flow**, and most were combined with **verification** (Alibaba Verified, Thomas Verified, IndiaMART TrustSEAL). None of them succeeded as a stand-alone feed.

| Network | Outcome | Lesson for Xelogram |

Meesho acquired it for **₹202 cr** (announced June 2026; three tranches closing by March 2027) to bolt the community onto commerce. Sources: [TechCrunch 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/09/15/tiger-global-led-100m-investment-makes-apna-indias-fastest-unicorn), [Orange Owl (secondary)](https://orangeowl.marketing/unicorn-chronicles/apna-success-story/) | Community helped as an **engagement layer on top of a utility (jobs)**. In September 2026 it relaunched **Business Pages** and a "Local Faves" business-discovery hub. Self-serve SMB ads are about 60% of revenue. Sources: [BNN Bloomberg](https://ampvideo.bnnbloomberg.ca/nextdoor-revenue-forecast-falls-short-as-advertisers-tighten-belts-1.1803673.amp.html), [Insider Monkey Q3 2025 transcript](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/nextdoor-holdings-inc-nysenxdr-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript-1641117/), [Briefglance](https://briefglance.com/articles/nextdoors-landmark-quarter-from-neighborhood-network-to-profit-engine) | Even a large local network is converging on **business discovery** as the useful core. A **local cluster feed** (for example Peenya) is a legitimate design idea. |
| **Houzz** (portfolio plus pros directory) | Professionals upload project photos that serve both as **their portfolio and as Houzz's SEO traffic**. Revenue comes from the Houzz Pro SaaS (CRM, leads, payments), ads and commerce. Reported revenue is over $400M, there are 2.7M+ pro profiles, and the 2019 valuation was about $4B. Sources: [Rapid Developers (secondary)](https://rapidevelopers.com/clone/houzz), [Sequoia, Houzz story](https://www.sequoiacap.com/company-story/houzz-story/), [YourStory interview with Adi Tatarko](https://yourstory.com/2017/03/adi-tatarko-houzz/) | **The closest analog.** It pairs a portfolio feed with a pros directory, monetised through **SaaS the pros already use**. XELOR's ERP is the equivalent of Houzz Pro. Its figures come from a secondary source, so confirm them before investor use. |
| **Behance** (Adobe) / **Dribbble** | Behance has 56M+ members and is tied to Adobe tools ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behance)). Dribbble was bootstrapped, is profitable through jobs, ads and subscriptions, had 12M users in 2020, and bought Creative Market ([TechCrunch 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/30/dribbble-a-bootstrapped-linkedin-for-designers-acquires-creative-market-grows-to-12m-users)) | Portfolio networks work when **the work product is the content** and when they are tied to the tool that produces it (Adobe) or to hiring demand. |
| **Polywork** (professional identity) | Raised $40M+ (a16z, the Collisons) and **closed on 31 January 2025**, returning the remaining capital. The founder said consumer social is "Level 900 difficulty". Sources: [Business Post](https://www.businesspost.ie/article/stripe-backed-irish-linkedin-challenger-polywork-to-close/), [The Currency](https://thecurrency.news/articles/174668/peter-johnston-sets-up-new-ai-start-up-as-he-winds-down-polywork/), [X post by Peter Johnston](https://x.com/multiplay3r/status/1873859669065978327) | A horizontal "better LinkedIn" fails without a must-have job to be done. |
| **Peerlist** (India, verified professional network) | Raised a $1.1M seed (Dharmesh Shah). About 20k users in early 2023, about 60% verified. Revenue about ₹1.7 cr in FY25. Sources: [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/23/hubspot-dharmesh-shah-peerlist-investment-linkedin-alternative), [Peerlist blog](https://blog.peerlist.io/peerlist-seed-funding/), [Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/peerlist/financials/) | Verification as a differentiator yields a small, credible niche, not scale. |

**Cold-start lessons:**
1. Seed content from work that already exists (Houzz project photos, Behance projects). Do not ask users to "create content".
2. Attach the network to a utility users already need: jobs for Apna, a ledger for Khatabook, Pro SaaS for Houzz, procurement and ERP for XELOR.
3. Monetise through the transaction or the SaaS, not through feed ads (Kirana Club, Nextdoor).
4. Horizontal social at Indian scale burns capital and creates moderation liability (Koo, ShareChat).

## 4. Existing "Instagram for manufacturers / factories / MSMEs" products

- **FactoryFeed: B2B Videos App** (iOS). It describes itself as a "B2B video-based social network" with video posts, networking and business profiles ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6636492628)). No traction, funding or user numbers were found. The app ID suggests a 2024 listing.
- **Biz Biz – B2B Videos** (iOS) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1621642477)) and **Live B2B** (iOS) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/mx/app/live-b2b/id6740695078)) are similar small apps with no visible traction.
- **Cretorial** (India) is an AI tool that creates social posts for MSMEs. It is a content tool, not a network ([Indian Conventions, Sept 2024](https://indianconventions.in/2024/09/26/cretorial-announces-the-launch-of-the-enterprise-version-of-its-ai-powered-app-that-simplifies-social-media-content-creation-for-millions-of-msmes-and-smbs/)).
- Searches for "Instagram for manufacturers", "Instagram for factories", "Instagram for MSMEs" and "TikTok for B2B / manufacturing" returned **no funded product with visible traction** ([search results included makersrow, Thomas blog and similar](https://blog.thomasnet.com/manufacturing-instagram-basics)).
- **Interpretation:** stand-alone "Instagram for factories" apps exist but appear to be sub-scale or abandoned. The scaled winners (Alibaba.com, 1688, Made-in-China, IndiaMART, Thomasnet, Douyin) put video **inside** marketplaces or general social apps. I found no evidence of a stand-alone factory social network that has succeeded. This absence may be partly a search limitation. **[UNVERIFIED that none exist]**

## 5. Cold-start and differentiation ideas, with supporting evidence

| Idea | Evidence or reasoning |
|---|---|
| **Auto-generated posts from verified business events.** Examples: "Delivered 4,000 machined housings on time to a Peenya OEM (verified GRN)", a new CNC commissioned, ISO or passport badge earned, inspection pass streak | Khatabook's growth came from transactional messages acting as social proof (sec. 3). SIDBI shows 87% of MSMEs do no social marketing (sec. 1c), so **manual posting will not happen at scale**. Houzz and Behance show the work output itself can be the content. This fits XELOR's earned record and factory passport, already identified as XELOR's most distinctive element (`reports/Accelerator startups like XELOR.md`). |
| **Verified-factory badge on every post** (Udyam/GST verified, geo-tagged visit, passport score) | Platforms that monetised trust all built verification: Alibaba Verified Supplier, Thomas Verified, IndiaMART TrustSEAL. IndiaMART explicitly launched video to bridge the **trust gap** (sec. 2). Douyin factory streams are valued because they let buyers **verify legitimacy** (sec. 1a). Content farms make factory videos (sec. 1b), so unverified video is weak evidence. |
| **Request-for-quote from a post** | Every successful B2B video feature sits next to a transaction action (sec. 2). Rest of World shows buyers act on videos by contacting or visiting factories (sec. 1b). |
| **Follow a supplier** and get updates on capacity, new machines and certifications | Supports XELOR's shared-capacity idea. It works like Houzz ideabooks, which keep users coming back. |
| **One-tap cross-posting to WhatsApp Status, Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube Shorts** | MSMEs already sit on WhatsApp (97% usage, 60% relying on WhatsApp Business). Instagram has an estimated 400M+ Indian users and LinkedIn 160M+. IndiaMART already **imports** Instagram and YouTube video into catalogs (sec. 2). Meesho grew by riding WhatsApp (sec. 2). Xelogram should **export to** these networks, not compete with them for attention. |
| **AI captions and voice-overs in Indian languages** (Kannada, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, English) | Kirana Club and ShareChat engagement was vernacular-first (sec. 3). Draft IT Rules on synthetic content would require **labelling AI-generated or AI-altered media** (sec. 7), so treat AI captions as fine and **AI-generated imagery of machines or facilities as a fraud risk** (label it, or ban it in proof posts). |

## 7. Risks

### 7a. IT Rules 2021: intermediary obligations

If XELOR hosts third-party posts, it is an **intermediary**. If the feed's main purpose is user interaction and sharing, it is likely a **social media intermediary** under the IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021. Safe harbour under s.79 of the IT Act depends on due diligence.

- **Applies to all intermediaries.**
  - **Grievance officer.** Complaints must be **acknowledged within 24 hours and resolved within 15 days**. Removal requests for obscene, sexual, privacy-invasive or child-harmful content must be actioned within **72 hours** (2022 amendment).
  - **Takedown within 36 hours** of actual knowledge through a court order or government notification (Rule 3(1)(d)).
  - **24 hours** to remove non-consensual intimate imagery or impersonation on a complaint by the affected person (Rule 3(2)(b)). A MeitY SOP of November 2025 reinforces this.
  - **72 hours** to give information or assistance to authorised agencies.
  - Users can appeal to the government **Grievance Appellate Committees** (2022 amendment).
  - Sources: [Stanford WILMAP](https://wilmap.stanford.edu/entries/information-technology-intermediary-guidelines-and-digital-media-ethics-code-rules-2021), [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-news-analysis/new-it-rules-2021), [Drishti IAS (2022 amendment)](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-news-analysis/information-technology-rules-2021-2), [The Leaflet, GAC](https://theleaflet.in/digital-rights/amended-it-rules-provide-for-grievance-redressal-social-media), [MediaNama 2022](https://www.medianama.com/2022/06/223-meity-india-it-rules-new-amendment/), [SCC Online, NCII SOP](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/11/12/meity-non-consensual-intimate-imagery-sop-24-hour-takedown-policy-scctimes/), [Khaitan ERGO](https://khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2025-10/ERGO%20-%20IT%20Rules%20Proposed%20Amendment%20-%2027%20October%202025.pdf).
- **Significant social media intermediary (SSMI): 50 lakh (5M) registered users in India.**
  - Must appoint a Chief Compliance Officer, a Nodal Contact Person and a Resident Grievance Officer, all **resident in India**.
  - Must meet further duties such as compliance reporting.
  - An MSME-only feed would be **far below 5M users for years**. Note that India has about 6.2 crore Udyam registrations ([Vision IAS on SIDBI](https://dce.visionias.in/news-today/2025-05-14/economics-(indian-economy)/understanding-indian-msme-sector-progress-and-challenges-report-released-by-sidbi)), so the ceiling is theoretically reachable but not a near-term concern.
- **Synthetic or AI content.**
  - MeitY published a **draft amendment in October 2025**. Users would declare whether content is AI-generated, intermediaries would **label** synthetic content (the draft proposed a label covering at least 10% of the display area), and SSMIs would verify declarations. The comment deadline was extended to 13 November 2025 ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/breaking-meity-releases-draft-it-rules-targets-deepfakes-synthetic-content-82926.htm), [Storyboard18, deadline](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/meity-extends-deadline-for-stakeholder-comments-on-draft-ai-deepfake-content-rules-to-november-13-83802.htm), [Vision IAS](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/monthly-magazine/2025-11-12/polity-and-governance/it-amendment-rules-2025), [Khaitan ERGO](https://khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2025-10/ERGO%20-%20IT%20Rules%20Proposed%20Amendment%20-%2027%20October%202025.pdf)).
  - **[UNVERIFIED]:** the final notified text and timelines, and any 2026 change to takedown windows. A "3-hour" takedown figure appeared in the search results but relates to election-campaign advisories for party handles, not to general intermediary duties. Check with counsel before launch.
- **Engineering impact.** XELOR's existing controls (audit logging, registered permissions, domain-owned writes) help with evidence trails and takedown records. A feed would need these additions:
  - a report button and a moderation queue with SLA timers (24h, 36h, 72h, 15 days);
  - a published grievance officer contact;
  - terms of use and a content policy;
  - takedown audit logs;
  - AI-content labels.

### 7b. Moderation burden

- Koo shows that UGC liability deters acquirers and partners (sec. 3).
- In a factory feed the main risks are not political speech. They are:
  1. **Fake or borrowed footage.** Content farms make factory videos (sec. 1b), and the 2025 "factory-direct" TikTok wave showed how misleading claims spread **[UNVERIFIED detail]**.
  2. **Counterfeit or IP-infringing products**, for example parts bearing OEM logos.
  3. **Customer-confidential parts or drawings** shown without permission, a real NDA risk in Peenya's auto and aero supply chain. **[my inference]**
  4. Spam and price-dumping posts.
- **Mitigation:**
  - Make **verified-event posts the default content type**. Free-form uploads would be secondary and limited to verified factories.
  - Check images against XELOR site-visit or passport photos.
  - Run an "OEM logo / drawing detected" warning before publishing.
  - Keep volume low with no anonymous accounts. Every poster would be a KYC'd business on GST or Udyam.

### 7c. Competitors poaching suppliers from a public feed

- A public feed of verified, high-performing suppliers is a **free lead list for IndiaMART, Zetwerk, OfBusiness, Procol and rival buyers**. It would leak exactly the earned-record asset that XELOR's moat depends on.
- Factories may also not want competitors to see their machines, utilisation or customers. **[inference]**
- **Mitigation:**
  - **Tiered visibility**: public showcase (capabilities, machines, certifications), buyer-only detail (passport score, delivery record), and private (customer names, volumes).
  - Supplier opt-in for each post.
  - Aggregate badges such as "98% on-time across 40 verified deliveries", not customer names.
  - Rate-limit scraping and require login for supplier lists.

### 7d. Attention competition with Instagram, YouTube and WhatsApp

- **The odds are against a separate feed.**
  - Instagram has an estimated 400M+ Indian users and is going Reels-first in India.
  - WhatsApp is used by about 97% of MSMEs.
  - YouTube Shorts is near-universal among Indian 18–44s (sec. 1c).
  - Only 13% of MSMEs do social marketing at all (SIDBI).
  - A separate feed asks factories to create **more** content for a **smaller** audience.
- **Mitigation:** treat XELOR as the **source of verified proof** and the external networks as **distribution** (cross-post with a "Verified on XELOR" deep link back to RFQ). Measure success by **RFQs started from posts and badges**, not by time spent or DAU.

## Synthesis and verdict

**Is Xelogram a good addition? Yes, in a narrow form. No, as an "Instagram for MSMEs".**

**What the evidence supports:**
- Video and social content do win factory business. This is shown at scale in China (Alibaba.com livestreams, Douyin factory streams, factory TikTok/Instagram leading to buyer visits).
- The winning pattern everywhere is **video plus verification plus a transaction button**, embedded in a marketplace or riding a giant social graph. Examples: Alibaba.com, IndiaMART video profiles, Thomas Verified + Video, Meesho Video Finds.

**What the evidence argues against:**
1. Indian MSMEs do not habitually create marketing content (13% per SIDBI).
2. Stand-alone vertical or horizontal social networks rarely monetise. Kirana Club was sold with ₹15.8 cr revenue, loss-making. Polywork closed. Koo closed. ShareChat burned capital for years.
3. Standalone "Instagram for factories" apps (FactoryFeed and others) show no traction.
4. A public feed leaks XELOR's moat to competitors.
5. The "-gram" name invites a Meta objection.

**Recommended form ("XELOR Proof Feed" / "Showcase", not "Xelogram"):**
1. **Auto-generated, verified posts** from ERP and network events: verified deliveries, inspection pass streaks, passport badges, new machines, certifications. The supplier approves with one tap, and AI writes captions in Kannada, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu or English.
2. **Every post carries the verified badge and a "Request quote" or "Check capacity" button** that goes into the existing RFQ flow.
3. **Follow supplier** and a **cluster feed** (Peenya first).
4. **Cross-post outward** to WhatsApp Status, Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube Shorts, with a verified deep link. Do not compete for attention.
5. **Tiered visibility** (public, buyer-only, private), so the earned record is not handed to competitors.
6. **Minimal free-form UGC at launch.** Allow it only for verified factories, with logo and drawing checks, AI-image labelling and an IT-Rules-compliant grievance workflow from day one.
7. **Success metric:** RFQs and awarded POs attributable to posts. Not DAU.

**Sequencing.** Build after the core loop (RFQ, PO, earned record, passport) has enough real events to generate content. In the Peenya pilot, start with a **shareable verified factory profile card** ("passport card") that suppliers forward on WhatsApp. It is the cheapest test of whether proof content creates inbound RFQs.
