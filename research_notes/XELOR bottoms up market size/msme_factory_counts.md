Method note: WebSearch worked; direct page fetches to mospi.gov.in, sansad.in, ibef.org, knnindia.co.in, kashmirlife.net and visionias.in were all blocked by the network egress proxy. Figures below therefore come from (a) search-result snippets of the named pages (cited to that page's URL), (b) files already in this repo, which themselves cite the MoSPI PDF, and (c) background knowledge, which is always marked **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge]**. ## Q1. Udyam Registration: total, micro/small/medium split, manufacturing vs services, Udyam Assist

### Takeaway
The latest figure found is **9.17 crore MSMEs on Udyam Registration Portal (URP) plus Udyam Assist Platform (UAP) as of 31 July 2026**. That splits into **9,11,07,705 micro, 5,40,908 small and 41,722 medium**, and into **1,83,17,965 manufacturing (20.0%), 3,47,33,886 services and 3,86,38,484 trading**. Only about 5.8 lakh small plus medium enterprises exist across all sectors, so XELOR's paying universe is tiny relative to the headline 9 crore.

### Cited Findings
- **As of 31 July 2026:** 9.16 crore MSMEs registered on URP + UAP, made up of 9,11,07,705 micro, 5,40,908 small and 41,722 medium enterprises. The sector split is 1,83,17,965 manufacturing, 3,47,33,886 services and 3,86,38,484 trading — [Kashmir Life, "J&K records over 9.64 lakh MSME registrations since 2020" (search snippet; page fetch blocked)](https://kashmirlife.net/jammu-kashmir-records-over-9-64-lakh-msme-registrations-since-2020-447368/)
  - Arithmetic check: the size split sums to 9,16,90,335 and the sector split also sums to 9,16,90,335. The two splits are internally consistent, so the precise total is **≈9.17 crore**.
- **As of 30 June 2026:** "over 8.84 crore" enterprises on URP + UAP, per MoS MSME Shobha Karandlaje in Parliament. Registrations rose from 28.13 lakh in 2020-21 (a partial year) to over 2.48 crore in 2023-24, "before moderating slightly in subsequent years" — [KNN India (search snippet)](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/msme/over-884-crore-msmes-registered-on-udyam-platforms-mos-karandlaje). The underlying Lok Sabha answer is likely [eparlib AU664](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2987877/1/AU664_J32stV.pdf), but its content was not readable.
- **As of 28 February 2026:** over 7.83 crore enterprises on URP + UAP, up from 0.79 crore in FY22 — [IBEF (search snippet)](https://www.ibef.org/news/over-7-83-crore-enterprises-registered-on-udyam-platforms-indicating-strong-msme-formalisation-growth)
- **Launch dates:** URP launched 1 July 2020 (online, paperless, self-declared). UAP launched 11 January 2023 for informal enterprises that cannot register on URP because they lack a PAN or GSTIN — [KNN India (search snippet)](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/msme/over-884-crore-msmes-registered-on-udyam-platforms-mos-karandlaje)
- **An older reading:** "Over 7.28 crore MSMEs have registered on Udyam Registration Portal: Minister." The date was not visible in the snippet — [Morung Express](https://morungexpress.com/over-728-crore-msmes-have-registered-on-udyam-registration-portal-minister)

### Inferences
- **Growth rate:** the series runs 7.83 crore (Feb-26) → 8.84 crore (Jun-26) → 9.17 crore (Jul-26). That is roughly 25–33 lakh new registrations a month, mostly UAP micro, so the headline number grows fast but is not a sizing input.
- **Small + medium = 5,82,630 (all sectors, Jul-2026).** UAP is for informal micro units, so effectively all small and medium enterprises sit on URP. If the 20% manufacturing share applied uniformly, manufacturing small + medium would be about 1.17 lakh. The manufacturing share among small and medium is probably higher than the overall 20%, because trading and services dominate UAP micro. **That share is an assumption, not a cited figure.**
- **Medium enterprises (all sectors) = 41,722.** This is the closest Udyam analogue to a "100+ worker factory", but it counts legal entities defined by investment and turnover, not by headcount.
- **The 2025 thresholds:** MSME classification thresholds were revised upward effective 1 April 2025. Investment/turnover limits became micro ₹2.5 Cr / ₹10 Cr, small ₹25 Cr / ₹100 Cr and medium ₹125 Cr / ₹500 Cr **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge; Union Budget 2025-26 announcement]**. Some secondary pages still quote the old medium limits of ₹50 Cr investment / ₹250 Cr turnover. A higher ceiling means more firms now qualify as "medium".

### Gaps
- **No split of small and medium by sector.** I found no source splitting the 5,40,908 small and 41,722 medium enterprises into manufacturing, services and trading. This is the single most useful Udyam number for XELOR. It is likely in a Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha answer or on the Udyam dashboard (udyamregistration.gov.in), which I could not fetch.
- **No URP vs UAP split.** The split of the 9.17 crore between URP and UAP as of 2026 was not found. UAP is believed to be the larger share **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge]**.
- **MSME Annual Report:** Ministry of MSME Annual Report 2025-26 figures were not retrieved.

## Q2. ASI latest round: factories in operation and distribution by persons engaged (100+, 20–99)

### Takeaway
The latest round found is **ASI 2023-24**, released 27 August 2025. It reports **2,60,061 total factories, of which 2,12,990 were in operation**, and about 1.95 crore persons engaged. Factories with 100+ employees total **47,762**: 19,623 in the 100–199 band and 28,139 at 200+. **No ASI 2024-25 release was found** as of October 2026. I could not source counts for the 20–99 and 50–99 bands.

### Cited Findings
- **Release:** MoSPI released ASI 2023-24 results on 27 August 2025 as a press note plus seven website tables — [Vision IAS (search snippet)](https://dce.visionias.in/monthly-magazine/2025-11-12/economy/annual-survey-of-industries-asi-for-2023-24)
- **Total factories:** 2,60,061 in ASI 2023-24 — [TNPSC Thervupettagam (search snippet)](https://www.tnpscthervupettagam.com/currentaffairs-detail/annual-survey-of-industries-2023%E2%80%9324/). For comparison, ASI 2022-23 had 2.53 lakh (up from 2.49 lakh in 2021-22) — [Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/the-annual-survey-of-industries-asi-for-2022-23/)
- **Employment:** industrial employment rose 5.92% in 2023-24 to 1.95 crore, from 1.84 crore — [All Things Talent, 28 Aug 2025](https://allthingstalent.org/industrial-employment-rises-5-92-to-1-95-crore-as-survey-highlights-robust-growth/2025/08/28/). A microdata catalogue snippet gives total persons engaged as 1,95,89,131 — [microdata.gov.in](https://microdata.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/256/download/3934)
- **Factories in operation and the 100+ bands:** 2,12,990 operating factories. By employment band: 100–199 = 19,623; 200–499 = 15,299; 500–999 = 6,552; 1000–1999 = 3,269; 2000–4999 = 2,120; 5000+ = 899. The 200+ subtotal is 28,139 and the 100+ total is **47,762**. The 100–199 band averages ₹74.14 Cr output and 123.5 persons engaged. Source: MoSPI, *ASI Summary Results 2023-24*, Statements 13A and 16A — [MoSPI PDF, as cited in repo file `platform/docs/reports/xelor-business-revenue-model.html` (lines 313, 462, 546) and hard-coded in `platform/apps/web/scripts/validation/check-business-revenue-model.mjs`](https://mospi.gov.in/uploads/publications_reports/publications_reports1759229789833_2b8ed8ad-314e-46b7-b123-b08cb17f1233_ASI_Summary_Results_2023-24C.pdf). I could not re-fetch the PDF this session (mospi.gov.in is blocked), so these band figures are repo-cited, not independently re-verified.
- **ASI 2024-25:** a search for its results found nothing. The latest available round is still 2023-24 — [search: MoSPI press release listing](https://mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/press_release/PR_ASI_30102024.pdf) (that link is the ASI 2022-23 press release dated 30 Oct 2024).

### Inferences
- **Factories under 100 employees (2023-24):** 2,12,990 − 47,762 = **1,65,228** operating factories. This is derived arithmetic from the figures above.
- **Share with 100+:** 22.4% of operating factories have 100+ employees.
- **The 50–99 band:** historical ASI rounds show it at roughly 1.3–1.6× the 100–199 band. That implies **~25,000–31,000 factories with 50–99 employees** in 2023-24 **[UNVERIFIED – background-knowledge ratio; do not cite]**.
- **The 20–99 band:** this would add the 20–49 band, which is larger still. A plausible order of magnitude for 20–99 is **~60,000–90,000 factories** **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge; do not cite]**.
- **What ASI counts:** ASI covers registered factories, meaning those with 10+ workers using power or 20+ without power under the Factories Act. Small Peenya job shops with fewer than 10 workers are therefore outside ASI but inside Udyam.
- **Headcount band ≠ MSME status:** the 100–199 band's ₹74 Cr average output fits inside the (revised) medium-enterprise turnover cap. Many 200+ factories are large enterprises, or plants of large groups.

### Gaps
- **Exact lower bands not obtained:** factory counts for 0–49, 20–49 and 50–99 (and persons engaged per band) in ASI 2023-24. These are in the same MoSPI Summary Results PDF (Statement 13A/16A). Someone needs to open it on an unblocked network.
- **ASI 2024-25 not yet located:** it would normally appear in Aug–Oct 2026. It should be checked for before finalising.

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

It was not readable this session — [AIKosh](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/datasets/details/district_level_manufacturing_msme_registered_enterprises_under_udyam_registration.html)

### Inferences
- **Working range:** manufacturing small enterprises are likely **~1.1–2.0 lakh** and manufacturing medium enterprises **~10,000–20,000**. This assumes a manufacturing share of 20–35% for URP small and medium registrants **[UNVERIFIED – assumption]**. Use it only as a placeholder until the official split is found.
- **Cross-check against ASI:** ASI shows 47,762 factories with 100+ employees, against only 41,722 Udyam medium enterprises across all sectors. Most 100+ factories are therefore either "small" by investment/turnover, large enterprises, or not registered on Udyam. Headcount and Udyam class are not interchangeable.

### Gaps
- **The manufacturing-only small/medium count is the main gap.** Possible sources: the Udyam dashboard, a parliament question (search "Udyam medium manufacturing enterprises state-wise"), or the MSME Annual Report 2025-26.

## Q6. Cross-check of the investor brief: "47,762 factories with 100+ people", "₹2,866 Cr ceiling", "5,000 named accounts"

### Takeaway
**47,762 is correctly derived from ASI 2023-24.** It is the sum of the six employment bands of 100+ (19,623 + 28,139), as cited in the repo's revenue model. **₹2,866 Cr = 47,762 × ₹6 lakh per plant per year.** **"5,000 named accounts" is a management assumption with no external source.** The brief and the revenue model use inconsistent prices (₹6 L vs ₹18 L/₹54 L).

### Cited Findings
- **The brief's claim:** `deliverables/XELOR-Investor-Brief.html` line 1069 reads "The ceiling 47,762 factories with 100+ people ₹2,866 Cr / yr". Line 1070 reads "First target list 5,000 named accounts ₹300 Cr / yr", at "₹50,000 a month per plant… ₹6.0 L" per year. Source: repo file, no URL.
- **Where 47,762 comes from:** `platform/apps/web/scripts/validation/check-business-revenue-model.mjs` hard-codes the ASI bands (100–199: 19,623; 200–499: 15,299; 500–999: 6,552; 1000–1999: 3,269; 2000–4999: 2,120; 5000+: 899). It asserts `tamSites === 47762`. `platform/docs/reports/xelor-business-revenue-model.html` cites "MoSPI, ASI Summary Results 2023–24, Statements 13A and 16A" — [MoSPI PDF](https://mospi.gov.in/uploads/publications_reports/publications_reports1759229789833_2b8ed8ad-314e-46b7-b123-b08cb17f1233_ASI_Summary_Results_2023-24C.pdf)
- **Arithmetic:** 47,762 × ₹6.0 L = ₹2,865.7 Cr ≈ ₹2,866 Cr, and 5,000 × ₹6.0 L = ₹300 Cr. Both match the brief.
- **5,000 is the model's TOM assumption:** in the revenue model, 5,000 is `tom_site_assumption` (TOM ₹1,260 Cr = 5,000 × ₹25.2 L blended ACV). Separately, the model's own SAM site count (27,135) is labelled "DENOMINATOR UNSOURCED", because ASI publishes no output-size-class statement. Source: repo file `platform/docs/reports/xelor-business-revenue-model.html` lines 324 and 341.

### Inferences
- **Headline numbers:** 47,762 is defensible as "ASI 2023-24 operating factories with 100+ employees". It counts **factory sites, not companies**, and "employees/persons engaged" should be stated precisely. Much of the 200+ portion (28,139) is large-enterprise and group plants, not MSMEs. An "MSME 100+" framing should lean on the 19,623 (100–199) band, or on 100–499 (34,922).
- **Repo inconsistency:** the brief prices at ₹6 L/plant/yr, while the revenue model uses ₹18 L (MSME) and ₹54 L (Multi-Plant). That gives a TAM of ₹18,727 Cr on the same 47,762 sites. Reconcile before external use.
- **TAM base for a ₹6 L/yr product:** at that price, the factories with 20–99 employees (unsourced; perhaps 60k–90k) become relevant TAM. Adding them would roughly double or triple the site count. The 100+ ceiling is therefore conservative for that price point.

### Gaps
- **5,000 named accounts:** no external basis was found. It needs to be built bottom-up from cluster lists (Peenya + Hosur + Coimbatore + Belagavi + Pune/Chennai, etc.) filtered by headcount and turnover.
- **Band figures not re-verified:** the ASI band figures were not independently re-fetched this session (MoSPI was blocked). Confirm Statement 13A/16A values directly.
