## Q1. How big is TReDS: volume (FY25, FY26), participants, platforms, pricing, and the 2026 changes?

### Takeaway
TReDS discounted about **₹2.35 lakh crore in FY2024-25** and about **₹3.47 lakh crore in FY2025-26**. Five RBI-authorised platforms share the volume, but RXIL, M1xchange and Invoicemart handle most of it. Each of the two largest platforms registered more than 60,000 MSME sellers by March 2026. Several 2026 changes push volume up further: the RBI TReDS Master Direction (June 2026), mandatory TReDS settlement by CPSEs, the MSMED Amendment Act (August 2026) and CGTMSE's 75% guarantee cover (live 25 September 2026). XELOR's verified delivery records fit best where invoices are financed on MSE-to-MSE trade, which is the segment the guarantee now covers.

**Pricing**
- **Discount rates:** TReDS financing typically costs **7–11% p.a.**, because pricing follows the corporate buyer's credit. Fee schedules differ by platform and should be checked with each one.

**2026 changes**
- **RBI TReDS Master Direction (final, 23 June 2026):**
  - consolidates all earlier instructions
  - **removes mandatory seller due diligence at platform level**, while operators must still validate MSME status and pay only into the seller's account
  - allows insurance on transactions, with the premium not charged to the seller
  - admits insurers and government credit-guarantee funds as participants
  - lets financiers obtain cover from any government credit-guarantee trust
  - sets **₹25 crore minimum net worth** for operators, with existing operators given until 31 March 2028
  - — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbis-treds-master-direction-2026-means-msmes-working-capital.html); [M1xchange blog](https://www.m1xchange.com/thought-xchange/rbi-rationalises-treds-rules-in-2026-one-master-direction-a-complete-overhaul/); [C4S Courses](https://c4scourses.in/banking-finance/rbi-issues-final-treds-master-direction-2026/)
- **CGTMSE guarantee on TReDS (live 25 September 2026, announced by the MSME Ministry):**
  - covers **75% of the amount in default** on eligible invoices where **both buyer and seller are micro or small enterprises**
  - limits: **₹2 crore per MSE seller and ₹10 crore per MSE buyer**, revolving
  - live on **M1xchange, RXIL and DTX (KredX)**
  - fee calculation and guarantee cover notes are digitised
  - — [Busy.in](https://busy.in/business-updates/cgtmse-guarantee-cover-on-treds-faster-invoice-cash-for-micro-and-small-enterprises/); [SuperKalam / PIB digest, 26 Sep 2026](https://superkalam.com/current-affairs/26-09-2026/cgtmse-guarantee-cover-goes-live-on-treds-to-strengthen-msme-working-capital-access-3999fe3b-5ae8-4421-8286-b7255c09a411); [The Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/government-guarantee-goes-live-for-small-firms-invoice-financing-on-treds); [M1xchange blog](https://www.m1xchange.com/thought-xchange/cgtmse-and-treds-understanding-the-2026-rbi-direction-on-guarantee-cover/)
- **MSMED (Amendment) Act 2026 (assent 13 August 2026):**
  - every CPSE must route payments for goods and services bought from MSMEs through TReDS, disclose TReDS-routed settlements, and obtain an annual statutory-auditor compliance certificate
  - adds time-bound mediation (90 days) and arbitration stages
  - keeps the 45-day payment cap
  - commencement depends on government notification
  - — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/08/19/micro-small-medium-enterprises-development-amendment-act-2026/) (via repo note 04); [TheMunim](https://themunim.com/blog/msme-new-rules-and-amendments/); [NIIR](https://www.niir.org/blog/msmed-amendment-bill-2026/); [BharatLaw.ai](https://www.bharatlaw.ai/blog/twenty-years-later-the-msmed-amendment-act-2026-rewrites-indias-rules-for-msme-payments-and-disputes)
- **CPSEs to settle MSME invoices on TReDS from 30 June 2026.** — [DP Jadhav](https://www.dpjadhav.com/post/govt-mandates-treds-for-cpse-msme-payments-what-manufacturers-need-to-know) (via repo note 04)
- **Mandatory onboarding threshold:** companies with turnover above **₹250 crore** (down from ₹500 crore), and all CPSEs, had to register on TReDS by **31 March 2025**, under the MCA notification of 7 November 2024. — [VJM Global](https://www.vjmglobal.com/blog/companies-with-turnover-more-than-inr-250-crores-have-to-get-registered-on-treds-by-31st-march-2025) (via repo note 02)

### Inferences
- **Growth:** about 1.48x year on year in FY26 (₹3.47 / ₹2.35 lakh crore). The rise from ₹0.4 lakh crore in FY22 is roughly 8.7x in four years, a CAGR of about 72%.
- **Platform split, FY26:** M1xchange (more than ₹1 lakh crore) plus RXIL (target ₹1.25 lakh crore; the March 2026 month alone was about ₹14,500 crore) probably make up about ₹2.2–2.3 lakh crore. That leaves about ₹1.1–1.2 lakh crore for Invoicemart, C2treds and DTX. This is consistent with Invoicemart's March 2026 month of about ₹10,800 crore.
- **Seller-registration counts overlap.** RXIL plus Invoicemart alone total about 1.34 lakh in March 2026. An MSME can register on several platforms, so the sum across platforms overstates unique MSMEs. The "about 1.35 lakh" figure is best read as FY25-end registrations, not unique sellers in 2026.
- **Discount-income pool, FY26 (illustrative):** discount cost ≈ volume × annual rate × tenor/365. Assumptions: average tenor 60–90 days **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge; TReDS invoices are typically 45–90 days]** and a rate of 8–10% p.a. That puts the discount cost at about **1.3–2.5% of invoice value**, so financiers earned roughly **₹4,500–8,600 crore** of discount income on ₹3.47 lakh crore in FY26. On the same tenor, platform transaction fees of 0.25–0.30% p.a. come to only about **0.04–0.07% of value, roughly ₹140–250 crore across all platforms**. Any referral share has to come out of these two pools.
- **Fit with XELOR.** The CGTMSE cover only applies to **MSE-to-MSE** trade, which is exactly the tier-2/tier-3 supplier-to-supplier trade that large-buyer TReDS has not reached. Financiers will still have to judge invoice genuineness, so XELOR's verified delivery and GRN records are most valuable in this segment.

### Gaps
- No official RBI annual totals for FY25 or FY26 were opened. ₹2.35 lakh crore and ₹3.47 lakh crore come from press summaries. Confirm them against the RBI Annual Report 2025-26 (payment-systems chapter) or by summing the monthly entity-wise TReDS statistics.
- No March 2026 seller, buyer or volume data was found for M1xchange, C2treds or DTX.
- No platform's official fee schedule was found. The 0.25–0.30% p.a. figure comes from secondary explainers.
- No source gave average invoice tenor or the average discount rate realised on TReDS in FY26.
- The exact notified commencement dates for the MSMED Amendment Act 2026 provisions were not confirmed.
- I did not find whether the CGTMSE fee is borne by the financier or passed through.

## Q2. How large are the MSME credit gap and the delayed-payment receivables?

### Takeaway
SIDBI (2025) puts the addressable MSME credit gap at about **₹30 lakh crore (24%)**. Delayed payments outstanding to MSMEs were about **₹7.34 lakh crore** at March 2024 (GAME/FISME/C2FO). Formal disputes are much smaller: MSME Samadhaan has logged **₹55,244 crore** across 2.57 lakh applications, of which ₹20,979 crore is still pending (August 2026). TReDS's ₹3.47 lakh crore a year is small against these pools.

### Cited Findings
- **SIDBI, "Understanding Indian MSME Sector: Progress and Challenges" (May 2025, unabridged July 2025):** the addressable credit gap is about **₹30 lakh crore, 24% of demand**. It is 27% in services and 35% for women-owned MSMEs. Credit is the top challenge, cited by 22% of respondents. — [SIDBI report PDF](https://www.sidbi.in/uploads/Understanding_Indian_MSME_sector_Progress_and_Challenges_13_05_25_Final.pdf); [Vision IAS summary](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2025-05-14/economy/understanding-indian-msme-sector-progress-and-challenges-report-released-by-sidbi) (via repo note 02)
- Older and larger estimate: only 19% of MSME credit demand was met formally by FY21, leaving about **₹80 lakh crore unmet**. — [Institute for Competitiveness](https://www.competitiveness.in/enhancing-msmes-competitiveness-in-india) (via repo note 02)
- M1xchange headlines cite a **₹25 lakh crore** MSME credit gap. — [M1xchange news](https://www.m1xchange.com/rs-25-lakh-crore-msme-credit-gap-treds-has-sparked-hope-for-small-businesses-but-whats-holding-it-back/)
- **Delayed payments stock:** **₹7.34 lakh crore at March 2024** (inflation-adjusted), down from ₹8.27 lakh crore in 2023 and about ₹10 lakh crore earlier. About 40% is owed by government agencies and PSUs. Source: GAME, FISME and C2FO, "MSMEs' Access to Finance and Timely Payments" (Delayed Payments Report 3.0). — [Storyboard18](https://storyboard18.com/how-it-works/delayed-payments-to-msmes-fall-to-rs-7-34-lakh-crore-report-84822.htm); [FISME](https://fisme.org.in/study/delayed-payments-to-msmes-decline-from-rs-10-lakh-cr-to-rs-7-lakh-cr-but-challenges-persist-game-fisme-report/)
- **MSME Samadhaan (as of 14 August 2026):** **2,56,892 applications covering ₹55,244 crore**. **₹20,979 crore is still pending**, and about 40,580 cases (16%) have been unresolved for more than a year. — [CRISIL Intelligence press release (Aug 2026)](https://intelligence.crisil.com/en/homepage/newsroom/press-releases/2026/08/executed-well-msme-bill-can-be-an-ibc-moment-for-delayed-payments.html) (via repo note 02)
- **CGTMSE:** record approvals of more than ₹3 lakh crore in FY25. The ceiling rose from ₹5 crore to ₹10 crore from 1 April 2025. — [CGTMSE](https://cgtmse.in/Blogs/VS/4); [LatestLY / Lok Sabha reply](https://www.latestly.com/agency-news/india-news-centre-implements-credit-guarantee-scheme-for-msmes-mos-informs-lok-sabha-7529327.html/amp) (via repo note 02)

### Inferences
- **Penetration:** FY26 TReDS volume (₹3.47 lakh crore a year, revolving every 2–3 months) is about 12% of the SIDBI gap if compared on a flow basis. Average TReDS balances outstanding are probably only about ₹0.6–0.9 lakh crore (₹3.47 lakh crore × 60–90/365), which is **about 1% of the ₹7.34 lakh crore delayed-payment stock**. There is a lot of headroom.
- **Samadhaan versus total delays:** the Samadhaan figure (₹55,244 crore cumulative since 2017) is under 8% of the delayed-payment stock. Most delayed receivables never reach a formal dispute, so a pitch should use **₹7.34 lakh crore** for "receivables stuck" and **₹55k crore** for "formally disputed".

## Q3. What referral or LSP fees are typical in India under the RBI Digital Lending Directions 2025?

### Takeaway
RBI does not set a percentage for LSP or referral fees. The 2025 Directions require the **regulated entity, not the borrower, to pay the LSP**, and require direct fund flows. Market practice for MSME term or working-capital loan referrals is about **1–3% of the disbursed amount**. Supply-chain-finance and invoice-discounting facilitators earn far less: a fraction of the discount or interest, in basis points. For TReDS-style referrals, XELOR should model **basis points of invoice value**, not loan-DSA percentages.

### Cited Findings
- **Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025:** circular RBI/2025-26/36, dated 8 May 2025, consolidating the 2022 guidelines. It covers LSP oversight, multi-lender LSP disclosure, disbursal protocols and data protection. — [RBI notification](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12848); [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/rbi-notifies-digital-lending-directions/); [ELP Law update (May 2025)](https://elplaw.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Regulatory-Update-on-RBIs-Consolidated-Digital-Lending-Framework-%E2%80%93-May-2025.pdf)
- Repo note 04 records these LSP duties: no pass-through or pool accounts, fees paid by the regulated entity rather than charged to the borrower, partner disclosure, unbiased display of all matching offers for multi-lender LSPs, and a grievance officer. — `research_notes/XELOR Marketplace and Xelogram/04_connect_only_model_and_regulation.md` §6.2 (tagged there as background, not checked against the primary text).
- **DSA/referral commission benchmarks:**
  - typically **1–3% of the loan amount**, depending on loan type and lender
  - Lendingkart (NBFC) pays at least **1%** on a ₹10 lakh business loan, **1.5%** above ₹30 lakh and **2%** above ₹50 lakh; about 90% is paid on first disbursal
  - — [Lendingkart blog](https://www.lendingkart.com/blog/earn-commission-on-the-same-day-by-partnering-with-lendingkart-business-loan/); [Lendingkart – DSA](https://www.lendingkart.com/blog/direct-selling-agents/)
- **Vayana Network (supply-chain-finance platform):** enables bank and NBFC credit for up to 90 days for a fee of **20–40 bps**, on interest priced at 12–15%. The search summary does not make clear whether the bps are of the invoice amount or of the interest. FY24 revenue was about ₹121.6 crore. — [Business Standard (2017)](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/companies/short-of-funds-get-your-trade-financed-through-vayana-network-117073000522_1.html); [Inc42 financials](https://inc42.com/company/vayana-network/financials/)
- **OfBusiness / Oxyzo (the integrated "procurement plus finance" benchmark; note that Oxyzo is itself an NBFC lender, not a referrer):**
  - FY25: Oxyzo revenue ₹1,207 crore (₹903 crore in FY24), 95% of it interest income; PAT ₹339 crore
  - FY26: revenue ₹1,494 crore (+23%), PAT ₹375 crore, **AUM ₹11,822 crore (+28%)**
  - parent OfBusiness FY26 profit ₹724 crore
  - — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/exclusive-oxyzo-clocks-rs-330-cr-pat-on-rs-1207-cr-revenue-in-fy25-9315799); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/oxyzos-profit-jumps-17-to-inr-339-cr-in-fy25); [StartupFeed](https://startupfeed.in/?p=8122); [Inc42 – OfBusiness FY26](https://inc42.com/buzz/ipo-bound-ofbusiness-fy26-profit-jumps-21-yoy-to-₹724-cr/)

### Inferences
- **Illustrative referral economics** (assumptions, not quotes):
  - **Model A, share of discount income:** if XELOR earned 5–10% of the financier's discount income (1.3–2.5% of value), that is **about 7–25 bps of invoice value**.
  - **Model B, flat referral:** **10–25 bps of value financed**, in line with Vayana's 20–40 bps facilitation fee but lower, since XELOR does not run the platform.
  - **Model C, term or working-capital loan referral:** **1–2% of disbursal**, with fees paid by the lender, as with DSAs.
  - So **every ₹100 crore of invoices financed through XELOR referrals earns about ₹10–25 lakh** under Model B. A ₹1 crore working-capital loan referral earns about ₹1–2 lakh under Model C.
  - 18% GST applies to referral fees **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge; also stated in repo note 04]**.
- Under the multi-lender LSP rule, XELOR cannot steer MSMEs to the lender that pays it more. Fee levels should be uniform or disclosed, or ranking neutrality will be questioned.

## Q4. How much procurement spend flows through Indian MSME manufacturers?

### Takeaway
The ASI factory sector produced about **₹153 lakh crore of output in 2023-24, with GVA of ₹24.6 lakh crore**. That implies about **₹129 lakh crore of input purchases** (raw materials, components, fuel and services) across 2.60 lakh factories. MSMEs produce **35.4% of manufacturing output (2023-24)**. On that share, MSME manufacturers likely buy on the order of **₹40–45 lakh crore of inputs a year**. That is the theoretical B2B spend pool that a supplier network could see.

### Cited Findings
- **ASI 2023-24 (MoSPI, released August 2025):**
  - **2,60,061 factories**
  - **total output ₹1,53,27,166 crore** (reported as ₹1,53,27,16,609 lakh)
  - **GVA ₹24,58,336 crore** (+11.89%), from ₹21,97,056 crore in 2022-23
  - invested capital ₹68,01,329 crore
  - employment 1.95 crore (+5.92%)
  - output grew 5.80% and inputs 4.71%
  - basic metals, motor vehicles, chemicals, food products and pharma produced about 48% of output
  - — [TNPSC Thervupettagam (citing MoSPI)](https://www.tnpscthervupettagam.com/currentaffairs-detail/annual-survey-of-industries-2023–24/13); [The Week / PTI (27 Aug 2025)](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/08/27/dcm50-biz-survey-asi.html); [Vision IAS](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2025-08-28/economy/annual-survey-of-industries-asi-results-for-2023-24-released); [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/annual-survey-of-industries-2023-24/print_manually)

### Inferences
- **Total ASI inputs, 2023-24:** ₹153.27 − ₹24.58 = **≈ ₹128.7 lakh crore**. The input-to-output ratio is about **84%**.
- **Average per factory:** about ₹58.9 crore output and **≈ ₹49.5 crore inputs**. These averages are heavily skewed by large plants. A typical MSME factory buys far less. For a Peenya CNC shop, ₹1,200 crore / 3,000 units ≈ ₹40 lakh turnover each (2018), and at about 60–70% material and bought-out intensity **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge]** that is roughly ₹25–30 lakh of purchases a year.
- **MSME manufacturing procurement pool (rough):** 35.4% × ₹153 lakh crore ≈ ₹54 lakh crore of output. At an input ratio of about 80–84%, that is **≈ ₹43–46 lakh crore of input purchases**. Caveat: the 35.4% is a share of all manufacturing output, including unregistered units, while ₹153 lakh crore is ASI (registered factories) only. MSMEs may also be less input-intensive. A conservative range is **₹35–45 lakh crore a year**.
- **Serviceable slice for XELOR:** MSME-to-MSME and MSME-to-OEM purchases of engineering inputs (metals, castings, forgings, machined parts, job work) in target clusters. ASI basic metals, fabricated metal and machinery sub-industries could narrow this, but the split was not found (see Gaps).
- **Illustrative GMV template for the deck, labelled synthetic:**
  - assume 1,000 verified supplier factories, each selling ₹3 crore a year, with 30% of sales passing through XELOR-verified orders and deliveries
  - that gives **₹900 crore a year of GMV visibility**
  - if 20% of that GMV is invoiced to eligible buyers and discounted on TReDS, ₹180 crore is financed; at 15 bps that is **≈ ₹27 lakh referral revenue a year**
  - one ₹1 crore loan referral per 50 suppliers a year at 1.5% adds ≈ ₹30 lakh
  - **Every input here is an assumption, not data.**
  - The lesson: financing referrals become material only at a network scale of thousands of active suppliers, or with loan-referral products alongside TReDS.
